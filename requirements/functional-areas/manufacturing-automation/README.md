# Manufacturing Automation

## Scope

Manufacturing automation requirements govern digital planning, production
evidence, material and tooling control, estimating, learning, and safe
automation improvement.

These documents describe intended product or operating behavior. They do not
by themselves approve a business niche, machine purchase, facility, production
process, or stage of market entry.

## Requirement Groups

### Near-Term Learning Prototypes

- [CNC Manufacturing Automation Prototype](cnc-prototype.md)
- [Geometry-Driven Prismatic 3+2 CAM](geometry-driven-3plus2-cam.md)

These prototypes test planning and simulation concepts. Demonstration output
must not be represented as production-ready manufacturing instructions.

### Operating-System Foundations

- [Inventory, Material Movement, Tooling, and Equipment Lifecycle Management](inventory-tooling-and-equipment-lifecycle.md)
- [Historical-Part Analog Estimating](historical-part-analog-estimating.md)
- [Learning-Job Economics and Process Tuning](learning-job-economics-and-process-tuning.md)

These requirements describe reusable controls that become more valuable after
the business has selected a customer, initial offering, and operating stage.
They should be implemented incrementally rather than treated as prerequisites
for the first market test.

### Automation Investment

- [Bottleneck-Driven Automation Improvement](bottleneck-driven-automation-improvement.md)

Automation investment depends on measured production evidence, a stable
process, validated safety, and a demonstrated economic constraint. It should
not precede selection and validation of the first customer and capability.
