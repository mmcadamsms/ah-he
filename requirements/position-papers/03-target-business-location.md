# Position Paper 03 - Target Business Location

## Status

- **Status:** Planned
- **Sequence:** 3
- **Decision:** Which regions and sites best support the selected customer and
  operating model?

## Core Question

Where should the business operate after considering access to target customers,
outbound delivery, raw materials, outside processes, installed equipment,
buildings, workforce, utilities, taxes, regulation, insurance, resilience, and
expansion?

The answer must follow the selected business model and customer story. There is
no universal best state or city for every version of the business.

## Customer and Output Geography

Determine:

- which customers require local presence;
- which interactions require plant visits, local pickup, source inspection,
  installation, or rapid response;
- what can be sold nationally;
- how size, weight, value density, fragility, urgency, quantity, and returns
  affect outbound shipping;
- ground, parcel, LTL, courier, and dedicated-route reach;
- customer concentration and diversification; and
- whether proximity improves trust, qualification, and repeat business.

## Input and Capability Geography

Compare:

- raw-material production, service centers, distributors, stock, freight, and
  scrap markets;
- outside heat treatment, coatings, grinding, inspection, welding, and other
  specialist processes;
- new and used machine availability;
- riggers, cranes, heavy transport, OEM support, technicians, rebuilders, and
  spare parts;
- industrial suppliers and consumables;
- workforce and training;
- airports, highways, rail, ports, and parcel hubs; and
- resilience and alternate suppliers.

## Building and Operating Environment

Compare:

- production-ready industrial lease and purchase cost;
- available small-shop buildings;
- power, gas, water, sewer, waste, communications, and reliability;
- required building improvements;
- labor, benefits, workers' compensation, training, and turnover;
- property, inventory, machinery, sales, income, franchise, payroll, utility,
  and local taxes;
- manufacturing exemptions and incentives;
- zoning, permitting, inspection, environmental, fire, and process approvals;
- lawful operating discretion and regulatory predictability;
- insurance;
- neighbors and community compatibility;
- natural hazards and climate; and
- expansion or relocation.

## Decision Levels

Evaluate separately:

1. national region;
2. state;
3. metropolitan area or industrial corridor;
4. county and municipality;
5. industrial district or park; and
6. specific property.

Do not allow a favorable state ranking to conceal a poor municipality,
utility, building, route, or property.

## Scenario Method

For each business scenario:

- define customer and product mix;
- define equipment and facility;
- identify mandatory gates;
- calculate one-time, fixed, variable, step-fixed, and risk-contingent cost;
- model normal, downside, and growth cases;
- vary customer distance, material distance, labor, rent, energy, taxes,
  freight, and installed equipment cost;
- identify assumptions capable of reversing the ranking; and
- require actual quotes before a final recommendation.

## Required Position

The paper must produce:

- a location thesis;
- mandatory gates;
- regional shortlist;
- customer and supplier access maps;
- production-ready building-cost model;
- tax and regulatory matrix;
- variable-cost model;
- sensitivity and break-even analysis;
- risks and contrary evidence;
- property-level due-diligence plan; and
- recommendation for where detailed validation should begin.
