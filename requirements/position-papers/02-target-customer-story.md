# Position Paper 02 - Target Customer Story

## Status

- **Status:** Planned
- **Sequence:** 1
- **Decision:** Who is the first target customer, what problem is solved, and
  why should that customer trust and buy from the business?

## Core Question

What should the target customer story be for a direct-to-customer business that
uses software, AI, transparent workflows, engineering, and automated
manufacturing to turn customer intent into physical products?

In this context, direct-to-customer does not automatically mean consumer retail.
The target may be an inventor, product company, maintenance team, engineer,
small manufacturer, other job shop, research organization, or another buyer
that interacts directly with the business rather than through several layers
of brokers.

## Story Components

The paper must define:

- the first target customer segment;
- the triggering problem or job to be done;
- what the customer has at the beginning: idea, sketch, failed part, CAD,
  drawing, specification, or production package;
- the risk, delay, uncertainty, or frustration in the current alternative;
- the smallest valuable initial offering;
- the promise the business can make credibly;
- how requirements, assumptions, design, price, and manufacturing approval
  work;
- what the customer sees while work progresses;
- what evidence supports trust;
- what the customer receives;
- what happens when requirements change or production fails;
- what support, returns, repair, or repeat ordering looks like; and
- why the customer chooses this business rather than a local shop, online
  platform, engineer, contract manufacturer, or internal solution.

## Candidate Customer Stories

Evaluate separately:

1. **Idea to manufacturable design:** Help a customer translate a functional
   need into an approved design and manufacturing package.
2. **CAD to visible production:** Manufacture from complete drawings with fast
   quoting, transparent status, and reproducible production evidence.
3. **Urgent replacement:** Recreate or repair a difficult-to-source part where
   customer downtime is expensive.
4. **Hardware startup partner:** Support prototypes, revisions, bridge
   production, and eventual supplier transition.
5. **Overflow supplier:** Provide reliable capacity to established shops or
   manufacturers.
6. **Configured direct product:** Offer parameterized physical products whose
   design and production can be highly automated.
7. **Growth-capacity supplier:** Enter a growing industry where qualified
   supplier capacity is constrained and buyers need responsive new sources.

## Trust and Buying Friction

Determine:

- what proof a new business needs before the first order;
- how sample work, references, insurance, inspection, guarantees, deposits,
  and staged approvals reduce risk;
- which customers will accept a remote relationship;
- when proximity and site visits matter;
- who influences and approves the purchase;
- how long qualification takes;
- how much engineering and quoting can be provided before payment;
- what confidentiality and intellectual-property controls are expected;
- what status information is valuable rather than distracting;
- what failures would permanently damage trust; and
- how repeat work becomes easier and more profitable.

## Required Position

The paper must produce:

- one primary initial customer;
- one secondary customer;
- a concise customer narrative;
- a clear initial offer and exclusions;
- the customer journey from first contact through repeat order;
- evidence and approval required at each stage;
- acquisition-channel hypotheses;
- pricing and payment assumptions;
- trust-building tests;
- reasons the position could be wrong; and
- measurable market tests and stop conditions.
