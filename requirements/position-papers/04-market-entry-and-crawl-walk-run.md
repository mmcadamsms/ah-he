# Position Paper 04 - Market Entry and Crawl-Walk-Run

## Status

- **Status:** Planned
- **Sequence:** 3
- **Decision:** How should the business enter, validate, add physical
  capabilities, and scale automation without taking on excessive complexity?

## Core Question

How difficult is it to create a direct-to-customer physical-products business
that gains leverage from being unusually good at software, AI, data, digital
workflow, and automation?

The business aspires to become very good at "moving electrons" so it can
coordinate and increasingly automate "moving atoms." The paper must identify
where digital leverage genuinely reduces physical-business complexity and
where materials, machines, safety, quality, logistics, maintenance, people,
capital, and customer trust remain hard constraints.

## Complexity Assessment

Assess:

- target-customer acquisition and qualification;
- requirements discovery and engineering;
- quoting and pricing;
- CAD, CAM, simulation, and software;
- supplier and outside-process coordination;
- raw materials and inventory;
- production processes and capability limits;
- facilities and utilities;
- safety, human intervention, and automation zones;
- inspection, quality, certification, and traceability;
- packaging, shipping, returns, and service;
- hiring, training, and management;
- equipment maintenance and downtime;
- cybersecurity and customer intellectual property;
- taxes, regulation, insurance, and contracts;
- working capital and cash timing;
- automation reliability and exception recovery; and
- the organizational ability to operate several of these systems at once.

## Market-Entry Options

Compare:

1. software prototype and simulated customer journey;
2. design, engineering, quoting, and project coordination with all physical
   work outsourced;
3. direct customer relationship with qualified partner manufacturing;
4. a broker, marketplace, or managed-supplier model;
5. one narrow in-house physical capability plus outsourced complements;
6. acquisition or partnership with an existing shop;
7. overflow supplier to established manufacturers or job shops; and
8. immediate vertically integrated physical operation.

Evaluate customer value, learning, cash flow, reputation, control, liability,
capital, margin, supplier dependence, and speed for each.

## Proposed Crawl-Walk-Run Framework

### Crawl - Validate digitally and through partners

Potential characteristics:

- narrow customer and problem;
- structured intake and requirements;
- CAD/design and transparent workflow;
- quoting and project records;
- qualified outsourced production;
- manual review and high-touch customer support;
- very limited physical assets;
- measured estimate-versus-actual learning; and
- no claim that unproven automation is production ready.

Possible gate to advance:

- repeated paid customer demand;
- demonstrated willingness to buy;
- acceptable contribution after supplier and internal effort;
- repeatable requirements and approval process;
- reliable qualified partners;
- evidence that one internal capability improves margin, lead time, quality,
  learning, or control; and
- sufficient capital and qualified ownership of physical risk.

### Walk - Own one coherent physical capability

Potential characteristics:

- one carefully selected anchor process;
- complete minimum support ecosystem;
- defined materials and product envelope;
- qualified people and safety systems;
- inspection and traceability;
- inventory and maintenance controls;
- partner network for complementary processes;
- customer-visible production;
- measured utilization, labor, quality, and cost; and
- targeted automation of stable repetitive tasks.

Possible gate to advance:

- sustained demand and utilization;
- process capability and delivery reliability;
- positive normal contribution;
- manageable scrap, rework, downtime, and working capital;
- proven safety and maintenance;
- repeat business;
- evidence that the next capability removes a real constraint; and
- management capacity for added complexity.

### Run - Integrate and automate multiple capabilities

Potential characteristics:

- selected machining, fabrication, finishing, assembly, and inspection;
- facility-wide digital thread;
- accountable material and WIP movement;
- time-phased inventory and capacity planning;
- safe human/automation zones;
- unattended or reduced-attendance operation only where validated;
- historical-part intelligence;
- automation selected by bottleneck and risk-adjusted return;
- scalable customer channels; and
- increasing value and margin per employee.

Advancement must remain evidence based. "Run" is not defined by owning more
machines; it is defined by a coherent, reliable, safe, and economically useful
operating system.

## Stage-Gate Rules

Each stage must define:

- customer evidence;
- economic thresholds;
- cash and capital limits;
- capability and quality evidence;
- safety and regulatory readiness;
- staffing and management capacity;
- supplier resilience;
- software and data readiness;
- maximum acceptable downside;
- stop, pivot, outsource, or sell criteria; and
- facts that would invalidate advancement.

Do not advance solely because time passed, equipment became available, or the
next stage is more exciting.

## Required Position

The paper must:

- score the major complexity domains;
- identify the minimum credible market entry;
- recommend the crawl, walk, and run boundaries;
- define what remains manual at each stage;
- define what is outsourced at each stage;
- identify the first owned physical capability;
- establish measurable gates and stop conditions;
- identify capital and working-capital exposure;
- explain where software and AI create genuine leverage;
- explain where physical expertise remains indispensable;
- present major failure modes and mitigations; and
- recommend the next paid experiments.
