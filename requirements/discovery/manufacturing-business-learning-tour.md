# Manufacturing Business Learning Tour

## Status

- **Status:** Proposed first discovery phase
- **Captured:** 2026-10-17
- **Authority:** Learning method, not an approved business decision or research
  conclusion

## Purpose

Use the broad discovery-question corpus to build practical understanding of the
manufacturing business before narrowing too quickly to one process, customer,
location, or operating model.

The learning tour is a breadth-first pass. It should reveal connections,
surprises, contradictions, unknown vocabulary, hidden dependencies, and areas
where the current business thesis is weak. It does not require final answers to
all 1,175 questions.

## Tour Activities

Use a mix of:

- structured review of every discovery topic;
- interviews with buyers, shop owners, operators, engineers, quality
  professionals, suppliers, lenders, insurers, and advisers;
- visits to additive, machining, fabrication, finishing, inspection, material,
  logistics, and training operations where access is permitted;
- equipment, tooling, software, material, facility, utility, insurance, and
  outside-process quote collection;
- representative part and external-quote exercises;
- review of public market, workforce, regulatory, safety, quality, and
  financial evidence; and
- small experiments that expose real workflow, cost, lead-time, quality, or
  customer behavior.

## Capture Method

Record insights under the relevant canonical question IDs. A tour note should
identify:

- the observation or provisional answer;
- source and date;
- whether it is fact, interpretation, anecdote, assumption, or hypothesis;
- confidence and contrary evidence;
- implications for other questions;
- follow-up needed for a decision-quality answer; and
- whether the question should become `Exploring`, remain `Open`, be
  `Deferred`, or enter a focused question packet.

Do not place durable insight only in a tour summary. The canonical question
record remains the source of truth.

## Tour Outputs

The tour should produce:

1. a cross-domain insight and contradiction map;
2. an updated vocabulary and business-system model;
3. a list of assumptions strengthened, weakened, or invalidated;
4. candidate process-by-market wedges and obvious no-go areas;
5. evidence sources, experts, facilities, and datasets worth deeper study;
6. a prioritized set of question packets for focused answering;
7. questions already answered or converted by existing requirements; and
8. a short learning-tour findings summary that explains why the first focused
   packet was selected.

## Guardrails

- Breadth comes before premature optimization, but the tour must be time-boxed
  and evidence-producing.
- One interview, vendor claim, shop visit, or anecdote is not a general answer.
- Do not force every question to the same depth.
- Do not let the tour become a reason to postpone customer contact, quotes, or
  small experiments.
- Do not buy equipment, select a site, or make regulated-work claims merely
  because a topic became interesting.
- Position papers still require focused question packets with decision-quality
  answers after the tour.

## Exit Criteria

The first learning tour is complete enough to narrow when:

- every major discovery domain has received an orientation pass;
- the most important cross-domain dependencies and contradictions are visible;
- major terminology and evidence-source gaps are identified;
- several plausible and implausible business paths can be explained;
- the first focused question packet can be selected for explicit reasons; and
- unresolved uncertainty is recorded rather than hidden.
