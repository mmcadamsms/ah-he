# Business and Commercial Strategy Questions

## Purpose

Questions about the business model, initial offering, commercial approvals, pricing, scheduling, and how prototypes become learning.

These are discovery questions, not approved requirements. Question IDs are permanent and canonical in this file.

## Business Purpose and Initial Market

### BIZ-001 — What precise customer problem are we solving first?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Start by solving a narrow problem: local engineers,
maintenance teams, and small manufacturers need non-safety-critical metal
parts in low quantities with faster requirements clarification and more
reliable delivery than their normal ad-hoc sourcing process. This is a testable
entry hypothesis, not yet a demonstrated market position.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. It requires a specific problem,
  target customer, competitive advantage, and sales process; it does not show
  which problem this business can win.
- [Protolabs CNC machining](https://www.protolabs.com/services/cnc-machining/) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial offering. A scaled supplier markets
  automated/semi-automated high-mix, low-quantity machining with DFM feedback;
  supplier marketing does not establish local unmet demand or profitability.

**Conflicting evidence:** Digital suppliers already offer rapid low-volume
parts nationally, so speed alone may not be differentiated; the stronger wedge
may be requirements help, local recovery, or unusual mixed-process work.

**Confidence:** Medium — the problem is commercially plausible but unvalidated
with this business's customers.

**Business-specific validation:** Interview 15-20 target buyers, collect their
last three delayed or no-bid jobs, and obtain at least five paid trial orders.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The conclusion
is directionally right, but the support is mostly SBA/NIST framing plus vendor
examples rather than observed local order data. No strong contrary public
evidence changed the answer, so I kept it and treated paid local jobs as the
deciding test.

### BIZ-005 — What should the business explicitly refuse to design or manufacture?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Refuse illegal or infringing work; weapons and controlled
technical data without a compliance program; safety-critical or regulated
parts outside documented qualifications; unclear ownership or requirements;
and any geometry, material, tolerance, inspection, or delivery promise the
business and its approved partners cannot verify.

**Evidence:**
- [SendCutSend terms](https://store.sendcutsend.com/pages/terms-of-service) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial terms. They require lawful,
  authorized files, reserve refusal rights, and disclaim fitness for purpose;
  one supplier's terms are not a complete risk policy.
- [FDA device-regulation overview](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/device-advice-comprehensive-regulatory-assistance/overview-device-regulation) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. It documents registration,
  premarket, quality-system, inspection, labeling, and reporting obligations
  for device manufacturers; applicability depends on the exact role and part.

**Conflicting evidence:** Some suppliers safely make components for regulated
customers under customer-controlled designs, so a blanket industry ban may be
unnecessarily broad; the refusal rule should turn on role, qualification,
traceability, and contract allocation.

**Confidence:** High on the refusal principles; low on final category boundaries.

**Business-specific validation:** Have manufacturing counsel, an insurer, and a
qualified quality professional approve a prohibited/restricted-work matrix and
escalation process.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The
regulatory/export/responsibility sources support a conservative scope
boundary, and the contrary case is always part-, role-, and contract-specific.
I kept the answer unchanged.

### BIZ-008 — What evidence would demonstrate product-market fit?

**State:** Exploring

multiple unrelated customers should reorder without founder subsidies while
jobs meet target contribution, acceptance, and on-time delivery. A practical
gate is a sustained cohort with repeat orders, referrals, falling quote effort,
and no single-customer dependence.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. It ties customer segments,
  channels, revenue streams, costs, and projections together; it gives no
  universal product-market-fit threshold.
- [NIST Baldrige improvement tools](https://www.nist.gov/baldrige/self-assessing/improvement-tools) —
  **Evidence class:** consensus-based official framework. It emphasizes
  measured organizational results and information gaps; it is not a startup
  validation study.

**Conflicting evidence:** A few high-value customers may support a viable job
shop without broad repeat cohorts, but that model increases concentration and
relationship risk and should not be confused with scalable market pull.

**Confidence:** Medium — the evidence types are strong, while numeric gates are
business-specific.

**Business-specific validation:** Predefine a 6-12 month gate using paid
customers, reorder rate, contribution after owner labor, on-time delivery,
acceptance/rework, referrals, concentration, and cash conversion.

**Confidence:** Medium — principles are well established; entity and control

### BIZ-013 — What does an automation-first operating philosophy require in practice?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** It requires standardized work, structured data,
instrumented actuals, stable interfaces, exception ownership, and manual
fallbacks before automation. Automate frequent, bounded, reversible work in
increments; do not automate ambiguity or hide a broken process behind AI.

**Evidence:**
- [Census working paper on business AI use](https://www.census.gov/library/working-papers/2024/adrm/CES-WP-24-16.html) —
  **Evidence class:** empirical official survey analysis; **AI maturity:
  early adoption.** AI adopters frequently reported staff training, new
  workflows, and cloud/storage purchases; association with performance is not
  proof that AI caused improvement.
- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) —
  **Evidence class:** consensus-based official framework; **AI maturity:
  governance.** It provides voluntary risk-management practices rather than
  measured manufacturing ROI.
- [NIST gauge performance guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section4/mpc4.htm) —
  **Evidence class:** official technical guidance. Reliable automation depends
  on measurement repeatability, reproducibility, stability, and bias control;
  it addresses metrology rather than enterprise workflow.

**Conflicting evidence:** Full automation can produce large gains in stable,
high-volume environments, but this business begins in high-mix, low-volume
work where exceptions and data scarcity weaken that case.

**Confidence:** High on prerequisites; medium on the optimal automation sequence.

**Business-specific validation:** Baseline touch time, error, queue, and
override rates for each workflow; automate only where a bounded trial improves
total cost or service without increasing uncontrolled risk.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. NIST/Census

### BIZ-017 — What capability-acquisition sequence allows the business to reach break-even or positive cash flow with acceptable risk?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Acquire commitments before fixed assets: first sell paid
engineering and partner-produced work; then own requirements, estimating,
supplier control, and metrology; next add the repeatedly outsourced bottleneck
only after utilization and cash gates are met; add adjacent processes and
automation from measured constraints rather than vision order.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. It requires funding needs,
  capital expenditures, monthly/quarterly projections, partners, and cost
  structure to align; it does not prescribe manufacturing sequencing.
- [Census Quarterly Survey of Plant Capacity Utilization](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/qpc.html) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official data. It establishes utilization as a
  measured manufacturing variable; national/sector data cannot set a machine
  purchase gate for one shop.
- [Federal Reserve capacity utilization](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g17/current/default.htm) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official data. Utilization changes materially
  over cycles, warning against straight-line demand assumptions.

**Conflicting evidence:** Buying equipment early can build competence and
capture margin, but it also creates debt, depreciation, maintenance, labor,
and demand risk before a repeatable order base exists.

**Confidence:** Medium — sequence logic is strong; break-even timing needs real quotes.

**Business-specific validation:** For each capability, compare partner actuals
