## Used Machinery and Capital Equipment

### SUP-009 — Where can the business find a good market for second-hand machinery?

**State:** Open

**Original wording:** "where do we find a good market for second-hand machinery"

The eventual answer should compare:

- reputable used-machinery dealers;
- OEM-certified used-equipment programs;
- machinery brokers;
- industrial auctions;
- plant closures and liquidations;
- direct sales from operating manufacturers;
- bank, lender, and leasing-company repossessions;
- trade-ins;
- regional machine-tool distributors;
- government and educational surplus;
- online marketplaces; and
- private industry networks.

The analysis should identify geographic regions with strong inventories of CNC
machine tools, fabrication equipment, inspection equipment, material handling,
and support equipment relevant to the phased shop plan.

### SUP-010 — Which types of equipment are reasonable to buy used?

**State:** Open

Evaluate separately:

- three-axis and five-axis CNC mills;
- CNC lathes and mill-turn machines;
- laser cutters;
- tube lasers and saws;
- press brakes;
- welding equipment;
- forklifts;
- cranes and hoists;
- compressors;
- metrology equipment;
- tooling and workholding;
- packaging equipment; and
- material-storage systems.

Some equipment may retain value and remain serviceable for decades, while
other equipment may carry unacceptable risk because of obsolete controls,
unsupported software, unavailable replacement parts, hidden damage, accuracy
loss, or expensive installation.

### SUP-011 — What inspection is required before purchasing used machinery?

**State:** Open

Potential checks include:

- serial number, year, ownership, liens, and service history;
- machine hours, spindle hours, cutting hours, and alarm history;
- crash and repair history;
- control, firmware, options, licenses, and parameter backups;
- spindle condition, runout, vibration, and warmup behavior;
- backlash, positioning, repeatability, geometry, and volumetric accuracy;
- ball screws, guideways, bearings, rotary axes, brakes, and encoders;
- tool changer, probing, coolant, chip, hydraulic, pneumatic, and lubrication
  systems;
- electrical cabinet, transformer, voltage, and phase;
- safety interlocks and guarding;
- corrosion, contamination, leaks, and environmental exposure;
- demonstration under power and representative cutting;
- independent technician or metrology inspection; and
- availability of manuals, maintenance records, keys, passwords, fixtures,
  accessories, and original documentation.

### SUP-012 — What is the complete installed cost of a used machine?

**State:** Open

Use a comparison such as:

```text
purchase price
+ buyer premium
+ taxes and fees
+ inspection and travel
+ decommissioning
+ cleaning and preparation
+ rigging and loading
+ freight and insurance
+ unloading and placement
+ foundation and facility changes
+ electrical, air, exhaust, gas, coolant, and networking
+ control or software updates
+ repairs and replacement parts
+ calibration and acceptance testing
+ initial tooling, holders, probes, and workholding
+ training
+ expected early downtime and contingency
- included accessories and recoverable resale value
= total installed and production-ready cost
```

### SUP-013 — How should auction purchases differ from dealer purchases?

**State:** Open

Compare inspection access, ability to power the machine, warranties, buyer
premiums, payment deadlines, removal windows, approved riggers, missing
components, title and liens, shipping responsibility, recourse, and the risk
that the machine cannot be tested before purchase.

### SUP-015 — When is a used machine better than a new, leased, or outsourced capability?

**State:** Open

Compare:

- total installed cost;
- financing and cash timing;
- depreciation and tax treatment;
- lead time;
- warranty;
- expected reliability;
- control familiarity;
- accuracy and process capability;
- automation readiness;
- energy use;
- parts and service;
- residual value;
- utilization ramp;
- customer demand uncertainty; and
- the option to outsource until demand is proven.

### SUP-016 — What acceptance test must a used machine pass?

**State:** Open

Define measurable criteria before final acceptance or production use, including
geometry, positioning, repeatability, spindle performance, rotary accuracy,
tool change, probing, safety, representative test cuts, surface finish,
dimensional capability, and documentation of known limitations.

### SUP-017 — How should used-equipment opportunities be tracked?

**State:** Open

Record:

- seller and source type;
- equipment identity and serial number;
- location;
- asking and expected total installed cost;
- configuration and options;
- inspection status;
- serviceability;
- known defects;
- removal deadline;
- comparable sales;
- target capability and business phase;
- estimated utilization and ROI; and
- reason purchased, rejected, or deferred.
