# Financial Model and Capital Readiness Questions

## Purpose

Questions needed to determine whether the proposed business can survive,
finance its learning, and earn an acceptable return. This topic complements
tax research by focusing on integrated cash, capital, unit economics, and
downside decisions.

These are discovery questions, not approved requirements. Question IDs are
permanent and canonical in this file.

## Integrated Financial Model

### FNC-001 — What integrated income-statement, balance-sheet, and cash-flow model is required for each crawl, walk, and run scenario?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use one driver-based model with linked income
statement, balance sheet, cash flow, debt schedule, fixed-asset schedule, and
working-capital roll-forward. Crawl, walk, and run should change documented
drivers—not use disconnected spreadsheets. As a prudent capital-readiness
baseline, model at least 24 months monthly and years 3-5 annually, with base,
downside, delayed-ramp, and failure cases, but treat the exact horizon and
reporting format as lender- and stage-specific rather than universal.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/#business-plan) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. SBA says lender-facing projections should include forecast income statements, balance sheets, cash-flow statements, and capital-expenditure budgets, with monthly or quarterly detail in year one.
- [SBA 7(a) Working Capital Pilot](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/#wcp-program) — **Evidence class:** observed financing practice. Participating borrowers must produce timely financial statements plus receivable, payable, and inventory reports, supporting an integrated rather than profit-only model.

**Conflicting evidence:** Lean one-page plans are useful for early exploration,
but SBA distinguishes them from the detailed projections lenders commonly
request; they are not an adequate capital-readiness model.

**Confidence:** High on integrated model structure; medium on the exact horizon;
low on the business-specific drivers.

**Business-specific validation:** Reconcile the model to quotes, payroll,
supplier terms, tax assumptions, and bank-format statements with a
manufacturing CPA and prospective lender.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. SBA clearly
supports integrated multi-statement projections and detailed first-year monthly
or quarterly forecasting, but it does not itself require a 24-month monthly
horizon. Kept the integrated-model conclusion, qualified the horizon as a
prudent baseline rather than a universal rule, and adjusted confidence.

### FNC-002 — What startup uses of cash and sources of funds are required before the first order, first owned capability, and first expansion?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Build a dated sources-and-uses schedule for three gates:
pre-revenue setup, first owned process, and expansion. Uses must include
deposits, professional fees, permits, insurance, software, tooling, material,
freight/rigging/installation, commissioning, payroll, tax, working capital,
contingency, and debt fees; sources must state availability date, restrictions,
repayment, owner guarantee, and whether they fund fixed assets or operations.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA 7(a) loans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/) — **Evidence class:** primary financing-program evidence. 7(a) may fund working capital, machinery, fixtures, supplies, real estate, and ownership changes, but lender approval and terms are transaction-specific.
- [SBA 504 loans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/504-loans/) — **Evidence class:** primary financing-program evidence. 504 supports qualifying real estate and long-life machinery but expressly does not fund working capital or inventory.
- [SBA microloans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/microloans/) — **Evidence class:** primary financing-program evidence. Microloans can fund working capital, inventory, supplies, machinery, and equipment, but not real estate or existing debt.

**Conflicting evidence:** A source suitable for a machine may be unavailable for
startup losses or inventory; program labels therefore cannot replace a
line-by-line eligibility and timing check.

**Confidence:** High on the schedule required; medium on source availability.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain written lender/equity term sheets and
vendor quotes, then prove every use has a committed source plus contingency.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The SBA program
pages support matching uses of funds to eligible financing sources and checking
restrictions source by source, but they do not show that every startup will
qualify or get funds on time. No substantive answer change was needed.

### FNC-003 — What fixed, variable, step-fixed, pass-through, maintenance, replacement-capital, and owner-labor costs belong in each scenario?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Public finance-program sources do not prescribe one
universal manufacturing cost taxonomy, but the model should classify material
costs by behavior and cash timing, not just by accounting account. Explicitly
test owner-labor replacement cost, unabsorbed factory overhead, maintenance and
consumables, scrap/rework, outside processing, freight, merchant/financing
fees, insurance, tax/compliance, software, selling effort, and expected
replacement capital where they are economically material; separate customer
pass-through amounts from margin.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/#business-plan) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. SBA requires detailed uses, operating assumptions, projections, and capital budgets when seeking financing.
- [Iowa State Extension: Managerial Costs](https://www.extension.iastate.edu/agdm/wholefarm/html/c5-209.html) — **Evidence class:** practitioner and university-extension evidence. The guide distinguishes fixed, variable, semi-variable, and step-variable costs for decision-making, supporting behavior-based classification rather than relying only on financial-statement categories.

**Conflicting evidence:** Tax-expense classifications and financial-accounting
classifications may differ from decision-useful cost behavior, and public
sources do not reveal the correct overhead or reserve amounts for this specific
shop. Both views are needed and must be reconciled with actual operating data.

**Confidence:** Medium on the classification approach; low on actual overhead
allocation and reserve amounts.

**Business-specific validation:** Time actual jobs, map invoices and payroll to
drivers, and have a manufacturing CPA or controller review overhead,
owner-labor replacement, and replacement-capital treatment.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Disagree. The earlier taxonomy was
directionally sensible, but the cited SBA and Census sources did not directly
support many of the specific cost buckets or the owner-labor/replacement-capital
treatment. I reframed the answer as a decision-useful modeling approach,
replaced the weaker Census citation with a cost-classification source, and
lowered confidence.

### FNC-004 — What unit economics and break-even utilization apply by service, process, customer type, and order profile?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Start with contribution per constrained hour and per
order, then calculate cash break-even from fixed costs and contribution. In a
capacity-constrained shop, comparing contribution per bottleneck hour is
usually more decision-useful than relying on average gross margin or quoted
machine rates alone. Model engineering, setup, run, inspection, outside
processing, material, scrap, payment cost, and collections separately by order
profile, and report both scheduled and sellable utilization because setup,
repair, training, and downtime consume capacity.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/#business-plan) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. SBA requires projections to connect the product/service model, sales plan, funding need, and capital budget.
- [SBA break-even point guidance](https://legacy.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/calculate-your-startup-costs/break-even-point) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. SBA defines break-even as the point where total cost equals total revenue and treats it as an important part of a business plan.
- [SBA 7(a) Working Capital Pilot](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/#wcp-program) — **Evidence class:** observed financing practice. The program is designed for contract and project financing using current financial statements, receivable/payable aging, and inventory reporting.

**Conflicting evidence:** Quoted machine-hour rates or average gross margins are
useful screening tools, but realized contribution depends on mix, setup,
quality, downtime, and collections; neither rate nor utilization alone proves
break-even.

**Confidence:** Medium on method; low until actual routing, yield, and
collection data exist.

**Business-specific validation:** Compare estimates with clocked labor/machine
time, scrap, invoice realization, and cash collection for the first 20-30 jobs.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The
contribution-margin and break-even logic is sound, but the original source set
only weakly supported the specific emphasis on constrained-hour economics. I
added a direct SBA break-even source, tightened the language from rule to
method, and kept low confidence until job-level data exists.

### FNC-005 — How do deposits, receivables, inventory, WIP, supplier terms, scrap, rework, and outside processing determine the cash-conversion cycle and peak working-capital need?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use a weekly cash waterfall from quote through cleared
customer payment. Peak working capital is the maximum cumulative cash deficit
after deposits and supplier credit but before collection, including tax,
payroll, scrap/rework, WIP, outside-process queues, and late-payment scenarios.
The standard receivable-days plus inventory-days minus payable-days metric is a
diagnostic, not a substitute for order-level timing.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA 7(a) Working Capital Pilot](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/#wcp-program) — **Evidence class:** observed financing practice. SBA requires receivable, payable, and inventory reporting and permits monitored working-capital lines for contract/project needs.
- [Census Manufacturing and Trade Inventories and Sales](https://www.census.gov/mtis/index.html) — **Evidence class:** official empirical data. Monthly sales and inventory measures show that inventory can move independently of shipments and must be modeled as cash tied up.
- [Federal Reserve 2024 Report on Employer Firms](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2024/2024-report-on-employer-firms) — **Evidence class:** empirical survey. Small firms reported persistent difficulty paying operating expenses and the report includes measured business-payment conditions.

**Conflicting evidence:** Industry-average cash-cycle ratios can help a
reasonableness check but conceal deposits, long-lead material, batch WIP, and
customer-specific lateness.

**Confidence:** High on the calculation logic; low on peak amount before real
terms exist, and the survey evidence is not manufacturing-specific.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain supplier/customer terms and run an
order-level simulation using actual lead times, dispute rates, scrap, and days
to cleared funds.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The sources
support explicit receivable, payable, inventory, and liquidity tracking, but
the Federal Reserve survey is broad employer-firm evidence rather than direct
proof for a manufacturing startup. I kept the weekly cash-waterfall conclusion
and clarified the generalization limit in confidence.

## Capital and Downside

### FNC-006 — How much cash runway and contingency reserve are required under base, downside, delayed-ramp, and equipment-failure cases?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** There is no universal number. Hold enough unrestricted
cash and committed undrawn liquidity to survive the worst credible modeled
trough plus a separately priced equipment/business-interruption event; set a
board/owner minimum-liquidity floor and action triggers before that floor is
reached. Do not count unapproved grants, unused credit that can be cancelled,
or customer receivables at face value.

**Evidence:**
- [Federal Reserve 2025 Report on Employer Firms](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2025/2025-report-on-employer-firms) — **Evidence class:** empirical survey. The report found weakening revenue performance and continuing financing constraints among employer firms, supporting explicit downside liquidity planning.
- [FDIC Commercial and Industrial Lending](https://www.fdic.gov/risk-management-manual-examination-policies/commercial-and-industrial-lending.pdf) — **Evidence class:** bank supervisory practice. FDIC guidance centers repayment analysis on business cash flow, borrower condition, collateral, and controls rather than optimistic asset values alone.

**Conflicting evidence:** Rules of thumb such as “three to six months” ignore
machine lead time, concentration, debt service, and ramp risk; scenario loss and
recovery time are more credible.

**Confidence:** High that scenario-based liquidity planning is required; low on
amount, because the survey evidence is broad and not startup-manufacturing-
specific.

**Business-specific validation:** Price downtime, expedited outsourcing, lost
margin, deductibles, repair lead time, debt service, and owner minimum draw;
review with insurer, lender, and CPA.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The answer is
right to reject generic runway rules and require downside scenarios, but the
Federal Reserve evidence is broad small-employer survey data, not a
manufacturing-startup standard. I kept the conclusion and tightened the
confidence statement.

### FNC-007 — Which equity, owner capital, bank debt, equipment finance, lease, seller finance, grants, or partner structures fit each stage?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Match duration and risk: owner/equity capital for
uncertain learning and losses; revolving working-capital debt for collectible
receivables and inventory; equipment term debt or leases for productive assets;
504 financing for eligible long-life fixed assets; and grants only after written
award. Seller or partner finance can bridge gaps but requires legal diligence,
subordination, control, and default terms.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA 7(a) loans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/) — **Evidence class:** primary financing-program evidence. 7(a) covers varied working-capital, equipment, real-estate, refinancing, and ownership uses.
- [SBA 504 loans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/504-loans/) — **Evidence class:** primary financing-program evidence. 504 is long-term fixed-asset financing and excludes working capital and inventory.
- [SBA microloans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/microloans/) — **Evidence class:** primary financing-program evidence. Intermediaries set credit terms for smaller startup/expansion needs.

**Conflicting evidence:** Debt avoids dilution but adds fixed claims and often
guarantees; equity absorbs volatility but costs ownership/control. Stage and
cash predictability, not nominal rate alone, determine fit.

**Confidence:** High on matching principle; medium on accessible structures.

**Business-specific validation:** Solicit comparable written proposals showing
all fees, guarantees, collateral, covenants, dilution, tax effects, and exit
rights; obtain CPA and attorney review.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The SBA program
pages support matching fixed-asset uses to 504 and broader working-capital or
equipment uses to 7(a) and microloans, but they do not establish access for
this borrower. No material answer change was needed.

### FNC-008 — What debt-service coverage, collateral, covenant, guarantee, and liquidity constraints would each financing option impose?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Constraints are lender- and transaction-specific and must
be copied into the model exactly. Model required debt-service coverage,
borrowing-base haircuts, lien priority, reporting frequency, minimum liquidity,
capital-spending/distribution limits, insurance, change-of-control terms,
personal guarantees, and cure/default remedies under both base and downside
cash flow.

**Evidence:**
- [FDIC Commercial and Industrial Lending](https://www.fdic.gov/risk-management-manual-examination-policies/commercial-and-industrial-lending.pdf) — **Evidence class:** bank supervisory practice. FDIC examination guidance addresses cash-flow repayment, collateral, guarantors, covenants, monitoring, and problem-credit controls.
- [SBA 7(a) loans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/7a-loans/) — **Evidence class:** primary financing-program evidence. SBA states that repayment is generally from business cash flow and that application documents and terms vary by lender and circumstances.

**Conflicting evidence:** Program maximums and public rate caps do not reveal a
specific lender's advance rates, covenants, collateral, or guarantee package.

**Confidence:** High that these constraints are material; low until term sheets.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain draft commitment and loan documents,
then have counsel and CPA build a covenant calendar and downside cure plan.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. FDIC
supervisory guidance and SBA program guidance support the listed categories of
constraints, but actual debt-service coverage, advance rates, covenants, and
guarantees remain lender- and transaction-specific. No material answer change
was needed.

### FNC-009 — What financial gates must be met before hiring, leasing space, buying equipment, internalizing an outside process, or adding automation?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Require a written gate showing demonstrated demand,
incremental contribution after all cash costs, peak working capital, downside
liquidity above the minimum floor, financing availability, qualified labor,
capacity/yield evidence, and a reversible fallback. Buy or internalize only
when risk-adjusted total cost and service performance beat outsourcing at
credible volume and the financing package still fits minimum-liquidity
requirements—not merely when the payment fits.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/#business-plan) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. SBA links funding requests to detailed uses, projections, capital budgets, and future repayment plans.
- [SBA 504 loans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/504-loans/) — **Evidence class:** observed financing practice. Fixed-asset financing requires a feasible business plan, qualified management, and ability to repay.

**Conflicting evidence:** Lowest unit cost at forecast volume may conflict with
cash preservation and flexibility; a lease may lower upfront cash yet increase
total fixed obligations.

**Confidence:** Medium on gate dimensions; low-to-medium on threshold values.

**Business-specific validation:** Require signed demand or measured utilization,
vendor quotes, pilot yield, staffing plan, and lender/insurer approval before
each irreversible commitment.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. SBA evidence
supports feasible planning, qualified management, and repayment capacity for
fixed-asset financing, but it does not prescribe the full internal gate
checklist. I kept the gate logic as prudent management discipline and tightened
the final sentence.

### FNC-010 — What stop, pause, sell, outsource, or wind-down thresholds protect the owner and business when demand, margin, cash, or execution differs from plan?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Precommit monthly triggers for minimum unrestricted cash,
maximum owner exposure, overdue receivables, covenant headroom, gross
contribution, backlog quality, schedule/yield, concentration, and months to
break-even. Each trigger needs an owner, cure period, and graduated action:
freeze commitments, require deposits, outsource, sell idle assets, seek capital,
or execute an orderly wind-down before payroll, tax, customer-property, or
secured-creditor obligations cannot be met.

**Evidence:**
- [FDIC Commercial and Industrial Lending](https://www.fdic.gov/risk-management-manual-examination-policies/commercial-and-industrial-lending.pdf) — **Evidence class:** bank supervisory practice. FDIC guidance emphasizes continuing credit monitoring, covenant compliance, repayment capacity, and timely response to deterioration.
- [Federal Reserve 2025 Report on Employer Firms](https://www.fedsmallbusiness.org/reports/survey/2025/2025-report-on-employer-firms) — **Evidence class:** empirical survey. Revenue declines and incomplete financing approvals remain real small-firm risks, arguing against assuming rescue capital will arrive.
- [U.S. Courts Bankruptcy Basics](https://www.uscourts.gov/court-programs/bankruptcy/bankruptcy-basics) — **Evidence class:** primary legal guidance. Federal courts caution that insolvency options are legal/financial processes requiring competent professional advice.

**Conflicting evidence:** Continuing may preserve option value during a temporary
shock, while delay can destroy creditor and owner value; predetermined triggers
should permit documented exceptions, not automatic denial of new evidence.

**Confidence:** High that triggers are needed; medium on exact levels.

**Business-specific validation:** Set thresholds with the owner, lender, CPA,
insolvency counsel, and insurer; test them in a tabletop wind-down and update
quarterly.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The FDIC and
bankruptcy sources support monitoring, covenant awareness, and early action
before distress worsens, while the Federal Reserve survey supports not assuming
easy rescue financing. I kept the trigger-based answer and made no material
changes beyond independent confirmation of the generalization limits.
