# Workforce Cost and Labor Market Questions

## Purpose

This topic tracks where manufacturing and machining labor is available and
what it costs an employer to recruit, employ, schedule, retain, train, and
protect that workforce.

The analysis must compare total employer labor cost and productive capacity,
not just advertised hourly wages.

**Research date and data vintages:** Sources were checked October 17, 2026.
Current public-data vintages used in this pass are OEWS May 2025; QCEW first
quarter 2026; ECEC June 2026; National Compensation Survey benefits March 2026;
BEA regional prices and ACS commuting 2024; DOL childcare prices 2008-2022;
BLS injury/illness data 2024; BLS projections 2024-2034; BLS union data 2025;
IPEDS completions 2024-25; Registered Apprenticeship fiscal 2025; and BLS
productivity second quarter 2026. JOLTS, LAUS, and LEHD/QWI/J2J/PSEO/VEO are
live series with different geographic lags; record the displayed month/quarter
and file vintage when extracting a candidate-market comparison.

## Question Supplied in Conversation

### LAB-001 — Which states and localities impose higher employee-labor costs for machining and manufacturing?

**Original wording:** "which states and localities require higher costs for employee labor in the maufacturing / machining type of areas"

The eventual answer should identify where and why total labor cost differs for
relevant occupations, including:

- CNC machinists;
- CNC setup technicians;
- CNC programmers;
- machine operators;
- tool and die makers;
- manufacturing and mechanical engineers;
- CAD designers and drafters;
- welders and fabricators;
- quality inspectors and metrology technicians;
- maintenance and industrial technicians;
- production supervisors;
- material handlers;
- assemblers; and
- safety, purchasing, scheduling, and customer-support roles.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** There is no defensible single high-cost-state ranking for
this occupation mix. California, Hawaii, New Jersey, New York, Washington, and
high-cost metros often combine above-national wages or living costs with more
state/local mandates, but the ordering changes by occupation, metro, shift,
claims history, and employer size. Compare metropolitan wage percentiles and
then add each site's statutory and quoted employer costs.

**Evidence:**
- [BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics by area](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oessrcma.htm) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Annual OEWS estimates support occupation-by-metro wage comparisons, but omit
  bonuses, benefits, and many statutory costs.
- [BEA regional price parities](https://www.bea.gov/data/prices-inflation/regional-price-parities-state-and-metro-area) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  The 2024 release shows large state and metro price-level differences,
  especially housing; price levels are not employer wage requirements.
- [DOL comparison of state unemployment-insurance laws](https://oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/statelaws.asp) — **Evidence class:** official law summary.
  Taxable wage bases and rate structures vary by state and experience.
- [Oregon workers' compensation premium ranking by state](https://www.oregon.gov/dcbs/reports/cost/Pages/oregon-vs-nation.aspx) — **Evidence class:** official comparative study.
  Its standardized-classification index permits broad comparison, not a quote
  for this shop.

**Conflicting evidence:** Low-wage states can have scarce-skill premiums,
higher turnover, or higher workers' compensation for a particular class;
high-wage clusters can deliver more skill and output. State averages therefore
conflict with some occupation- and employer-specific results.

**Confidence:** Medium. The cost components are established, but candidate
sites, staffing mix, and insurance classifications are not.

**Business-specific validation:** For each candidate county, price the exact
SOC/job levels, shifts, benefits, UI account, workers' compensation class and
experience modifier, leave programs, and local taxes with payroll, a licensed
broker, and qualified employment/tax counsel.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. It is correct to avoid a faux state ranking and to analyze occupation/metro wages plus statutory and quoted employer costs. State averages and anecdotal 'low-cost state' claims are too coarse to prove actual employer cost.

## Wage and Compensation Questions

### LAB-002 — What are prevailing wages for each relevant occupation?

Compare state and metropolitan-area wage distributions rather than only
averages. Include entry, median, experienced, and highly specialized workers,
plus shift differentials, overtime, bonuses, and incentive pay.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use the latest OEWS 10th, 25th, median, 75th, and 90th
percentiles for each occupation and metro as the public baseline; do not label
those percentiles automatically as entry or experienced pay. Add current local
offers and payroll data for job level, shift differential, overtime, bonus,
certification, and regulated-industry experience because OEWS does not report
those components separately.

**Evidence:**
- [BLS OEWS data by area](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oessrcma.htm) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Provides consistent occupation and percentile estimates for states and
  metropolitan/nonmetropolitan areas.
- [BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Separates wages from broad benefit categories by industry and worker group,
  but is not occupation-by-metro data.
- [BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages](https://www.bls.gov/cew/) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  County and industry employment/pay data help check whether an OEWS estimate
  sits in a growing or thin local market.

**Conflicting evidence:** Advertised ranges and salary websites may be newer
than annual OEWS, but can mix job levels, exclude filled jobs, or reflect
aspirational postings. OEWS is more consistent; live offers are more timely.

**Confidence:** High on the method; low on actual prevailing rates until roles
and candidate metros are fixed.

**Business-specific validation:** Match each job description to an SOC and
internal level, obtain at least three local recruiter/employer benchmarks, and
record accepted offers, shift premiums, bonuses, and overtime for 60-90 days.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. OEWS percentiles are the right public baseline, but lagged data and broad SOC buckets can hide specialty, shift, and regulated-industry premiums. Live offers and accepted-pay data must update the model.

### LAB-003 — How much wage premium is required for scarce manufacturing skills?

Investigate five-axis machining, complex setup, CAM programming, inspection,
toolmaking, automation, robotics, maintenance, welding certifications, and
experience with regulated industries.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** No public national dataset reliably prices each listed
skill. The required premium is the smallest role- and market-specific increase
that produces qualified acceptances and retention, measured against the same
base occupation and shift. Five-axis setup/programming, advanced inspection,
controls/robotics, difficult maintenance, current welding credentials, and
regulated-industry documentation should be priced separately rather than
collapsed into a generic “skilled labor” premium.

**Evidence:**
- [BLS OEWS occupation profiles](https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes_stru.htm) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Wage distributions provide a base occupation comparison but do not identify
  machine axis count, software, certification, or industry-specific skill.
- [O*NET OnLine](https://www.onetonline.org/) — **Evidence class:** official occupational information.
  Detailed tasks, technologies, skills, and work activities support comparable
  job definitions; O*NET does not publish a market premium for each skill.
- [BLS Employment Projections](https://www.bls.gov/emp/) — **Evidence class:** official forecast.
  Occupation openings help frame replacement demand, but national projections
  do not establish a local recruiting premium.

**Conflicting evidence:** Job postings often show premiums, but title inflation,
bundled duties, and unobserved bonuses make them noncomparable; broad wage
percentiles may understate a genuinely scarce niche or overstate it by mixing
seniority and supervision.

**Confidence:** Medium on which capabilities can be scarce; low on the premium
without local applicant and offer evidence.

**Business-specific validation:** Run matched local searches for base and
specialty versions of each role, have technical staff verify capability, and
measure qualified applicants, acceptance, time-to-fill, and 12-month retention.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. It is sound to reject a universal scarce-skill premium because bundled duties and title inflation confound public data. Only matched local recruiting and retention evidence can show whether a premium actually causes better fill rates.

### LAB-004 — How do cost of living and commuting affect required compensation?

Consider housing, transportation, childcare, healthcare, taxes, commute time,
and the availability of suitable communities near industrial facilities.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Housing and childcare commonly set the compensation
floor, while commute time, vehicle dependence, tolls, and shift-time transit
can shrink the reachable workforce. Cost of living should inform—not dictate—
pay: required compensation depends on competing wages, household circumstances,
schedule, benefits, and how many qualified workers can reach the plant
reliably.

**Evidence:**
- [BEA regional price parities](https://www.bea.gov/data/prices-inflation/regional-price-parities-state-and-metro-area) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Measures annual state and metro price-level differences, including housing.
- [Census commuting data](https://www.census.gov/topics/employment/commuting.html) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  ACS and related products measure travel mode and time, but normal commute
  statistics may not represent overnight manufacturing shifts.
- [DOL National Database of Childcare Prices](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/wb/topics/NDCP-updated) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  County childcare-price estimates expose a major work constraint, though
  availability and shift-hour coverage still require local validation.
- [BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Shows that employer health and other benefits are a material part of
  compensation.

**Conflicting evidence:** Lower-cost exurbs may reduce housing expense but add
commute cost and unreliability; high-cost metros may require higher nominal pay
while offering deeper transit and labor pools.

**Confidence:** Medium. Direction is well supported, but effects are household,
shift, and site specific.

**Business-specific validation:** Map 30-, 45-, and 60-minute drive/transit
sheds by shift; survey candidates on housing, childcare, and transport; compare
decline reasons and acceptance at tested compensation packages.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Cost of living and commute burden clearly affect acceptance, but they do not mechanically set pay. Household constraints, shift timing, and local competitors matter more than generic cost-of-living indexes alone.

### LAB-005 — What benefits are required to compete for employees?

Compare health insurance, retirement contributions, paid time off, holidays,
shift premiums, training, tool allowances, uniforms, safety footwear, tuition,
relocation, bonuses, flexible schedules, and career progression.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** A credible manufacturing offer normally needs affordable
health coverage, paid time off/holidays, retirement support, predictable
scheduling, paid required PPE, and clear wage progression; scarce roles may
also require shift premiums, training/tuition, tools, relocation, or bonuses.
The competitive package must be benchmarked by occupation and metro, including
employee premium and deductible—not merely whether a benefit exists.

**Evidence:**
- [BLS National Compensation Survey benefits](https://www.bls.gov/ebs/) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Reports access and participation for health, retirement, leave, and other
  benefits by worker and establishment characteristics.
- [BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Quantifies broad employer benefit cost by industry and occupation group.
- [KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/report/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/) — **Evidence class:** empirical employer survey.
  Provides plan premiums, worker contributions, and deductibles; national
  results are not a local recruiting test.
- [DOL registered-apprenticeship evaluation](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ETA/publications/ETAOP_2012_10.pdf) — **Evidence class:** empirical research.
  The 10-state study found durable earnings benefits, supporting structured
  skill progression while not prescribing a specific employer package.

**Conflicting evidence:** National benefit prevalence can overstate what small
local firms offer, while tight skilled markets may require more. Workers also
value cash, schedule, insurance, or advancement differently.

**Confidence:** Medium. Core categories are clear; competitive design is local.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain local competitor plan summaries and
recruiter input, present two or three total-reward packages to qualified
candidates, and have benefits/payroll counsel verify ERISA, tax, and state-law
compliance.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The listed benefit categories are credible, but competitiveness depends on employee premium, deductible, predictability, and progression—not mere plan existence. Local testing is needed because small-firm norms vary widely.

## Employer Tax and Mandatory-Cost Questions

### LAB-006 — Which state and local payroll-related costs apply?

Include:

- state unemployment insurance rates and taxable wage bases;
- experience-rating effects;
- workers' compensation premiums and classification codes;
- state disability insurance;
- paid-family and medical-leave programs;
- paid-sick-leave mandates;
- local payroll, head, occupational, or employer taxes;
- employer healthcare or benefit mandates;
- training assessments; and
- other employment-related state or local charges.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Applicable cost is a site-and-headcount calculation, not
a state checklist. At minimum model federal FICA/FUTA; state UI rate and wage
base; workers' compensation class and experience rating; employer shares of
disability/paid-leave programs; paid-sick-time cost; training assessments; and
city/county payroll, occupational, or benefit mandates. Employee-funded
deductions still affect take-home pay and administration.

**Evidence:**
- [IRS employment taxes](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employment-taxes) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Defines baseline federal withholding, Social Security/Medicare, and FUTA
  obligations.
- [DOL state UI law comparison](https://oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/statelaws.asp) — **Evidence class:** official law summary.
  Publishes state taxable wage bases and rate structures, which change
  annually and with experience.
- [California payroll taxes](https://edd.ca.gov/en/payroll_taxes/what_are_state_payroll_taxes/) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Illustrates employer UI/training tax and employee disability/leave
  withholding in one jurisdiction.
- [Washington Paid Leave premium calculator](https://paidleave.wa.gov/estimate-your-paid-leave-payments/) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Shows headcount-dependent employer shares and annually changing premiums.
- [Seattle payroll expense tax](https://www.seattle.gov/city-finance/business-taxes-and-licenses/seattle-taxes/payroll-expense-tax) — **Evidence class:** official local guidance.
  Demonstrates that local payroll taxes can depend on total payroll and
  individual compensation thresholds.

**Conflicting evidence:** “No state income tax” does not mean low payroll cost,
and a high statutory maximum may not equal this employer's effective UI or
workers' compensation rate.

**Confidence:** High that these categories must be checked; low on amounts
before legal entity, location, payroll, headcount, and claims history are known.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain written state/local registrations and
rate notices, a workers' compensation quote by class, and a jurisdiction matrix
reviewed by qualified payroll/tax counsel and the broker before hiring.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The cost-category checklist is solid and appropriately warns that 'no state income tax' is not the same as low employer payroll cost. Exact amounts still need jurisdiction-specific notices and broker/payroll review.

### LAB-007 — How do workers' compensation costs differ for machining and manufacturing work?

Compare classification, injury history, experience modification, machine
guarding, lifting, welding, material handling, noise, repetitive motion, and
state insurance-market structure.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Premiums can differ materially because the same payroll
is multiplied by state-approved loss costs/rates, classification, insurer
loadings, and an employer-specific experience modifier. Machining, welding,
installation, clerical, and material-handling work may require different
codes; poor guarding, ergonomics, housekeeping, lifting, noise control, and
claims management raise expected loss even where manual rates are low.

**Evidence:**
- [Oregon 2024 workers' compensation premium ranking](https://digitalcollections.library.oregon.gov/nodes/view/317678) — **Evidence class:** official comparative study.
  Applies a common industry mix across states, useful for screening but not an
  employer quote.
- [NCCI ABCs of experience rating](https://www.ncci.com/ServicesTools/Pages/ABCSOFEXPRATING.aspx) — **Evidence class:** rating-organization guidance.
  Explains how actual versus expected losses adjust eligible employers'
  premiums in NCCI jurisdictions.
- [BLS Injuries, Illnesses, and Fatalities](https://www.bls.gov/iif/) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Provides industry injury rates and case characteristics, not insurer price.
- [OSHA machine guarding](https://www.osha.gov/machine-guarding) — **Evidence class:** official safety guidance.
  Identifies core machine hazards and controls relevant to loss prevention.

**Conflicting evidence:** Interstate premium indices may rank a state cheaply
while a particular machining class, assigned-risk placement, or adverse
experience modifier makes it expensive; monopolistic and competitive systems
also price differently.

**Confidence:** High on rating mechanics; low on site premiums without
operations and loss history.

**Business-specific validation:** Give licensed brokers the same payroll by
duty, subcontracting, travel/installation, safety program, and five-year loss
runs; confirm codes with the applicable rating authority and safety controls
with a qualified safety professional.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The answer is right that classification, state rate filing, and loss history matter more than a generic manufacturing label, but NCCI and state materials are directional rather than a quote. Real comparisons require actual class codes, payroll mix, and modifier history.

### LAB-008 — How do employee taxes affect the wages necessary to recruit workers?

Compare employee take-home pay after state and local income taxes, payroll
deductions, paid-leave contributions, disability programs, and commuting
costs. Link the analysis to:

- [Tax, Finance, and Capital Questions](tax-finance-and-capital.md)

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Candidates generally compare spendable pay and benefits,
so state/local income tax, mandatory disability or leave deductions, benefit
contributions, and commute expense can change the gross wage needed to make an
offer competitive. Do not gross up mechanically: household filing status,
residency, remote-work sourcing, benefits, and competing offers produce
different take-home outcomes.

**Evidence:**
- [IRS Tax Withholding Estimator](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/tax-withholding-estimator) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Supports individualized federal estimates rather than a single assumed tax
  rate.
- [California payroll taxes](https://edd.ca.gov/en/payroll_taxes/what_are_state_payroll_taxes/) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Distinguishes employer-paid UI/training tax from employee SDI/PFL and income
  tax withholding.
- [Philadelphia Wage Tax](https://www.phila.gov/services/payments-assistance-taxes/taxes/business-taxes/business-taxes-by-type/wage-tax-employers/) — **Evidence class:** official local guidance.
  Shows residence- and workplace-based local withholding with rates that
  change over time.
- [Washington Paid Leave premiums](https://paidleave.wa.gov/estimate-your-paid-leave-payments/) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Shows an employee share that directly reduces current take-home pay.

**Conflicting evidence:** A no-income-tax location may have higher housing,
sales/property taxes, insurance, or commuting costs; employees may also value
paid-leave insurance despite its deduction. Tax alone does not predict wage
acceptance.

**Confidence:** Medium. The mechanisms are clear, but recruiting response is
individual and local.

**Business-specific validation:** Build sample pay statements for representative
households and shifts with a qualified payroll provider/tax adviser, then test
whether candidates understand and accept the total-compensation comparison.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Employee-side taxes do affect take-home pay and therefore recruiting, but the effect varies by household status and local deductions. Do not convert tax differentials into hiring conclusions without live offer-acceptance evidence.

## Labor Law and Scheduling Questions

### LAB-009 — Which state and local employment rules materially change operating cost?

Investigate:

- minimum wage;
- overtime rules that exceed federal requirements;
- daily overtime;
- meal and rest periods;
- reporting-time and call-in pay;
- predictive or fair-workweek scheduling;
- split-shift premiums;
- required paid sick time;
- paid-family and medical leave;
- vacation payout;
- final-pay timing;
- pay-frequency rules;
- wage-statement requirements;
- expense reimbursement;
- employee-classification tests;
- temporary-worker obligations; and
- local employment ordinances.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** The largest operating differences arise where state or
local law adds daily overtime, higher minimum/exempt salary thresholds,
mandatory meal/rest or reporting-time pay, paid leave, final-pay/vacation rules,
expense reimbursement, stricter classification tests, or scheduling duties.
Manufacturing is often outside retail/hospitality fair-workweek laws, so every
rule must be checked for industry, headcount, worker, and city coverage rather
than inferred from headlines.

**Evidence:**
- [DOL state labor-law portal](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state) — **Evidence class:** official law index.
  Links state minimum-wage, payday, meal/rest, and related sources; local
  ordinances and recent changes require separate checks.
- [Federal overtime requirements](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/overtime) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Establishes the federal workweek baseline and notes that state rules may be
  more protective.
- [California overtime FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_overtime.htm) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Illustrates daily and seventh-day overtime beyond the federal weekly rule.
- [Oregon predictive-scheduling guidance](https://www.oregon.gov/boli/workers/pages/predictive-scheduling.aspx) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Shows substantial scheduling duties but also that coverage is limited to
  large retail, hospitality, and food-service employers—not ordinary
  manufacturing.
- [DOL paid-leave overview](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/wb/featured-paid-leave) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Confirms no general federal private-sector paid-leave guarantee and material
  state/local variation.

**Conflicting evidence:** Compliance summaries often call a jurisdiction
“high burden” without accounting for exemptions; conversely, state-only
matrices can miss city ordinances and wage-order rules.

**Confidence:** High on the categories; low on application until sites,
headcount, roles, and schedules are specified.

**Business-specific validation:** Have qualified employment counsel produce a
dated state-and-local matrix and have payroll validate timekeeping, wage
statements, leave accrual, final pay, reimbursement, and staffing-agency
workflows before operations begin.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. It is correct that wage-hour, leave, scheduling, safety, and local rules can materially change operating cost, but legal effect is state/local and employer-size specific. Counsel review is needed before treating any rule as applicable or pricing around it.

### LAB-010 — How do overtime and shift rules affect machine utilization?

Compare one-shift, two-shift, three-shift, weekend, compressed-workweek, and
overtime models while accounting for fatigue, supervision, maintenance,
quality, safety, and shift premiums.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** More scheduled spindle hours do not guarantee more good
output. A second or weekend shift can improve fixed-asset utilization, but only
if programming, setup, inspection, maintenance, material, and supervision are
available. Routine long overtime and rapid rotation can increase fatigue,
errors, injury exposure, absence, and turnover; compressed schedules can work
when rest, handoff, and state daily-overtime rules are designed explicitly.

**Evidence:**
- [DOL federal overtime guidance](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/overtime) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Requires time-and-a-half after 40 hours for covered nonexempt workers but
  does not cap adult weekly hours.
- [NIOSH overtime and extended-work-shift review](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/niosh/docs/2004-143/default.html) — **Evidence class:** empirical research review.
  Reports associations between long hours and adverse health/safety outcomes;
  study designs and occupations vary.
- [OSHA worker-fatigue guidance](https://www.osha.gov/worker-fatigue) — **Evidence class:** official safety guidance.
  Identifies long, irregular, and night shifts as fatigue risks.
- [BLS Injuries, Illnesses, and Fatalities](https://www.bls.gov/iif/) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Supports industry safety benchmarking but does not isolate a plant's shift
  design.

**Conflicting evidence:** Well-designed 10- or 12-hour schedules may improve
coverage and employee preference, while poorly controlled long/night work
raises risk. Evidence is heterogeneous; schedule label alone is not causal.

**Confidence:** Medium. Fatigue risk is established, but utilization and
quality effects depend on this process.

**Business-specific validation:** Pilot each shift pattern and measure staffed
hours, spindle uptime, setups, first-pass yield, scrap, incidents, absence, and
turnover; obtain employment-counsel and qualified safety/ergonomics review.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The direction is right, but overtime and shift rules affect utilization through fatigue, absenteeism, and maintenance windows as well as wage premiums. Legal minimums do not prove an efficient or safe schedule.

### LAB-011 — When do prevailing-wage rules apply?

Determine whether public contracts, grants, tax incentives, construction,
installation, project labor agreements, or local programs impose wage,
benefit, apprenticeship, reporting, or certified-payroll requirements.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Prevailing wage does not ordinarily govern private
factory production merely because the customer is public. It can apply to
federal or federally assisted construction/alteration/repair over the statutory
threshold, covered federal service contracts, and broader state/local public
works or incentive programs. Installation at a project site may be covered even
when off-site fabrication is not; the solicitation, funding statute, wage
determination, and place/classification of work control.

**Evidence:**
- [DOL Davis-Bacon and Related Acts](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/government-contracts/construction) — **Evidence class:** official law guidance.
  Covers laborers and mechanics on qualifying federal/federally assisted
  construction and includes prevailing fringe benefits.
- [DOL Davis-Bacon wage-determination FAQ](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/government-contracts/construction/faq) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Explains geographic, construction-type, classification, and revision rules.
- [DOL Service Contract Act](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/government-contracts/service-contracts) — **Evidence class:** official law guidance.
  Applies wage/fringe determinations to covered federal service contracts over
  $2,500.
- [SAM.gov wage determinations](https://sam.gov/content/wage-determinations) — **Evidence class:** official contract data.
  Provides the determinations incorporated into covered solicitations.

**Conflicting evidence:** “Government-funded” is overbroad because statutes and
contract clauses differ; assuming fabrication is always off-site and exempt is
also unsafe when work is dedicated to, adjacent to, or performed on the site.

**Confidence:** High on federal triggers; medium for unknown state/local and
incentive terms.

**Business-specific validation:** Before bidding or accepting incentives, have
qualified government-contract/employment counsel review funding flow, clauses,
site boundaries, classifications, fringe credit, apprenticeship/PLA terms,
certified payroll, and subcontract flow-downs.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The distinction between prevailing wage and union status is correct, but applicability turns on funding source, contract chain, site work, and classification detail. Solicitations and counsel review, not general summaries, control.

### LAB-012 — Which jurisdictions make employee classification especially consequential?

Evaluate employees, independent contractors, temporary labor, staffing
agencies, consultants, installers, field service, and remote professional
workers based on actual law and working conditions.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Classification is consequential everywhere because
federal wage-hour, tax, benefits, workers' compensation, unemployment, and
anti-discrimination regimes use different tests. Risk is higher where states
use broad ABC-style tests, joint-employer or wage-theft liability, or strict
staffing/temporary-worker rules. Core machinists, operators, inspectors, and
maintenance workers directed in the company's plant are especially difficult
to treat as independent businesses merely by contract.

**Evidence:**
- [DOL FLSA employment-relationship guidance](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/13-flsa-employment-relationship) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Applies an economic-realities analysis to actual working conditions.
- [IRS employee-versus-contractor guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/independent-contractor-self-employed-or-employee) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Uses behavioral control, financial control, and relationship factors for
  federal tax purposes.
- [California independent-contractor guidance](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_independentcontractor.htm) — **Evidence class:** official state guidance.
  Illustrates an ABC test with statutory exceptions and separate tests.
- [DOL state labor-law portal](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/state) — **Evidence class:** official law index.
  Supports jurisdiction-specific review rather than one national rule.

**Conflicting evidence:** A worker can be an independent contractor under one
law and an employee under another; staffing-agency payroll does not necessarily

**Confidence:** High on the control principle; low on headcount before process,
shift, and recovery targets exist.

**Business-specific validation:** Perform a role-by-role single-point-of-failure
and absence exercise, test recovery on every shift, verify customer/regulatory
authorization, and fund training or contracts for uncovered functions.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Treating redundancy as risk management rather than a generic ratio is correct, but required depth depends on process criticality, single-point skills, absence patterns, and response-time commitments. Vacation and incident coverage should be modeled separately from average demand.

## Training and Talent-Pipeline Questions

### LAB-018 — Which locations have strong machining and manufacturing training pipelines?

Evaluate apprenticeships, technical schools, community colleges, high-school
programs, university engineering programs, incumbent-worker training, state
grants, employer partnerships, and placement outcomes.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** A strong pipeline is not the number of program names; it
is recent occupation-relevant entrants, completions, placement, retention, and
employer-validated competence at usable commute distance. Compare CNC/
mechatronics/welding/metrology credentials, registered apprenticeships,
engineering awards, high-school CTE articulation, instructor/equipment
capacity, employer partnerships, and the share of graduates who enter and
remain in related local work.

**Evidence:**
- [NCES IPEDS data](https://nces.ed.gov/ipeds/use-the-data) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Measures postsecondary awards by program and institution, with lag and no
  direct competency measure.
- [Apprenticeship.gov data and statistics](https://www.apprenticeship.gov/data-and-statistics) — **Evidence class:** official program data.
  Provides registered-program participation and completions, but excludes some
  nonregistered training.
- [Census Post-Secondary Employment Outcomes](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/data/pseo/) — **Evidence class:** official experimental data.
  Links selected institutions/programs to earnings and employment geography;
  coverage is incomplete.
- [CareerOneStop training resources](https://www.careeronestop.org/toolkit/Training/training.aspx) — **Evidence class:** official directory.
  Helps discover programs but does not prove quality or current capacity.

**Conflicting evidence:** High enrollment can coexist with low completion,
outmigration, obsolete equipment, or graduates choosing other industries;
small employer-designed programs may outperform large generic ones.

**Confidence:** Medium for screening, low without outcome and employer evidence.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain three-year enrollment/completion/
placement data, inspect labs and curricula, interview employers and graduates,
and run a paid work-sample or cohort with each finalist partner.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The training-pipeline sources are appropriate first-pass evidence, but counts of schools or apprentices do not prove output quality, employer fit, or willingness to commute. Completion, placement, and employer interviews matter.

### LAB-019 — What does it cost to train an entry-level employee to productive independence?

Estimate wages during training, instructor and mentor time, machine access,
scrap, tooling, coursework, credentials, supervision, and expected retention.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** The cost is occupation-, process-, and starting-skill
specific and should be measured by cohort. Sum trainee wages/benefits,
instructor and mentor time, coursework/credentials, PPE/tools, machine and
fixture capacity, consumables, scrap/rework, additional inspection/supervision,
and safety controls through demonstrated independent work; then adjust for
completion and expected retention. Time-to-independence must require safe,
repeatable quality—not merely elapsed weeks.

**Evidence:**
- [DOL registered-apprenticeship evaluation](https://www.dol.gov/sites/dolgov/files/ETA/publications/ETAOP_2012_10.pdf) — **Evidence class:** empirical research.
  Found positive participant and social outcomes in 10 states, but does not
  supply current machining-employer unit cost.
- [BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Supports valuing benefits and paid training time beyond hourly wages.
- [OSHA training requirements in OSHA standards](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/osha2254.pdf) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Identifies required safety training that cannot be omitted from the cost and
  readiness gate.
- [O*NET OnLine](https://www.onetonline.org/) — **Evidence class:** official occupational information.
  Helps define task progression and competency, not employer training cost.

**Conflicting evidence:** Apprenticeship benefits do not mean every program has
positive employer ROI; faster apparent ramp-up may shift cost into scrap,
rework, incidents, or excessive mentor intervention.

**Confidence:** High on the cost model; low on dollars and duration.

**Business-specific validation:** Run a small paid cohort, time mentor and
machine use, record all failure/rework and assessments, and calculate cost per
retained independently qualified employee at 6, 12, and 24 months.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Training cost should be tied to time-to-competence, supervision, safety, and scrap rather than wage alone. Public averages cannot substitute for observing this shop's actual learning curve and failure costs.

### LAB-020 — Which incentives reduce workforce-development cost?

Consider training grants, apprenticeship tax credits, wage subsidies,
on-the-job training, incumbent-worker programs, equipment grants, and
community-college partnerships. Record eligibility, reimbursement timing,
reporting, wage commitments, and clawbacks.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** WIOA on-the-job and incumbent-worker funds, registered-
apprenticeship support/tax credits, state customized-training grants, community-
college contracts, and occasional equipment or wage subsidies can reduce cost.
None should enter the base case until the administering agency confirms
eligibility, approved costs, reimbursement rate/timing, wage and retention
commitments, reporting, procurement, related-party limits, and recapture terms
in writing.

**Evidence:**
- [DOL Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/eta/wioa) — **Evidence class:** official program guidance.
  Establishes the federal-state-local workforce framework; awards and
  priorities are locally administered.
- [20 CFR 680.700, on-the-job training](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-680/subpart-F/section-680.700) — **Evidence class:** primary regulation.
  Defines OJT as employer-provided training with limited-duration wage
  reimbursement for eligible participants.
- [20 CFR 680.780, incumbent-worker training](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-20/chapter-V/part-680/subpart-F/section-680.780) — **Evidence class:** primary regulation.
  Defines business and worker criteria for incumbent-worker support.
- [Apprenticeship.gov investments and tax credits](https://www.apprenticeship.gov/investments-tax-credits-and-tuition-support) — **Evidence class:** official program directory.
  Identifies federal/state support, but terms and availability require current
  state confirmation.

**Conflicting evidence:** Headline award amounts overstate value when funds are
reimbursement-based, restricted, delayed, competitive, or conditioned on jobs/
wages; a grant can add administration and constrain training design.

**Confidence:** Medium. Program types are established, availability is local
and time-sensitive.

**Business-specific validation:** Request term sheets from the local workforce
board, state economic-development/workforce agency, apprenticeship office, and
college; have qualified tax/employment counsel and finance model cash timing,
compliance, and clawback downside.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Incentive programs can reduce workforce-development cost, but they are jurisdiction-, size-, and compliance-specific and often impose paperwork or retention conditions. Do not book savings until eligibility and administrative burden are verified.

## Union and Workforce-Relations Questions

### LAB-021 — How do union presence and collective bargaining affect the business model?

Evaluate wages, benefits, apprenticeship, skill standards, scheduling,
seniority, work rules, grievance procedures, stability, productivity, safety,
retention, project access, and management capability without assuming union or
nonunion status is inherently better.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** The effect is bargaining-unit and contract specific.
Collective bargaining can raise or stabilize wages/benefits, provide
apprenticeship and skill standards, formalize safety and dispute resolution,
and improve retention or project access; it can also constrain unilateral
scheduling, assignments, subcontracting, progression, discipline, and benefit
design. Model the actual agreement, workforce, and management capability—do
not assume union or nonunion operations are inherently more productive.

**Evidence:**
- [BLS Union Members report](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/union2.htm) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Compares union representation and median weekly earnings, but differences are
  descriptive and reflect occupation, industry, geography, and worker mix.
- [29 U.S.C. § 157](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/157) — **Evidence class:** federal statute.
  Defines protected organizing, bargaining, and concerted activity relevant to
  covered employers.
- [Apprenticeship.gov data and statistics](https://www.apprenticeship.gov/data-and-statistics) — **Evidence class:** official program data.
  Documents registered pipelines, including joint labor-management programs,
  without proving a universal performance advantage.
- [Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service](https://www.fmcs.gov/services/collectivebargaining/collective-bargaining-mediation/) — **Evidence class:** official practice guidance.
  Shows formal bargaining/mediation infrastructure available to labor and
  management.

**Conflicting evidence:** Union wage differentials do not prove causation or
net productivity; case evidence varies by contract quality, technology,
management, labor relations, and product market.

**Confidence:** Medium. Effects are real but cannot be priced without a specific
unit/agreement and local organizing context.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain relevant agreements and benefit/
apprenticeship terms, interview both represented employers and worker
representatives, model work rules and grievances, and use qualified labor
counsel without interfering with protected rights.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The answer correctly rejects blanket pro- or anti-union productivity claims and keeps contract terms primary. Effects on cost and flexibility must be modeled from actual CBAs, apprenticeship terms, and labor-relations context.

### LAB-022 — Which customer contracts or project types favor or require union labor?

Consider public work, construction and installation, project labor agreements,
customer-site rules, and regulated or highly organized industries.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Ordinary product sales rarely require union labor by law.
Union participation may be required or favored by a project labor agreement,
customer-site access rule, collectively bargained subcontracting clause, or
project-specific public/private construction arrangement. Prevailing-wage laws
set wages/fringes and reporting but do not themselves require union membership.
Off-site fabrication and on-site installation can receive different treatment.

**Evidence:**
- [DOL Davis-Bacon and Related Acts](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/government-contracts/construction) — **Evidence class:** official law guidance.
  Requires prevailing wages/fringes on covered construction, not union status.
- [FAR Subpart 22.5, project labor agreements](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-22.5) — **Evidence class:** primary federal procurement rule.
  Governs PLA use on covered federal construction; current solicitation terms
  and any legal changes control.
- [DOL Service Contract Act](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/government-contracts/service-contracts) — **Evidence class:** official law guidance.
  May carry forward predecessor collective-bargaining rates on covered federal
  services without universally requiring union labor.
- [29 U.S.C. § 157](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/157) — **Evidence class:** federal statute.
  Frames protected organizing, bargaining, and concerted activity for covered
  workers.

**Conflicting evidence:** Buyers may describe union labor as “required” when it
is a site policy or commercial preference rather than statute; conversely,
ignoring a PLA or subcontracting clause can make an otherwise compliant bid
ineligible.

**Confidence:** High on the distinctions; low for unknown customer/project
documents.

**Business-specific validation:** Review every solicitation, prime contract,
site handbook, PLA/CBA, funding source, and installation scope with qualified
labor/government-contract counsel before pricing or staffing.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The distinction between prevailing wage and union requirement is important and correct. Actual PLAs, site rules, funding conditions, and subcontracting clauses still need document-level review.

## Location Comparison

### LAB-023 — What is the fully loaded annual cost per productive employee?

For each occupation and location, model:

```text
cash wages
+ overtime and shift premium
+ employer payroll taxes
+ unemployment
+ workers' compensation
+ mandatory leave and programs
+ healthcare and retirement
+ paid nonworking time
+ training and recruiting
+ tools, uniforms, and safety equipment
+ facility and supervisory support attributable to labor
- workforce grants and credits
= fully loaded employer labor cost
```

Then divide by realistic productive hours and expected good output rather than
nominal paid hours alone.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use the stated formula by role, level, shift, and site,
but calculate three denominators: paid hours, available production hours, and
earned good-output hours/units. Annual cost must include cash compensation,
employer taxes/insurance/leave, benefits, paid nonworking time, recruiting and
training amortization, PPE/tools, and attributable supervision/facility support,
net only of incentives that are approved and collectible. Overtime should
increase both cost and realistic fatigue/quality assumptions.

**Evidence:**
- [BLS Employer Costs for Employee Compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/ecec.htm) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Benchmarks wages and broad benefit cost, but not a particular occupation and
  metro.
- [BLS National Compensation Survey benefits](https://www.bls.gov/ebs/) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  Supports leave, insurance, and retirement assumptions.
- [IRS employment taxes](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/employment-taxes) — **Evidence class:** official guidance.
  Defines federal employer payroll-tax categories.
- [DOL state UI comparison](https://oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/statelaws.asp) — **Evidence class:** official law summary.
  Supplies changing state wage bases/rate structures.
- [Oregon workers' compensation premium ranking](https://www.oregon.gov/dcbs/reports/cost/Pages/oregon-vs-nation.aspx) — **Evidence class:** official comparative study.
  Supports interstate screening but must be replaced by class-specific quotes.

**Conflicting evidence:** National load factors are convenient but can badly
miss local benefits, claims, overtime, paid leave, turnover, and small-employer
pricing; grants may lower accounting cost while adding restrictions or delayed
cash.

**Confidence:** High on the model; low on outputs until roles and locations are
quoted.

**Business-specific validation:** Have finance, payroll, the benefits adviser,
and workers' compensation broker reconcile a per-role model to written quotes
and production engineering's measured good-output/productive-hour assumptions.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Fully loaded cost per productive employee should be built from wages, benefits, statutory load, training, turnover, and nonproductive time rather than wage alone. The remaining uncertainty is local input data, not the framework.

## Research Standards

Future analysis should:

1. State the year represented by each data source.
2. Compare metropolitan areas and local jurisdictions, not only state
   averages.
3. Use consistent occupations and experience levels.
4. Separate statutory cost from market compensation.
5. Include benefits, taxes, insurance, leave, compliance, and turnover.
6. Distinguish nominal hourly cost from cost per productive good part.
7. Prefer authoritative sources such as the Bureau of Labor Statistics,
   state labor agencies, workers' compensation rating organizations,
   unemployment agencies, local ordinances, and training institutions.
8. Identify uncertainty and avoid declaring one universally best location.
