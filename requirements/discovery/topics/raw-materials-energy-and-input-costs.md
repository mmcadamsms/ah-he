# Raw Materials, Energy, and Input Cost Questions

## Purpose

This topic tracks reliable, specification-compliant steel, aluminum, fuel,
electricity, and other production inputs at competitive total usable cost.
Public indexes establish context; only comparable delivered quotes, tariffs,
invoices, and operating measurements establish this business's cost.

## Question Supplied in Conversation

### INP-001 — Where can the business find reasonably priced wholesale steel, aluminum, fuel, and electricity?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use competitive regional RFQs for metals and fuels and
the serving utility's filed tariff for electricity; benchmark, but do not
substitute, national data. "Reasonable" means the lowest risk-adjusted total
usable cost for the same grade, form, quantity, certification, location, lead
time, and service. Every conclusion is quote-, site-, product-, and
date-specific.

**Evidence:**
- [FAR 15.404-1 price analysis](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/15.404-1) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official procurement rule. It favors competition,
  comparable historical prices, and market research rather than one posted
  price.
- [BLS Producer Price Index](https://www.bls.gov/ppi/) and
  [EIA energy data](https://www.eia.gov/) — **Evidence class:** official data.
  They show market movement and regional context, not a delivered small-buyer
  price or an industrial tariff bill.

**Conflicting evidence:** Indexes, online prices, and actual supplier or
utility bills can move differently because conversion, quantity, freight,
demand charges, taxes, and timing differ; the specification-matched delivered
quote and filed tariff control the decision.

**Confidence:** High on the sourcing method; low on any location or supplier
until actual requirements and quotes exist.

**Business-specific validation:** Issue identical RFQs to at least three
qualified sources per recurring item and model twelve months of interval load
against each candidate site's current tariff.

### INP-003 — When should steel be purchased from a mill, master distributor, service center, or local supplier?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Buy direct only when repeat volume clears mill/master
minimums and the freight, inventory, and processing savings exceed carrying
risk. Use service centers for recurring cut/processed stock and local suppliers
for prototypes, short drops, and urgent replenishment. The crossover is
product-, quantity-, credit-, and lane-specific.

**Evidence:**
- [FAR 15.404-1](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/15.404-1) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official procurement rule. Adequate competition
  and valid historical comparisons support price reasonableness.
- [Census Commodity Flow Survey](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cfs.html) —
  **Evidence class:** official observed shipment data. Freight demand varies by
  commodity, mode, distance, weight, and value.

**Conflicting evidence:** Mill unit prices can be lower while total cost is
higher after minimums, full-length yield loss, freight, storage, and lead time;
local unit prices can be higher but avoid machine idleness.

**Confidence:** Medium; the logic is sound but crossovers need quotes.

**Business-specific validation:** Quote one annual-repeat item and one urgent
prototype through all feasible tiers, including cuts, freight, lead time,
terms, returns, usable yield, and inventory carrying cost.

  **Evidence class:** official market data. It supplies production, trade, and

**Conflicting evidence:** Different indexes can diverge because they cover
different products, transaction stages, geographies, and time periods.

**Confidence:** High on benchmark limitations; medium on series selection.

**Business-specific validation:** Map each recurring SKU to a documented index,
record quote-to-index basis by supplier, and back-test against invoices.

### INP-006 — How should alloy, scrap, energy, freight, and tariff surcharges be handled?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Require every quote to state base price, each surcharge,
index and observation period, effective date, cap/floor, freight terms, tariff
assumption, and whether price can change after order acceptance. Treat
unbounded or retroactive adjustments as commercial risk and mirror only
verifiable exposure in customer quote terms.

**Evidence:**
- [USITC Harmonized Tariff Schedule](https://hts.usitc.gov/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official tariff source. Duties depend on
  classification, origin, and effective date.
- [EIA diesel update](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/) —
  **Evidence class:** official weekly market data. It can audit a stated fuel
  index but does not validate a carrier's formula.

**Conflicting evidence:** Supplier labels such as "alloy" or "energy"
surcharge may not track a public series one-for-one and can include commercial
margin.

**Confidence:** High on controls; low on future surcharge amounts.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain supplier formulas and two years of
quote/invoice history; have customs counsel or a broker confirm
classification-, origin-, and date-specific tariff exposure.

### INP-010 — When are remnants or near-net stock economical?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use remnants when documented identity, dimensions, and
condition are adequate and purchase savings exceed extra search, setup,
inspection, yield, and repeatability risk. Near-net stock is economical when
material and cycle-time savings exceed premium, tooling, qualification, and
minimum-order cost.

**Evidence:**
- [FAR 15.404-1](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/15.404-1) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official procurement rule. Total circumstances
  and comparable historical prices matter, not list price alone.

  **Evidence class:** observed implementation program. It emphasizes measured
  waste, defects, lead time, inventory, and capacity.

**Conflicting evidence:** A cheap drop may win a one-off job yet fail
traceability or repeat production; premium near-net stock may reduce machining
enough to win on total cost.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Business-specific validation:** Run paired job-cost trials recording purchase

  **Evidence class:** official aggregate data. It supplies state/sector context,

**Conflicting evidence:** Average industrial prices can conflict with a
modeled bill because customer load factor, demand peaks, riders, and eligibility
differ.

**Confidence:** High on method; low before site and load are known.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain written rate-class eligibility and a
utility bill simulation for each parcel using measured or defensible interval
loads.

**Evidence:**
- [NIST MEP lean and process improvement](https://www.nist.gov/mep/lean-and-process-improvement) —

- [NIST MEP supply-chain guidance](https://www.nist.gov/mep/supply-chain) —
  **Evidence class:** official practitioner/observed implementation guidance.
  It identifies inventory, delayed materials, schedules, and work stoppages as

**Conflicting evidence:** Bulk buying lowers unit price and shortage risk but
can worsen liquidity and obsolete certified or revision-specific stock.

**Confidence:** High on formula; low on amount.

**Business-specific validation:** Measure days on hand, turns, aged/obsolete

## Defining a Reasonable Price

### INP-022 — What total-cost formula should be used?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use: quoted material/energy + conversion/processing +
cutting/handling + freight/fuel + taxes/fees + incoming inspection + unusable
drop/yield loss + inventory carrying cost + expected delay/shortage cost minus
scrap recovery, rebates, credits, and discounts = total usable input cost.
Report per usable pound and per accepted finished part where possible.

**Evidence:**
- [FAR 15.404-1](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/15.404-1) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official procurement rule supporting price and
  cost analysis appropriate to the acquisition.

  **Evidence class:** observed implementation evidence that defects, waste,
  inventory, and lead time affect operating value.

**Conflicting evidence:** A supplier's "delivered" price can omit inspection,
yield, carrying, shortage, or disposal/recovery effects.

**Confidence:** High.

**Business-specific validation:** Reconcile the formula to at least twenty
invoices and completed job-cost records; define accounting ownership for every
field.

terms, ownership, or supply chains are not independent or comparable.

**Confidence:** Medium; the threshold is a policy choice.

**Business-specific validation:** Track quote spread, win/loss, supplier
