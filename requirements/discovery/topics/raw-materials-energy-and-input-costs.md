# Raw Materials, Energy, and Input Cost Questions

## Purpose

This topic tracks where a machining and manufacturing business can obtain
reasonably priced steel, aluminum, fuel, electricity, and related production
inputs.

The objective is not merely to find the lowest posted commodity price. The
business needs reliable, specification-compliant inputs at a competitive total
delivered and usable cost.

## Question Supplied in Conversation

### INP-001 — Where can the business find reasonably priced wholesale steel, aluminum, fuel, and electricity?

**State:** Open

**Original wording:** "need to look a raw material costs for wholesale steel, aluminum, fuel, electricity too ... where do we find reasonable prices"

The eventual answer must:

- define "reasonable" relative to grade, specification, form, quantity,
  location, lead time, certification, and service;
- compare national benchmarks with actual regional supplier quotes;
- separate commodity value from conversion, distribution, freight, cutting,
  handling, surcharges, and taxes;
- distinguish spot purchasing from contracts or index-linked pricing;
- account for minimum orders and inventory carrying cost;
- compare price reliability and availability, not only the lowest observed
  quote; and
- identify the data sources and quote process needed to keep the answer
  current.

## Steel Questions

### INP-002 — Which steel products will the business buy most often?

**State:** Open

Build purchasing scenarios for:

- hot-rolled and cold-finished carbon steel;
- alloy steel;
- tool steel;
- stainless steel;
- sheet and plate;
- round, square, rectangular, and hex bar;
- tube and pipe;
- structural shapes;
- castings and forgings; and
- specialty ground, hardened, or certified stock.

Price comparisons are invalid unless grade, condition, dimensions, tolerance,
surface finish, certification, and quantity are equivalent.

### INP-003 — When should steel be purchased from a mill, master distributor, service center, or local supplier?

**State:** Open

Compare:

- minimum order;
- price;
- freight;
- cut-to-size service;
- processing and handling;
- lead time;
- grade and size availability;
- certifications and traceability;
- credit terms;
- returns;
- remnant availability; and
- ability to respond to urgent prototype work.

### INP-004 — Which regional steel markets have strong supplier competition?

**State:** Open

Map mills, processors, service centers, warehouses, ports, rail, trucking,
scrap supply, and manufacturing demand. Determine where supplier density lowers
delivered cost or improves lead time.

### INP-005 — Which steel price benchmarks are relevant?

**State:** Open

Potential references include producer-price indexes, mill indexes, industry
price-reporting services, futures where relevant, import prices, tariffs,
surcharges, and actual supplier quotes.

The analysis must state which benchmark corresponds to each purchased product;
flat-rolled coil pricing does not directly equal the delivered price of a cut
piece of tool-steel bar.

### INP-006 — How should alloy, scrap, energy, freight, and tariff surcharges be handled?

**State:** Open

Determine whether quotes are fixed, indexed, separately surcharged, subject to
change before shipment, or adjusted after order acceptance.

## Aluminum Questions

### INP-007 — Which aluminum alloys and forms will the business buy?

**State:** Open

Include common machining alloys and forms such as:

- 6061 plate, sheet, bar, tube, and extrusion;
- 7075 plate and bar;
- cast tooling plate;
- MIC-6 or equivalent products;
- 2024 and other aerospace alloys;
- custom extrusions;
- castings and forgings; and
- certified or domestic-origin stock.

### INP-008 — What components make up the delivered aluminum price?

**State:** Open

Separate:

- underlying metal value;
- regional premium;
- alloy and conversion premium;
- rolling, extrusion, casting, or forging cost;
- temper and heat treatment;
- dimensional tolerance and flatness;
- certification;
- distributor margin;
- cutting and handling;
- freight; and
- tariffs or duties.

### INP-009 — Where are aluminum mills, extruders, plate suppliers, and service centers concentrated?

**State:** Open

Determine how proximity and supplier competition affect minimum quantity,
freight, lead time, alloy availability, and access to remnants or short drops.

### INP-010 — When are remnants or near-net stock economical?

**State:** Open

Compare lower acquisition cost against inconsistent availability, excess
setup, uncertain provenance, additional inspection, and inability to repeat a
production order.

## Electricity Questions

### INP-014 — What does electricity actually cost an industrial customer at each candidate site?

**State:** Open

Do not compare only the advertised cents-per-kilowatt-hour energy charge.
Model:

- energy charge;
- demand charge;
- ratchet demand;
- time-of-use periods;
- coincident peak charges;
- capacity and transmission charges;
- power-factor penalties or credits;
- fixed customer charges;
- riders and fuel adjustments;
- taxes and franchise fees;
- minimum bills;
- interruptible or curtailment programs; and
- seasonal rates.

### INP-015 — How does the expected machine load shape affect the utility bill?

**State:** Open

Build scenarios for:

- one machine versus multiple machines;
- simultaneous spindle startup;
- air compressors;
- coolant and chip systems;
- welding;
- heat treatment;
- HVAC and ventilation;
- metrology and office loads;
- one, two, and three shifts;
- unattended overnight machining; and
- battery, generator, or load-management options.

### INP-016 — What electrical infrastructure cost is required before production can begin?

**State:** Open

Consider:

- available voltage and phase;
- service amperage;
- transformers;
- switchgear;
- utility extensions;
- service upgrades;
- trenching and construction;
- lead time;
- deposits;
- power quality;
- outage frequency;
- backup power; and
- utility responsibility versus customer responsibility.

### INP-017 — Which regions combine competitive industrial electricity with adequate reliability?

**State:** Open

Compare utilities and specific rate classes, not only state averages. Include
reliability, restoration time, capacity availability, interconnection lead
time, rate-case risk, and exposure to extreme weather.

### INP-018 — Which efficiency investments have the best return?

**State:** Open

Evaluate:

- high-efficiency compressors;
- leak detection;
- variable-speed drives;
- machine standby management;
- demand control;
- thermal storage;
- efficient HVAC and ventilation;
- heat recovery;
- LED lighting;
- power-factor correction;
- solar;
- batteries; and
- utility rebates.

## Defining a Reasonable Price

### INP-022 — What total-cost formula should be used?

**State:** Open

Use a consistent comparison such as:

```text
quoted material or energy price
+ conversion and processing
+ cutting and handling
+ freight and fuel surcharge
+ taxes and fees
+ incoming inspection
+ unusable drop and yield loss
+ inventory carrying cost
+ expected delay or shortage cost
- scrap or recycling recovery
- rebates, credits, and discounts
= total usable input cost
```

### INP-023 — How many supplier quotes are enough?

**State:** Open

Define quote frequency and a supplier set appropriate to the item:

- local full-service supplier;
- regional service center;
- national distributor;
- mill or master distributor when volume permits;
- online metal supplier;
- remnant or surplus source; and
- customer-approved or certified supplier.

### INP-024 — When should the business prioritize availability over price?

**State:** Open

Consider customer downtime, prototype urgency, machine idle time, expedite
cost, contractual delivery, certification, substitution approval, and risk of
scrapping work because the material is unsuitable.

### INP-025 — How should price volatility be handled in customer quotes?

**State:** Open

Options may include:

- quote-validity periods;
- material escalation clauses;
- deposits;
- immediate material purchase after approval;
- index-linked adjustments;
- customer-supplied material;
- alternate approved materials; and
- contingency reserves.

## Data Sources and Procurement Evidence

### INP-026 — Which public sources establish market context?

**State:** Open

Potential sources include:

- US Energy Information Administration;
- Bureau of Labor Statistics producer-price indexes;
- Federal Reserve economic data;
- US Geological Survey mineral statistics;
- Department of Energy;
- utility tariffs and regulatory filings;
- regional grid operators;
- state energy offices;
- customs and trade data;
- ports and freight data; and
- industry associations.

Public indexes provide context but do not replace current supplier and utility
quotes for the required specification and location.

### INP-027 — What private or commercial price sources are justified?

**State:** Open

Evaluate paid metal-price services, distributor portals, procurement networks,
freight indexes, utility consultants, and energy brokers based on accuracy,
coverage, update frequency, licensing, and value to purchasing decisions.

### INP-028 — How should actual quotes and invoices become business intelligence?

**State:** Open

Record:

- supplier;
- location;
- date;
- material specification;
- dimensions and quantity;
- base price;
- surcharges;
- processing;
- freight;
- tax;
- promised and actual lead time;
- accepted substitutions;
- quality or certification issues;
- payment terms; and
- final usable yield.

Use the data to compare estimates with actual delivered costs and supplier
performance.
