# Lifecycle and Sustainability Questions

## Purpose

Questions about warranty, returns, repair, field failures, replacement, waste,
recycling, environmental impact, and end-of-life responsibilities. These are
discovery answers, not approved requirements. Question IDs remain permanent.
Contract, safety, waste, tax, and remedy conclusions are product-, customer-,
transaction-, date-, and jurisdiction-specific.

## Returns, Warranty, and Lifecycle

### LIF-001 — What happens when a part does not fit, function, or meet the approved specification?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Protect people/equipment first, stop use where risk is
credible, preserve the part and evidence, identify the approved revision and
acceptance criteria, and open a controlled nonconformance/return record. Provide
containment, then decide repair, remake, replacement, concession, refund, or
rejection from verified cause, contract, and applicable law.

**Evidence:**
- [NIST MEP lean and process improvement](https://www.nist.gov/mep/lean-and-process-improvement) —
  **Evidence class:** observed manufacturing-improvement program supporting
  structured defect and root-cause reduction.
- [CPSC duty to report](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Recall-Guidance/Duty-to-Report-to-the-CPSC-Your-Rights-and-Responsibilities) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official safety guidance. Covered consumer-
  product hazards may require prompt reporting before investigation is complete.

**Conflicting evidence:** Immediate replacement improves service, while cause
and responsibility may remain unresolved; neither should delay safety action.

**Confidence:** High on workflow; low on remedy without contract/product facts.

**Business-specific validation:** Have quality and counsel approve triage and
remedy rules; exercise them on realistic fit, function, specification, and
safety scenarios.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The lifecycle workflow is sound, but FTC and CPSC sources mainly cover consumer-warranty or safety contexts; custom B2B part remedies still depend on contract, state law, and technical causation evidence.

### LIF-003 — What warranty applies to prototypes versus production parts?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use separate written terms. Prototypes should state their
experimental purpose, approved requirements, test limits, and excluded uses;
production terms should define conformity, duration, remedy, exclusions, and
claim process. Disclaimers and remedy limits are not universally effective and
must be transaction- and jurisdiction-specific.

**Evidence:**
- [Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-50) —
  **Evidence class:** primary federal warranty law for covered consumer-product
  warranties; custom B2B work often depends principally on contract/state law.
- [16 CFR Part 701](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-701) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official consumer-warranty disclosure rule where
  applicable.

**Conflicting evidence:** Calling an item a prototype does not eliminate
express promises, safety duties, or mandatory law; production-like use can
undermine experimental assumptions.

**Confidence:** High that terms must differ; low on enforceability without facts.

**Business-specific validation:** Commercial counsel should draft terms for
customer type, product, state, industry, and use; validate that quotes,
drawings, acceptance, and app language are consistent.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The lifecycle workflow is sound, but FTC and CPSC sources mainly cover consumer-warranty or safety contexts; custom B2B part remedies still depend on contract, state law, and technical causation evidence.

## Sustainability and Waste

### SUS-004 — When should repair, remanufacture, or redesign be offered instead of a new part?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Offer the option when inspection can establish remaining
condition and traceability, engineering can restore required performance and
safety, customer/regulated approvals permit it, and total lifecycle cost and
impact beat replacement. Redesign is favored when recurrence or poor
repairability dominates one-off repair.

**Evidence:**
- [EPA Sustainable Materials Management](https://www.epa.gov/smm/sustainable-materials-management-basics) —
  **Evidence class:** official lifecycle guidance supporting source reduction,
  reuse, and recovery where impacts improve.
- [NIST MEP lean and process improvement](https://www.nist.gov/mep/lean-and-process-improvement) —
  **Evidence class:** observed implementation evidence supporting defect,
  waste, lead-time, and capacity reduction.

**Conflicting evidence:** Repair saves material but can add inspection, cleaning,
transport, uncertain life, and liability; new manufacture may be safer or more
efficient.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Business-specific validation:** Run engineering tear-down and life/cost
studies on candidate families; compare accepted-life, warranty return, energy/
material, lead time, and customer approval with new parts.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The repair and remanufacture logic is sensible, but remaining-life, liability, and approval limits need engineering evidence rather than assumed environmental benefit.

### SUS-005 — Which environmental claims can be supported with evidence?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Make only specific, qualified claims whose defined
boundary, method, baseline, period, geography, and evidence are retained—for
example measured material yield, energy per accepted part, verified recycled
content, or documented recovery outcome. Avoid broad unqualified "green,"
"eco-friendly," "recyclable," "zero waste," or carbon claims.

**Evidence:**
- [FTC Green Guides](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-260) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official environmental-marketing guidance on
  qualifications and substantiation for general and specific claims.
- [EPA WARM](https://www.epa.gov/warm) —
  **Evidence class:** official lifecycle screening model for waste-management
  greenhouse-gas and energy comparisons; model outputs are not product-specific
  proof without appropriate data and boundaries.

**Conflicting evidence:** Supplier certificates, generic databases, modeled
estimates, and measured facility results have different boundaries and cannot
be combined as if equivalent.

**Confidence:** High on claim discipline; low on any claim before measurement.

**Business-specific validation:** Create a claims register with source data,
calculation, version, owner, expiry, and approved wording; obtain technical and
legal review, and independently verify material claims where customer or market
risk warrants.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The claim discipline is right, and the Green Guides remain current, but modeled tools such as WARM are only screening aids unless backed by measured, product-specific data.
