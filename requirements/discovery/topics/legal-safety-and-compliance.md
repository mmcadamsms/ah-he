# Legal, Safety, and Compliance Questions

## Purpose

Questions about liability, regulation, prohibited work, insurance, human oversight, hazards, and safe operating boundaries.

These are discovery questions, not approved requirements. Question IDs are permanent and canonical in this file.

## Legal, Liability, and Regulatory Boundaries

### LEG-001 — Which products or applications are prohibited?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** There is no universal prohibited-product list. Intake should reject unlawful, deceptive, counterfeit, or sanctions-prohibited work and place a legal/export hold on weapons or defense articles, nuclear or missile uses, safety-critical medical or aviation parts, banned consumer products, and any order with an undisclosed or implausible end use. A qualified counsel and, for cross-border or controlled-technology work, an export professional must clear the product, customer, destination, end user, and end use before acceptance; this is a screening policy, not jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

**Evidence:**
- [EAR Part 736 — General Prohibitions](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/subtitle-B/chapter-VII/subchapter-C/part-736) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. The prohibitions can apply because of the item, destination, end user, end use, or knowledge of a violation, not merely because a product appears on a list.
- [ITAR Part 121 — United States Munitions List](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-M/part-121) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Defense articles and related technical data require a separate jurisdiction and authorization analysis.
- [CPSC duty to report](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Recall-Guidance/Duty-to-Report-to-the-CPSC-Your-Rights-and-Responsibilities) — **Evidence class:** regulator guidance. Consumer-product firms have immediate reporting duties for specified defects, unreasonable risks, and violations; suspect products cannot be treated as ordinary jobs.
- [21 CFR Subchapter H — Medical Devices](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Medical-device manufacturers and contract manufacturers can face classification, registration, quality-system, premarket, and reporting controls.

**Conflicting evidence:** EAR99 items and ordinary components are often licensable without prior authorization, and contract manufacturers may have narrower duties than finished-product manufacturers. Neither fact makes the work unrestricted; actual classification, intended use, customer role, and jurisdiction control.

**Confidence:** High on the need for a gated, fact-specific screen; medium on any particular product until qualified review.

**Business-specific validation:** Have qualified counsel and an export professional approve a written prohibited/restricted-work matrix for every served jurisdiction and market, with licensed engineer or product-regulatory review for safety-critical applications.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The intake-hold approach is correct, but prohibited or restricted status is jurisdiction-, end-use-, and customer-specific; no generic source list can clear work. Export, product-safety, and sector counsel must own the final screen.

### LEG-002 — What terms allocate responsibility for customer-provided requirements and operating conditions?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** The MSA and statement of work should say that the customer owns and warrants the authority, completeness, and accuracy of its specifications, materials, intended use, environment, interfaces, and regulatory declarations; the manufacturer owns conformance to the accepted drawing, process, and agreed quality plan. Use documented assumptions, order-of-precedence and change-control clauses, acceptance criteria, access to necessary operating data, mutual notice duties, and proportionate indemnities. Terms must not purport to transfer nondelegable safety, reporting, professional, or statutory duties; qualified counsel must tailor them to the governing law.

**Evidence:**
- [California Commercial Code § 2313](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=COM&sectionNum=2313) — **Evidence class:** state statute example. Descriptions, samples, and factual promises can create express warranties, supporting disciplined control of proposals and specifications.
- [California Commercial Code § 2316](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=COM&sectionNum=2316) — **Evidence class:** state statute example. Warranty exclusions must satisfy specific wording and conspicuousness rules.
- [California Commercial Code § 2719](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=COM&sectionNum=2719) — **Evidence class:** state statute example. Commercial parties may structure remedies, but unconscionability and failure of essential purpose limit that freedom.
- [OSH Act § 5 duties](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/oshact/section5-duties) — **Evidence class:** federal statute. An employer's workplace-safety duties remain with the employer despite customer contract language.

**Conflicting evidence:** Sophisticated commercial parties usually can allocate substantial economic risk, but product liability, fraud, gross negligence, worker safety, licensed practice, and regulatory reporting may be nonwaivable or treated differently by state.

**Confidence:** High on the allocation structure; medium on enforceability because governing law and bargaining context matter.

**Business-specific validation:** Qualified counsel should review the actual MSA, quote, drawing hierarchy, customer flow-downs, insurance, indemnity, and intended-use questionnaire in each governing jurisdiction.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The allocation structure is sound, but contract language cannot waive nondelegable duties or fully reassign product-liability or regulatory exposure. Enforceability depends on governing law, bargaining context, and the facts the customer actually provided.

### LEG-003 — What professional engineering, export control, product safety, environmental, consumer protection, or industry regulations apply?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Applicability must be determined per job through a regulatory matrix covering where the company and customer operate, what is designed or made, who uses it, and its end use. Likely gates include state engineering-practice and sealing rules; EAR/ITAR and sanctions; CPSC or FDA product regimes; EPA and state air, water, waste, and chemical rules; FTC advertising and warranty rules; and sector flow-downs such as aerospace, medical, automotive, defense, food, or pressure equipment. Qualified counsel, an export professional, a licensed engineer, an industrial hygienist, a machine-safety engineer, or another safety professional must review the portions within their competence.

**Evidence:**
- [Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1001](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/OC/htm/OC.1001.htm) — **Evidence class:** state statute example. This illustrates that engineering practice, firm registration, responsible charge, and sealing are state-defined rather than nationally uniform.
- [BIS Export Administration Regulations](https://www.bis.gov/regulations/ear) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation portal. Export controls reach commodities, software, technology, end users, end uses, and releases to foreign persons.
- [21 CFR Subchapter H — Medical Devices](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Device classification and the firm's role determine registration, premarket, quality, labeling, and reporting obligations.
- [EPA hazardous-waste generator requirements](https://www.epa.gov/hwgenerators) — **Evidence class:** regulator guidance. Generator category and waste streams determine federal duties, with potentially stricter state programs.
- [15 U.S.C. § 45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45) — **Evidence class:** federal statute. Unfair or deceptive acts and claims create consumer-protection exposure beyond technical conformance.

**Conflicting evidence:** Component suppliers and business-to-business shops may be outside some finished-product or consumer rules, while their contracts or actual role can still make them a regulated manufacturer, specification developer, exporter, or engineering provider. Labels chosen by the parties do not settle applicability.

**Confidence:** High that a job-level matrix is required; low on the final set of rules before the business locations, processes, products, and markets are fixed.

**Business-specific validation:** Qualified counsel should lead a jurisdiction and industry assessment; an export professional, licensed engineer, industrial hygienist, machine-safety engineer, and environmental or product-safety professional should sign off where their disciplines apply.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. It is correct that applicability must be job- and jurisdiction-specific, not inferred from company labels. Export, engineering-practice, environmental, and product-regulatory review should remain separate professional gates.

### LEG-004 — What insurance is required?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** There is no universal package or limit. The likely baseline is statutory workers' compensation and employers' liability, commercial general liability including products/completed operations, property and equipment-breakdown coverage, and commercial auto when vehicles are used. Product recall, professional/errors-and-omissions, cyber, pollution, inland marine, umbrella/excess, and key-person or business-interruption coverage depend on the services and contracts. A qualified commercial insurance broker and counsel must reconcile legal minimums, customer or lease requirements, exclusions, deductibles, and realistic loss severity.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA guidance on business insurance](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/get-business-insurance) — **Evidence class:** federal agency guidance. It identifies common coverage categories but confirms that need depends on business risks.
- [California workers' compensation information for employers](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dwc/employer.htm) — **Evidence class:** state authority example. Employers generally must carry workers' compensation in that state; other states set their own thresholds and mechanisms.
- [FMCSA insurance filing requirements](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/registration/insurance-filing-requirements) — **Evidence class:** federal regulator guidance. Regulated interstate motor carriers face activity-specific financial-responsibility filings.
- [FAR 28.307-2 — Liability](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/28.307-2) — **Evidence class:** federal procurement regulation. Government contracts may prescribe workers' compensation, employer's liability, general liability, automobile, and vessel or aircraft coverage.

**Conflicting evidence:** A certificate or stated policy limit does not prove a loss is covered; professional services, pollution, cyber events, recalls, controlled products, and assumed contractual liability may be excluded. Conversely, buying every specialty policy before exposure exists may be uneconomic.

**Confidence:** Medium; coverage categories are stable, but requirements and limits are business- and contract-specific.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain a broker-led exposure study using projected revenue, payroll, products, jurisdictions, machine values, largest credible injury/property loss, customer terms, and subcontractor certificates, then have qualified counsel review gaps and indemnity alignment.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The baseline coverage list is reasonable, but 'required' varies by statute, vehicles, leases, customers, lenders, and contracts, and certificates do not prove adequacy. Broker and counsel review remain necessary before treating any package as sufficient.

### LEG-005 — How are warranties, remedies, limitation of liability, and consequential damages handled?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Put them in a counsel-approved, conspicuous contract: narrowly define the conformance warranty and warranty period; state exclusions and customer duties; provide a workable repair, replace, reperform, or refund remedy; set a defensible liability cap; and address indirect, special, incidental, and consequential damages. Carve-outs should reflect governing law and negotiated risks such as fraud, willful misconduct, confidentiality/IP breaches, bodily injury, and obligations that cannot lawfully be limited. Consumer transactions require separate review; this is not a definitive conclusion on enforceability in any jurisdiction.

**Evidence:**
- [California Commercial Code § 2313](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=COM&sectionNum=2313) — **Evidence class:** state statute example. Factual promises, descriptions, samples, and models can become express warranties without using the word “warranty.”
- [California Commercial Code § 2316](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=COM&sectionNum=2316) — **Evidence class:** state statute example. Implied-warranty disclaimers are subject to wording and conspicuousness requirements.
- [California Commercial Code § 2719](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=COM&sectionNum=2719) — **Evidence class:** state statute example. Limited remedies can fail of essential purpose, and consequential-damage exclusions remain subject to unconscionability.
- [Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-50) — **Evidence class:** federal statute. Written consumer warranties trigger federal disclosure and implied-warranty constraints not assumed for negotiated commercial sales.

**Conflicting evidence:** Commercial limitation clauses are often enforced, but outcomes turn on drafting, notice, bargaining power, remedy performance, type of loss, public policy, and state law. A clause that works for economic loss may not limit personal-injury or statutory liability.

**Confidence:** High on the drafting framework; medium on any clause's enforceability without jurisdiction- and transaction-specific analysis.

**Business-specific validation:** Qualified counsel should produce separate commercial-product, services/design, prototype, and consumer-facing forms and test them against insurance, customer flow-downs, and the jurisdictions selected in the contract.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. This is a good commercial-contract framework, but remedy caps and consequential-damage waivers are state-law and transaction specific and may fail for personal injury, statutory claims, or badly performed limited remedies. Counsel drafting is essential.

### LEG-006 — How are patents, trade secrets, copyrights, design rights, and customer IP handled?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Contracts should distinguish each party's pre-existing IP, customer materials and controlled data, project inventions, drawings/CAD/CAM, software, tooling, process know-how, and deliverables. State ownership, license scope, assignment timing, employee/contractor invention and copyright assignments, confidentiality, permitted subcontracting, return/destruction, residual knowledge, open-source use, and infringement procedures. Protect access and provenance technically as well as contractually, and have qualified IP counsel address patent clearance, design rights, trade-secret measures, and jurisdiction-specific ownership.

**Evidence:**
- [35 U.S.C. § 261](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title35-section261&num=0&edition=prelim) — **Evidence class:** federal statute. Patents and patent applications are assignable by a written instrument.
- [Defend Trade Secrets Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1836](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1836&num=0&edition=prelim) — **Evidence class:** federal statute. Trade-secret remedies depend in part on information being secret and subject to reasonable protective measures.
- [Copyright Office Circular 30 — Works Made for Hire](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ30.pdf) — **Evidence class:** federal authority guidance. Commissioning and paying for work does not automatically make every work a statutory work made for hire.
- [USPTO patent basics](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics) — **Evidence class:** federal authority guidance. Patent protection is claim- and jurisdiction-dependent and differs from copyright or trade-secret protection.

**Conflicting evidence:** Customers may expect payment to transfer all output, while default patent and copyright ownership can remain with individual creators absent effective assignments; broad customer ownership can also unintentionally transfer reusable shop know-how. Contract language must resolve, not assume, that conflict.

**Confidence:** High on the need to separate IP categories and obtain written rights; medium on ownership in any existing relationship without reviewing facts and law.

**Business-specific validation:** Qualified IP counsel should inventory founders, employees, contractors, customer agreements, software and model licenses, prior disclosures, export-controlled data, and reusable process IP, then implement assignments and access controls.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The IP categories and assignment/control themes are right, but ownership defaults and enforceability vary across patents, copyrights, trade secrets, contractors, and jurisdictions. Operational secrecy controls are as important as paper assignments.

### LEG-007 — What records must be retained for legal defensibility?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use a counsel-approved retention schedule and legal-hold process, not one blanket period. Preserve contract versions, requirements and assumptions, design/risk reviews, professional approvals, export screens and licenses, material and process traceability, machine programs and revisions, inspections, nonconformance/CAPA, training, maintenance, incidents, complaints, shipments, and customer communications in auditable form. Keep records for the longest applicable statutory, contractual, insurer, limitation/repose, product-life, and litigation-hold period; privacy and secure-destruction duties also apply.

**Evidence:**
- [29 C.F.R. § 1904.33](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1904/1904.33) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Covered OSHA injury and illness records generally must be retained for five years after the calendar year they cover.
- [EAR Part 762 — Recordkeeping](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-15/subtitle-B/chapter-VII/subchapter-C/part-762) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Export-control records generally have a five-year retention framework and prescribed accessibility.
- [ITAR § 122.5 — Records](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-M/part-122/section-122.5) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Defense-trade registrants must maintain specified records, generally for five years.
- [40 C.F.R. § 262.40](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-I/part-262/subpart-D/section-262.40) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Hazardous-waste generators face three-year federal record periods for manifests and specified reports, subject to extensions and state rules.

**Conflicting evidence:** Mandatory periods differ and are often minimums; retaining everything forever can increase privacy, discovery, security, and storage exposure. Destruction must stop immediately when litigation, investigation, recall, or audit is reasonably anticipated.

**Confidence:** High that category-specific retention and legal holds are required; medium on durations until jurisdictions, products, and contracts are known.

**Business-specific validation:** Qualified counsel, an export professional, environmental/safety professionals, the insurer, and records/security owners should map every record class to authority, owner, format, retention trigger, hold rule, and defensible destruction method.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Category-specific retention plus legal hold is the correct structure and is better than a single blanket period. Durations still need to be mapped to the governing statutes, contracts, and product life.

### LEG-008 — When must legal or regulatory review block a project?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** A mandatory hold should stop quotation, design, data access, purchasing, production, release, or shipment at the stage affected whenever jurisdiction/classification is unresolved; a sanctions, end-user, end-use, bribery, counterfeit, IP, or privacy red flag remains; regulated-product authorization or licensed-engineering approval is missing; requirements are materially incomplete; risk controls are unverified; or a defect, incident, environmental release, or reporting duty may exist. Only the named qualified counsel, export professional, licensed engineer, industrial hygienist, machine-safety engineer, safety professional, or regulator should clear the corresponding hold.

**Evidence:**
- [BIS “Know Your Customer” and red-flags guidance, Supplement No. 3 to Part 732](https://www.bis.gov/regulations/ear/732) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation and guidance. Unresolved abnormal circumstances require inquiry rather than proceeding self-blinded.
- [CPSC duty to report](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Recall-Guidance/Duty-to-Report-to-the-CPSC-Your-Rights-and-Responsibilities) — **Evidence class:** regulator guidance. Potential substantial product hazards and violations can trigger immediate reporting rather than ordinary internal disposition.
- [OSH Act § 5 duties](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/oshact/section5-duties) — **Evidence class:** federal statute. Production pressure does not displace the duty to address recognized serious workplace hazards.
- [Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1001](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/OC/htm/OC.1001.htm) — **Evidence class:** state statute example. Work within regulated engineering practice must be performed and approved under the state's professional framework.

**Conflicting evidence:** Not every uncertainty requires a company-wide stop; a scoped hold can isolate affected data, operation, lot, or shipment. However, deadlines, sunk cost, customer assurances, or an AI-generated conclusion are not competent clearance.

**Confidence:** High on the stop-work categories; medium on exact triggers and approvers until a jurisdictional compliance matrix exists.

**Business-specific validation:** Qualified counsel should establish a stage-gate and escalation matrix with documented authority, response times, evidence required to release a hold, regulator-reporting clocks, and anti-retaliation protection.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The hold triggers are appropriately cautious and correctly reject schedule pressure or AI output as clearance. Release authority must remain with the named qualified professional for the relevant discipline.

### LEG-009 — How are controlled technical data and export-restricted work identified?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Screen before accepting files or granting access. Capture item/technology description, origin and prior classifications, customer and beneficial owner, all destinations, end users/end uses, foreign-person access, subcontractors, defense/nuclear/missile context, and sanctions indicators. A qualified export professional, with counsel where jurisdiction is uncertain, must determine EAR scope and ECCN or EAR99 status, ITAR/USML jurisdiction, license exceptions or authorizations, party screening, deemed-export controls, technology-control measures, and recordkeeping; re-screen changes and shipment.

**Evidence:**
- [BIS Export Administration Regulations](https://www.bis.gov/regulations/ear) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation portal. Classification is only one part of EAR analysis; general, destination, end-use, end-user, and activity restrictions also apply.
- [BIS end-user and end-use controls guidance](https://www.bis.gov/licensing/guidance-on-end-user-and-end-use-controls-and-us-person-controls) — **Evidence class:** regulator guidance. Certain military, intelligence, weapons, nuclear, missile, and other uses or users create controls independent of ordinary item classification.
- [Consolidated Screening List](https://www.trade.gov/consolidated-screening-list) — **Evidence class:** federal screening tool. It consolidates multiple U.S. restricted-party lists but does not replace ownership, end-use, or sanctions analysis.
- [ITAR Part 120 definitions](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-M/part-120) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Defense article, technical data, defense service, export, release, and U.S. person definitions determine whether file access itself is controlled.

**Conflicting evidence:** EAR99 does not mean “uncontrolled,” a customer's classification is useful but not conclusive, and cloud access or an oral/visual release can matter even without a physical export. Conversely, not every drawing for a government or aerospace customer is ITAR-controlled.

**Confidence:** High on the required workflow; low on any job's classification without the technical data and parties.

**Business-specific validation:** Engage a qualified export professional and export counsel to classify the actual product and technology set, verify ownership and sanctions screening, approve a technology-control plan, configure access by nationality/location, and test records before work begins.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The workflow is sound and correctly rejects common mistakes such as treating EAR99 or customer classifications as conclusive. Actual classification and technology-control decisions still need qualified export review.

## Safety and Human Oversight

### SAF-001 — Which decisions must always be made or approved by a qualified human?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** A qualified human must own hazard identification and risk acceptance; requirements and intended-use approval; safety-function architecture and validation; production release of designs, tooling, fixtures, programs, and process changes; overrides or degraded modes; regulatory/export classification; nonconformance disposition affecting safety; incident reporting and restart; and emergency response. Automation may calculate, simulate, draft, or flag, but cannot be the accountable approver. The approver must have authority to stop work and be a licensed engineer, industrial hygienist, machine-safety engineer, safety professional, export professional, or counsel where that competence is required.

**Evidence:**
- [OSH Act § 5 duties](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/oshact/section5-duties) — **Evidence class:** federal statute. Workplace responsibility rests with the employer; software cannot assume the statutory duty.
- [ISO 12100:2010](https://www.iso.org/standard/51528.html) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. Machinery safety requires a documented designer-led process of hazard identification, risk estimation/evaluation, and risk reduction across foreseeable use and misuse.
- [ISO 13849-1:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/73481.html) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. Safety-related control-system design and integration require defined safety functions and achieved performance, including software aspects.
- [NIOSH hierarchy of controls](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/hierarchy-of-controls/about/index.html) — **Evidence class:** federal safety guidance. Elimination and engineering controls are preferred over procedures and PPE, requiring competent judgment rather than automated acceptance of a low score.

**Conflicting evidence:** Automated checks can be more consistent than unaided review and may stop obvious errors, but evidence does not support delegating accountable safety or legal judgment to an unvalidated model. Human approval alone is also insufficient without competence, information, independence, and verification.

**Confidence:** High on retaining accountable qualified-human approval; medium on the exact role matrix until operations are defined.

**Business-specific validation:** A qualified safety professional and machine-safety engineer should create a RACI and competence matrix; counsel, an export professional, licensed engineers, and an industrial hygienist must define the decisions legally reserved to them.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Human approval is correctly reserved for safety-critical decisions, but the exact boundary should be tied to hazard severity, regulatory duties, and competence requirements rather than a generic preference for manual review. Automation can assist, not own, accountability.

### SAF-002 — How are hazards identified across design, manufacturing, use, maintenance, and disposal?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use one lifecycle hazard process beginning with intended use, reasonably foreseeable misuse, users, environments, materials, and operating modes. At concept and every change, identify hazards for transport, installation, setup, normal and abnormal operation, cleaning, jam clearing, maintenance, fault recovery, decommissioning, and disposal; trace each hazardous situation through consequence, exposed person, controls, verification, residual risk, warnings, and owner. A cross-functional review led by a qualified safety professional or machine-safety engineer should incorporate operator and maintenance experience, incidents, SDS data, and environmental requirements.

**Evidence:**
- [ISO 12100:2010](https://www.iso.org/standard/51528.html) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. It provides the core machinery lifecycle methodology for hazard identification, risk assessment, and iterative risk reduction.
- [NIOSH Prevention through Design](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/ptd/about/index.html) — **Evidence class:** federal safety guidance. NIOSH recommends anticipating and designing out hazards in equipment, processes, tools, facilities, new technology, and work organization.
- [OSHA hazard identification and assessment](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/hazard-Identification) — **Evidence class:** federal safety guidance. Effective programs use routine inspections, incident investigation, emergency/nonroutine analysis, trend data, and worker participation.
- [EPA hazardous-waste generator requirements](https://www.epa.gov/hwgenerators) — **Evidence class:** regulator guidance. Waste characterization, storage, shipment, and disposal create lifecycle duties beyond point-of-use worker safety.

**Conflicting evidence:** Checklists and prior machine designs accelerate identification but can miss novel interactions, maintenance work, software/control failure, and foreseeable misuse. Conversely, listing every imaginable harm without exposure or scenario analysis obscures the significant hazards.

**Confidence:** High on the lifecycle method; medium on completeness until real operators, machines, materials, and waste streams are observed.

**Business-specific validation:** A qualified safety professional or machine-safety engineer should facilitate design and task risk assessments with operators and maintainers; an industrial hygienist, licensed engineer, fire professional, and environmental specialist must assess their relevant hazards.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Lifecycle hazard identification is the right method, but completeness depends on actual tasks, maintenance states, foreseeable misuse, and chemical or environmental conditions. A documented multidisciplinary assessment is still required.

### SAF-003 — How is severity distinguished from probability and detectability?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Record them separately. Severity is the worst credible harm; probability is the combined likelihood of exposure, hazardous-event occurrence, and failure to avoid or limit harm. Detectability is the likelihood that a defect or developing condition will be found before harm and belongs in an FMEA or monitoring analysis, not as an automatic discount to machinery risk. Do not let a high detection score neutralize catastrophic severity or replace required safeguarding; a qualified safety professional or machine-safety engineer must approve the method.

**Evidence:**
- [ISO 12100:2010](https://www.iso.org/standard/51528.html) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. Machinery risk estimation distinguishes severity of harm from probability factors and uses the result in iterative risk reduction.
- [IEC 60812:2018](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/26359) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. FMEA/FMECA is a failure-mode method that can use severity and other prioritization measures; it is not by itself a machinery safety case.
- [OSHA hazard identification and assessment](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/hazard-Identification) — **Evidence class:** federal safety guidance. OSHA recommends prioritizing hazards using severity and likelihood while immediately controlling hazards that present imminent danger or serious harm.

**Conflicting evidence:** Some industries use severity-occurrence-detection risk priority numbers, while machinery standards commonly evaluate severity and probability without detectability as a coequal factor. Simple multiplication can rank different risk profiles identically and hide low-frequency catastrophic hazards.

**Confidence:** High on keeping the dimensions distinct; medium on scoring bands until the applicable sector standard is selected.

**Business-specific validation:** Have a qualified safety professional or machine-safety engineer select and document the applicable risk graph/matrix and FMEA method, calibrate examples with actual tasks and incident data, and prohibit score-only acceptance.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Separating severity, probability, and detectability is the correct analytical boundary and avoids false reassurance from high detection scores. The remaining task is choosing the sector-appropriate method and examples.

### SAF-004 — What risk level requires rejection or redesign?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** No universal numeric score is defensible. Reject or redesign whenever law or an applicable standard is not met; a credible fatality, permanent impairment, fire/explosion, ejection, crushing, toxic exposure, or uncontrolled startup lacks effective engineering controls; a required safety function is unverified or can be defeated by ordinary software/network failure; or residual risk has not been accepted by the authorized qualified person. Apply inherently safe design, then engineering safeguards, then information/procedures and PPE; cost or schedule alone cannot accept an intolerable risk.

**Evidence:**
- [ISO 12100:2010](https://www.iso.org/standard/51528.html) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. Risk reduction is iterative and prioritizes inherently safe design, safeguarding/protective measures, and information for use.
- [29 C.F.R. § 1910.212](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.212) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Machines must use guarding methods to protect operators and others from point-of-operation, rotating-part, chip, and spark hazards.
- [29 C.F.R. § 1910.147](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Servicing hazards from unexpected energization, startup, or stored energy require an energy-control program.
- [NIOSH hierarchy of controls](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/hierarchy-of-controls/about/index.html) — **Evidence class:** federal safety guidance. Elimination, substitution, and engineering controls are generally more reliable than administrative controls and PPE.

**Conflicting evidence:** Risk matrices may label some residual risk “acceptable,” but matrix boundaries are organization-specific and cannot waive prescriptive rules or compensate for poor assumptions. ### SAF-006 — What prevents demonstration G-code or simulation output from being mistaken for production-ready instructions?

Production release must include independent review, simulation appropriate to the actual machine, setup verification, guarded prove-out using the manufacturer's safe modes, first-article inspection, and authorized sign-off; simulation alone is never approval.

**Evidence:**
- [Haas Mill Operator's Manual](https://www.haascnc.com/content/dam/haascnc/en/service/manual/operator/english_mill_interactive_manual_print_version_2023.pdf) — **Evidence class:** manufacturer primary source. - [ISO 12100:2010](https://www.iso.org/standard/51528.html) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. Foreseeable misuse and risk reduction must be addressed in the real machinery context, not inferred solely from a software model.
- [NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final) — **Evidence class:** federal technical guidance. Operational-technology controls should account for safety, integrity, change control, access, and separation from ordinary information systems.

**Conflicting evidence:** High-fidelity simulation and digital twins catch many collisions and logic errors, while postprocessor, controller state, offsets, tooling, fixture, material, machine dynamics, and operator actions can still differ from the model. ### SAF-007 — How are emergency stops, machine interlocks, and operator procedures kept outside unsafe software control?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Safety functions must be implemented in a validated safety-related control system with the required performance, diagnostic coverage, fault response, and safe state; an ordinary application, AI model, cloud service, network, or standard PLC task must not be the sole safety layer. Procedures supplement but do not replace guarding, interlocks, energy isolation, or lockout/tagout.

**Evidence:**

- [IEC 60204-1:2016](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/26037) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. Machine electrical equipment requirements include control circuits, emergency stop, drive safety functions, protective bonding, and documentation.
- [29 C.F.R. § 1910.147](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Control-circuit stop commands are generally not energy isolation for servicing; hazardous energy must be controlled through the required program.

**Conflicting evidence:** Safety PLCs and software can legitimately implement safety functions when developed, configured, and validated to the applicable standard. **Confidence:** High on independence from ordinary software and validation; medium on architecture until hazards and required performance levels are calculated.

### SAF-009 — How are legacy-machine automation retrofits designed and validated safely?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Treat every retrofit as a new change to the machine system, not an add-on software project. Establish the original design and safety baseline, intended use and foreseeable misuse; perform a documented task/lifecycle risk assessment; identify all changed hazards and safety functions; design guarding, interlocks, emergency stops, modes, energy isolation, control reliability, fault response, cybersecurity, and power-recovery behavior; then verify and independently validate before production. Use management of change, updated drawings/manuals/training/lockout procedures, controlled commissioning, and post-start monitoring. A qualified machine-safety engineer and licensed controls/electrical engineer must lead technical approval, with a safety professional and industrial hygienist where exposures apply.

**Evidence:**
- [ISO 12100:2010](https://www.iso.org/standard/51528.html) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. The retrofit requires renewed lifecycle hazard identification and iterative risk reduction for the altered system.
- [ISO 13849-1:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/73481.html) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. Modified safety-related control functions require specified performance and validated integration across electrical, hydraulic, pneumatic, mechanical, and software elements.
- [29 C.F.R. § 1910.212](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.212) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Existing age does not remove guarding duties for points of operation, rotating parts, chips, sparks, and machine anchoring.
- [29 C.F.R. § 1910.147](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147) — **Evidence class:** federal regulation. Retrofit design and maintenance must control unexpected startup and stored electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, pneumatic, chemical, thermal, or other energy.
- [IEC 60204-1:2016](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/26037) — **Evidence class:** standards-body source. Altered machine electrical equipment implicates protective bonding, overcurrent protection, control circuits, emergency stop, drive functions, and technical documentation.

**Conflicting evidence:** A retrofit can improve safety beyond the original machine, but adding remote start, powered doors, robots, new drives, or network control can invalidate assumptions and create new failure modes. “Grandfathered,” original-OEM, or component-certified status does not by itself validate the modified machine as a system.

**Confidence:** High on the risk-assessment and validation process; medium on feasibility for any particular legacy machine without drawings, condition data, and applicable standards.

**Business-specific validation:** Require a qualified machine-safety engineer, licensed controls/electrical engineer, safety professional, and industrial hygienist as applicable to inspect the machine, determine whether retrofit or replacement is defensible, calculate/validate safety functions, witness fault and power-recovery tests, and sign the release.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Treating a retrofit as a system change requiring new risk assessment and validation is the correct conclusion. OEM legacy status or component certification is not enough after controls or automation are changed.

Require qualified risk assessment for guarding, interlocks, emergency stops,
unexpected startup, stored energy, robot or cobot interaction, pinch and crush

No retrofit may bypass or weaken required safety functions merely to reduce
