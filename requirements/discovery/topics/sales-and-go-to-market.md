# Sales and Go-to-Market Questions

## Purpose

Questions about finding, qualifying, winning, onboarding, retaining, and
expanding customers. This topic turns a selected market and customer story into
a measurable commercial system rather than assuming demand will arrive after
capability is built.

These are discovery questions, not approved requirements. Question IDs are
permanent and canonical in this file.

## Positioning and Initial Offer

### GTM-001 — What narrow offer, proof, and call to action should be presented to the first target customer?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Offer nearby engineers and maintenance teams a paid,
fixed-scope “requirements-to-delivered-part” pilot for one simple,
non-safety-critical fixture, bracket, or replacement part. Prove it with a
revisioned drawing/DFM review, itemized quote, documented inspection, and
realistic delivery commitment; ask for a 20-minute job review and the actual
problem file or sample, not a generic sales meeting.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. It requires a specific problem,
  customer, value proposition, channel, sales process, and revenue stream; it
  does not validate this offer.
- [Protolabs CNC machining](https://www.protolabs.com/services/cnc-machining/) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial offering. Interactive quoting, DFM
  feedback, and high-mix low-quantity production are commercially delivered;
  a scaled digital supplier is not proof a local startup can deliver.

**Conflicting evidence:** A free sample can reduce buyer friction, but it may
attract low-intent prospects and conceal willingness to pay. A small paid pilot
is stronger evidence even if conversion is lower.

**Confidence:** Medium — the offer is bounded and credible but untested.

**Business-specific validation:** Present the same offer to 20 named prospects
and record qualified conversations, files received, paid pilots, objections,
delivery results, and repeat orders.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The commercial
logic is plausible, but the support is still proxy evidence plus vendor
exemplars rather than measured channel conversion or local buyer behavior. I
kept the answer and validation steps.

### GTM-002 — Which customer pains are urgent and expensive enough to produce a near-term buying decision?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Prioritize downtime or delayed commissioning, unavailable
legacy parts, fixture/tooling bottlenecks, supplier rejection or no-bid, late
engineering changes, and short-run needs that block revenue or labor. Urgency
must be verified from an actual recent event, decision authority, budget, and
cost of delay—not inferred from a broad industry label.

**Evidence:**
- [Xometry CNC machining](https://www.xometry.com/capabilities/cnc-machining-service/) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial offering. It identifies legacy
  replacement, specialized upgrades, one-offs, and short turnaround as
  commercial uses; vendor claims do not quantify buyer urgency.
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. It requires specificity about
  the problem and customers served plus market and competitive research; it
  offers no manufacturing pain ranking.

**Conflicting evidence:** Emergency work can close quickly and pay premiums,
but it also brings incomplete requirements, disruption, and higher failure
risk; planned repetitive shortages may be commercially better.

**Confidence:** Medium — candidate pains are observed, their local frequency is unknown.

**Business-specific validation:** Interview buyers about their last three
events and collect cost-of-delay, current alternative, approval path, and
whether they paid for a faster solution.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The commercial
logic is plausible, but the support is still proxy evidence plus vendor
exemplars rather than measured channel conversion or local buyer behavior. I
kept the answer and validation steps.

### GTM-003 — Which claims can the business credibly make before it has extensive production history, references, or certifications?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Claim only verifiable process facts: response time,
supported scope, named qualifications of people/partners, revision-controlled
approvals, inspection method, transparent status, and delivery dates confirmed
per job. Do not claim superior quality, guaranteed speed, certification,
autonomous AI, capacity, or savings without representative operating evidence.

**Evidence:**
- [SendCutSend CNC machining](https://sendcutsend.com/services/cnc-machining/) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial offering. It makes a narrow,
  measurable tolerance claim and explicitly excludes custom tolerancing,
  illustrating credible scope-bounded claims.
- [NIST process-model validation guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmd/section4/pmd44.htm) —
  **Evidence class:** official technical guidance. It warns that a strong
  summary statistic does not establish valid performance without residual and
  model validation; application to marketing claims is analogous.
- [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) —
  **Evidence class:** consensus-based official framework; **AI maturity:
  governance, not delivery proof.** It supports measured trustworthy use
  rather than broad AI claims.

**Conflicting evidence:** Aspirational messaging can attract early adopters,
but overclaiming before references increases legal, reputational, and
expectation risk.

**Confidence:** High — evidence-bounded claims are the defensible starting point.

**Business-specific validation:** Counsel and insurer should review claim
language; every quantitative claim should link to a dated test or job sample.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The answer stays inside what can
be claimed or contractually accepted, and the official/legal sources are
stronger than any contrary go-fast sales theory. I kept it.

## Channels and Pipeline

### GTM-004 — Which acquisition channels can reach the first customers: direct outreach, referrals, local networks, distributors, digital content, marketplaces, partnerships, or existing job shops?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Start with founder-led direct outreach to a named local
account list, warm referrals from engineers/suppliers/advisors, and partnerships
with existing shops that need engineering or overflow help. Use marketplaces
and content as controlled experiments, not the primary plan, until lead quality,
fees, price pressure, and repeat ownership are known.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. It says channels and customer
  relationships should be explicit and optimized over time; it provides no
  manufacturing-channel benchmarks.
- [Protolabs CNC machining](https://www.protolabs.com/services/cnc-machining/) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial offering. Direct digital quoting and
  a manufacturing network are both used commercially, confirming multiple
  channel/delivery models; economics are undisclosed.
- [FAR Part 9](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/part-9) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official procurement framework. Some institutional
  channels require documented supplier responsibility and eligibility; federal
  procurement is not representative of the first commercial channel.

**Conflicting evidence:** Marketplaces can provide immediate RFQs but may
produce commodity competition and weak customer ownership; referrals yield
trust but can be too sparse or socially selected.

**Confidence:** Medium — channel order is plausible; no measured CAC exists.

**Business-specific validation:** Run each channel with a fixed time/cash budget
and compare qualified lead rate, quote effort, conversion, contribution,
payment, repeat, and customer ownership.

logic is plausible, but the support is still proxy evidence plus vendor

### GTM-005 — What qualification criteria distinguish a useful lead from work that is unsafe, unprofitable, uncollectable, outside capability, or strategically distracting?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Qualify intended use and consequence, lawful file
ownership/export status, requirements maturity, process/material/tolerance fit,
quantity and schedule feasibility, inspection/documentation, buyer authority,
budget, payment/credit, liability/terms, supplier availability, expected
contribution, repeat/learning value, and opportunity cost. Any red-line safety,
legal, insurance, or capability failure is a no-quote or escalation.

**Evidence:**
- [FAR Part 9](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/part-9) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official procurement framework. It uses explicit
  contractor responsibility and exclusion processes; commercial qualification
  can be lighter but should still be evidence-based.
- [SendCutSend terms](https://store.sendcutsend.com/pages/terms-of-service) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial terms. They require lawful authorized
  files, supported designs/materials, account security, and defined design
  responsibility; one provider's policy is not complete due diligence.
- [BIS EAR Part 734](https://www.bis.gov/regulations/ear/734) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Export treatment can depend on
  item, technology, destination, end use, and end user; specialist review may be required.

**Conflicting evidence:** Heavy qualification can reject valuable early work
and frustrate buyers, while weak qualification merely moves discovery cost and
risk downstream.

**Confidence:** High on criteria; medium on thresholds and automation.

**Business-specific validation:** Apply a scored checklist to 30 leads, review
false accepts/rejects monthly, and have counsel, insurer, and quality/safety
professionals approve red lines.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The answer stays inside what can
be claimed or contractually accepted, and the official/legal sources are
stronger than any contrary go-fast sales theory. I kept it.

### GTM-006 — What stages, owners, evidence, and exit criteria define the path from lead through quote, first order, qualification, and repeat business?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use: target account; contacted; discovery complete;
qualified/no-quote; requirements ready; quote issued; commercial/technical
review; won/lost; order accepted; approvals complete; production released;
delivered/accepted; issue closed; repeat candidate; repeat order. Every stage
needs one accountable owner, dated evidence, next action, and objective exit criteria.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. It requires the complete
  marketing/sales process, customer relationship, channels, and projections to
  connect; it does not prescribe CRM stages.
- [NIST Baldrige improvement tools](https://www.nist.gov/baldrige/self-assessing/improvement-tools) —
  **Evidence class:** consensus-based official framework. It emphasizes process
  ownership, customers, operations, information, and results; it is not a sales methodology.

**Conflicting evidence:** Detailed stages improve learning but invite stale
administration; too few stages hide where technical, commercial, or approval
friction occurs.

**Confidence:** Medium — stage logic is useful, workload is untested.

**Business-specific validation:** Process the first 20 opportunities, measure
stage aging and data completeness, and merge any stage that does not change action.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The commercial
logic is plausible, but the support is still proxy evidence plus vendor
exemplars rather than measured channel conversion or local buyer behavior. I
kept the answer and validation steps.

### GTM-007 — Which reasons for no-quote, lost quote, delayed decision, and lost customer must be recorded?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Record a primary and optional secondary reason with
evidence: prohibited/risk, capability, capacity/lead time, requirements
incomplete, quantity, price, total value, quality/certification, geography/
shipping, terms/liability, credit/payment, incumbent/relationship, buyer delay,
project canceled, competitor, no response, delivery/quality failure, service,
or strategic fit. Separate verified buyer feedback from salesperson inference.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Competitive research and a
  complete sales strategy should explain why alternatives win; it does not
  define loss codes.
- [NIST Baldrige improvement tools](https://www.nist.gov/baldrige/self-assessing/improvement-tools) —
  **Evidence class:** consensus-based official framework. It encourages
  identifying information gaps and performance opportunities; it does not
  validate self-reported buyer reasons.

**Conflicting evidence:** Mandatory codes enable trends but can create false
certainty when buyers withhold reasons; “unknown” should remain valid and visible.

**Confidence:** High on categories; medium on reliable attribution.

**Business-specific validation:** Review codes after 30 decisions, compare them
with buyer interviews and quote data, and track unknown/assumed rates.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The commercial
logic is plausible, but the support is still proxy evidence plus vendor
exemplars rather than measured channel conversion or local buyer behavior. I
kept the answer and validation steps.

## Commercial Economics and Learning

### GTM-008 — What customer-acquisition cost, sales effort, quote effort, conversion rate, sales cycle, order value, repeat rate, and lifetime value are acceptable for each target segment?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Public benchmarks cannot set acceptable values. Derive
segment limits from contribution after expected quality/support cost, cash
timing, capacity, retention, concentration, and risk. Count founder and
engineering time in acquisition/quote cost, use cohort repeat behavior rather
than optimistic lifetime assumptions, and set a payback period compatible with cash reserves.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Marketing/sales assumptions
  must connect to financial projections and cost structure; no acceptable CAC is given.
- [Census Economic Census tables](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/economic-census/data/tables.html) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official data. Industry receipts and expenses
  give broad context but not segment-level acquisition or lifetime value.
- [IRS owner-compensation guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/paying-yourself) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Owner effort should not be
  treated as free; tax treatment is not CAC accounting.

**Conflicting evidence:** High initial CAC can be rational for long-lived repeat
programs, but early cohorts and friendly customers often exaggerate retention
and understate founder effort.

**Confidence:** High that thresholds are business-specific; low on values.

**Business-specific validation:** Track fully loaded opportunity-level hours and
cash by segment for at least two cohorts, then set payback and concentration limits.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The discipline
is right, but acceptable CAC, experiment, and principal/agent thresholds are
intrinsically segment- and cash-specific. I kept the answer and its emphasis
on measured actuals plus counsel/CPA review where needed.

### GTM-009 — How will paid pilots, sample parts, first-article work, guarantees, deposits, and references reduce first-order risk without creating an unsustainable sales subsidy?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Use paid, tightly bounded pilots or first articles with
credited-but-visible engineering/NRE, a deposit covering committed cash,
specific acceptance criteria, and a limited remedy tied to the pilot. Offer
free samples only from owned standard specimens, cap total subsidy, obtain
permission for references, and require normal-price transition rules before work starts.

**Evidence:**
- [SendCutSend terms](https://store.sendcutsend.com/pages/terms-of-service) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial terms. Custom work is paid through
  ordering, production limits cancellation, and return reasons/remedies are
  bounded, illustrating risk allocation; terms are supplier-favorable and not
  necessarily suitable here.
- [UCC 2-209](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-209) —
  **Evidence class:** primary legal text/model law. Changes and rescission should
  be controlled, supporting explicit pilot-to-production transitions; counsel
  must apply the governing contract and state law.
- [NIST hypothesis-testing guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section1/prc13.htm) —
  **Evidence class:** official technical guidance. A pilot needs predefined
  pass/fail criteria rather than a post-hoc success story.

**Conflicting evidence:** Free work can speed trust and generate references,
but it selects for price-sensitive leads and hides both willingness to pay and
delivery economics.

**Confidence:** High on bounded paid pilots; medium on credit/guarantee design.

**Business-specific validation:** Approve a per-pilot subsidy ceiling and track
conversion, normal-price repeat, remedy cost, reference permission, and cash exposure.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The commercial
logic is plausible, but the support is still proxy evidence plus vendor
exemplars rather than measured channel conversion or local buyer behavior. I
kept the answer and validation steps.

### GTM-010 — What evidence and thresholds determine whether to scale, revise, or stop a channel, segment, offer, or customer-acquisition experiment?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Predefine qualified leads, paid conversion, fully loaded
CAC/quote effort, sales-cycle and cash timing, contribution, acceptance/on-time
delivery, repeat/referral, concentration, and strategic fit. Scale only after
repeatable positive cohorts; revise when demand exists but one controllable
metric fails; stop when losses breach budget, buyers do not pay/repeat, or the
channel systematically creates unsafe, unsuitable, or unowned relationships.

**Evidence:**
- [NIST hypothesis-testing guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section1/prc13.htm) —
  **Evidence class:** official technical guidance. Decision thresholds and
  alternative hypotheses should be set before outcomes are observed.
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Lean plans are designed for
  refinement, while channels, customers, costs, and revenues remain linked.
- [NIST process-model validation guidance](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmd/section4/pmd44.htm) —
  **Evidence class:** official technical guidance. A single strong metric is
  insufficient without broader validation.

**Conflicting evidence:** Small samples make early thresholds noisy, but
continuing every weak experiment “for learning” creates selection and sunk-cost bias.

**Confidence:** High on dimensions; medium on minimum cohort and thresholds.

**Business-specific validation:** Set a fixed budget/time/cohort before launch,
show every observation, and require an independent owner to approve continuation.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The discipline
is right, but acceptable CAC, experiment, and principal/agent thresholds are
intrinsically segment- and cash-specific. I kept the answer and its emphasis
on measured actuals plus counsel/CPA review where needed.

### GTM-011 — Which master terms, purchase-order clauses, blanket orders, release schedules, price locks, warranties, indemnities, and liability terms are acceptable or require review?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Require legal review for conflicting PO terms, broad
indemnity, uncapped or consequential liability, safety/regulatory flow-downs,
IP ownership, audit/cyber/export duties, long price locks, forecasts presented
as commitments, unilateral changes/cancellation, chargebacks, unusual warranty,
most-favored pricing, consignment, tooling ownership, and pay-when-paid terms.
Use an approved clause matrix and no-start rule for unresolved exceptions.

**Evidence:**
- [UCC 2-207](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-207) —
  **Evidence class:** primary legal text/model law. Additional/different terms
  between merchants can become part of a contract or be supplied through
  conduct, making form conflict material; state law varies.
- [UCC 2-209](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/2/2-209) —
  **Evidence class:** primary legal text/model law. Modification, waiver, and
  signed-writing requirements affect releases and changes; counsel is required.
- [SendCutSend terms](https://store.sendcutsend.com/pages/terms-of-service) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial terms. Design responsibility,
  cancellation, shipping risk, remedies, warranty disclaimers, and liability
  limits are commercially material; these are one seller's terms.

**Conflicting evidence:** Refusing all buyer paper blocks established B2B
accounts, while accepting it unread can create liabilities far larger than the order.

**Confidence:** High that these clauses need controlled review; no conclusion on acceptability.

**Business-specific validation:** Manufacturing counsel and insurer should
create fallback language, dollar/authority thresholds, and a signed deviations log.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The answer stays inside what can
be claimed or contractually accepted, and the official/legal sources are
stronger than any contrary go-fast sales theory. I kept it.

### GTM-012 — In partner-manufacturing or broker models, is the business acting as principal or agent, and can markup cover engineering, quality, late delivery, defects, customer support, and payment risk?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Default to a disclosed principal model only if the
business intentionally contracts to deliver the finished result, controls
supplier/customer terms, carries suitable insurance, and prices all delivery
risk. Use an agent model only with explicit authority, fee, disclosure, and
allocation showing the customer contracts with the manufacturer. A pass-through
markup is adequate only after actual engineering, quality, delay, support,
warranty, credit, tax, and working-capital costs are included.

**Evidence:**
- [Protolabs CNC machining](https://www.protolabs.com/services/cnc-machining/) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial offering. Owned/automated sites and a
  manufacturing network coexist under a customer-facing service, proving a
  principal network model is operationally possible; economics are undisclosed.
- [SendCutSend terms](https://store.sendcutsend.com/pages/terms-of-service) —
  **Evidence class:** observed commercial terms. The seller owns the order
  relationship while allocating design, shipping, cancellation, remedy, and
  liability risk; its allocation cannot simply be copied downstream.
- [SBA business-plan guidance](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) —
  **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Partners, activities, resources,
  costs, and revenue must be modeled together; it is not agency law.

**Conflicting evidence:** Principal status supports one accountable customer
experience and higher revenue, but can create warranty, tax, product-liability,
and cash exposure that a small markup cannot absorb.

**Confidence:** Medium — the commercial distinction is clear; legal status and
economics are transaction-specific.

**Business-specific validation:** Counsel, CPA, and insurer should approve the
model; then reconcile markup against full cost and losses over at least 10 partner jobs.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The discipline
is right, but acceptable CAC, experiment, and principal/agent thresholds are
intrinsically segment- and cash-specific. I kept the answer and its emphasis
on measured actuals plus counsel/CPA review where needed.
