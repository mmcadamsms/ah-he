# Customer Demand and Market Geography Questions

## Purpose

This topic tracks where customers that purchase subtractively manufactured and
additively manufactured parts are concentrated in the United States.

The goal is not merely to count manufacturing companies. The business needs to
identify buyers with recurring needs for design support, prototypes,
replacement parts, tooling, fixtures, low-volume production, bridge
production, and assemblies that match the planned capabilities.

## Question Supplied in Conversation

### MKT-001 — Where are the most customers for subtractively and additively manufactured parts?

**State:** Open

**Original wording:** "where are the most customers for parts made via subtractive / additive type maufacturing"

The eventual answer should:

- identify customer concentrations by metropolitan area, industrial corridor,
  state, and region;
- distinguish total manufacturing activity from addressable demand for custom
  parts;
- separate subtractive, additive, and hybrid demand;
- identify industries, company sizes, applications, and buyer roles;
- compare prototype, maintenance, tooling, low-volume, bridge-production, and
  recurring-production demand;
- distinguish demand served locally from work that can be sourced nationally;
- measure both market size and competitive supplier density; and
- identify underserved demand rather than simply the largest industrial
  regions.

- turning and mill-turn;

### MKT-007 — Which industries have recurring low-volume machining demand?

**State:** Open

Look for high-mix environments where custom brackets, housings, shafts,
manifolds, tooling, fixtures, replacement parts, test hardware, and machine
components are repeatedly required.

### MKT-008 — Where is urgent replacement-part demand strongest?

**State:** Open

Potential demand may be associated with factories, utilities, mines, farms,
processing plants, transportation, construction equipment, and legacy
machinery where downtime is expensive and drawings may be missing.

### MKT-009 — Which buyers require advanced five-axis capability?

**State:** Open

Identify applications where complex geometry, compound angles, fewer physical
setups, tight positional relationships, impellers, medical geometry, aerospace
features, or difficult workholding justify five-axis machining.

### MKT-016 — Which markets are underserved?

**State:** Open

- long quote times;
- excessive minimum orders;
- weak design assistance;
- poor status visibility;
- long lead times;
- lack of five-axis capability;
- limited material or finishing options;
- weak prototype-to-production support;
- difficulty obtaining replacement parts; or
- incumbent suppliers focused only on large accounts.

## Market Size and Economics

### MKT-026 — How should addressable market size be measured?

**State:** Open

Avoid treating the total manufacturing economy as the addressable market.
Estimate:

```text
relevant customers
x annual projects per customer
x suitable outsourced share
x realistic average order value
x attainable geographic and capability coverage
= serviceable addressable demand
```

### MKT-027 — Which customer segments have attractive unit economics?

**State:** Open

Include sales effort, quoting cost, engineering effort, order size, repeat
rate, material, machine time, inspection, documentation, payment terms,
returns, liability, and customer concentration.

### MKT-028 — Which segments value transparency enough to pay for it?

**State:** Open

Test whether customers will pay or preferentially select a supplier for
requirements traceability, design approvals, real-time project status,
simulation, inspection evidence, and a complete digital record.

### MKT-029 — Which segments should be avoided initially?

**State:** Open

Identify segments requiring certifications, liability, capital, sales cycles,
security, quantities, financing, or regulatory expertise beyond the initial
business.
