# Tax, Finance, and Capital Questions

## Purpose

This topic tracks how federal, state, and local tax rules affect a machining,
manufacturing, engineering, and physical-product business.

The questions are intended to identify decisions, records, system behavior,
professional advice, and location tradeoffs that must be understood before
relying on a business model or financial forecast.

This backlog is not tax or legal advice. Final decisions must be reviewed by
qualified tax and legal professionals for the jurisdictions where the business
operates, employs people, owns equipment, stores inventory, sells products, or
has customers.

## Current Priority

The current emphasis is:

> "thinking a lot about state and local taxes ... also opportunity zones for small buisness"

Research should therefore begin with location-specific scenarios for a small,
equipment-intensive design and manufacturing business rather than a generic
national tax summary.

## Question Supplied in Conversation

### TAX-001 — How do taxes affect a machining and manufacturing business?

**State:** Open

**Original wording:** "need to do analysis around how tax impacts a machineing / maufacturing type buisness"

**The eventual analysis should:**

- distinguish federal, state, county, city, and special-district taxes;
- separate the tax treatment of engineering/design services, software,
  prototypes, tooling, manufactured parts, assemblies, freight, installation,
  repair, and warranties;
- compare candidate legal entities and tax elections;
- account for where the business, employees, equipment, inventory, suppliers,
  and customers are located;
- identify both tax costs and manufacturing-specific exemptions or incentives;
- model cash timing, not only annual tax expense; and
- identify questions requiring a CPA, tax attorney, payroll provider, customs
  specialist, or state/local incentive specialist.

### TAX-007 — Which manufacturing purchases qualify for sales/use-tax exemptions?

## State Tax and Opportunity Zones

### TAX-036 — How do the taxes of different states affect the business?

**State:** Open

**Original wording:** "how do taxes of different states and/or opportunity zones impact buisness taxes"

The comparison must evaluate total burden and cash timing rather than ranking
states by one headline tax rate. Include:

- corporate or pass-through income tax;
- franchise, margin, commercial-activity, and gross-receipts taxes;
- apportionment and sourcing rules for products and services;
- throwback or throwout rules;
- sales and use tax rates and manufacturing exemptions;
- taxation of engineering, design, software, tooling, repairs, installation,
  freight, and tangible products;
- machinery and tangible-personal-property tax;
- inventory tax;
- real-property tax;
- utility, fuel, and local-option taxes;
- unemployment, workers' compensation, paid-leave, disability, and payroll
  costs;
- state conformity to federal depreciation and research rules;
- state research, investment, jobs, training, and manufacturing credits;
- filing, registration, audit, and certificate-administration costs; and
- interactions with city, county, school-district, and special-district taxes.

### TAX-037 — How should candidate states be compared for this specific business model?

**State:** Open

Build scenarios rather than one generic ranking:

Each scenario should model revenue mix, payroll, profit, equipment, inventory,
property, utilities, customer locations, and capital investment.

### TAX-039 — How do state manufacturing exemptions change equipment and operating costs?

**State:** Open

Compare which states exempt or reduce tax on:

- quality-control and testing equipment;
- material handling;
- research and development;

### TAX-040 — What is an Opportunity Zone likely to change for this business?

**State:** Open

Determine separately:

- benefits available to investors contributing eligible capital gains through
  a Qualified Opportunity Fund;
- benefits available to the operating business itself;
- whether the business and its property satisfy the applicable location,
  asset, income, use, and timing tests;
- how leased property, purchased equipment, used equipment, land, buildings,
  improvements, and working capital are treated;
- whether the business would need a separate property or investment structure;
- federal benefits versus state conformity or state-specific incentives;
- reporting, valuation, audit, and ongoing compliance costs;
- deadlines, expiration dates, and legislative uncertainty; and
- what happens if the business relocates, sells assets, fails a test, or exits
  before the intended holding period.

Do not assume an Opportunity Zone designation is a general exemption from
income, sales, payroll, or property tax. The eventual answer must state exactly
who receives each benefit, which gain or income is affected, and what
compliance conditions apply.

### TAX-041 — How do Opportunity Zone benefits compare with ordinary state and local incentives?

**State:** Open

Compare Opportunity Zone treatment against:

- property-tax abatements;
- tax-increment financing;
- investment and job-creation credits;
- sales-tax exemptions;
- training grants;
- utility incentives;
- low-interest financing;
- enterprise, redevelopment, or rural-zone programs;
- federal or state grants; and
- negotiated local infrastructure support.

Include application timing, approval discretion, job and wage commitments,
capital-spending requirements, reporting, prevailing-wage or community-benefit
conditions, transferability, refundability, and clawbacks.

### TAX-042 — Could an incentive distort the location decision?

**State:** Open

Determine whether a tax benefit compensates for or merely hides disadvantages
in workforce, suppliers, freight, utilities, insurance, permitting, customer
access, property, resilience, or quality of life.

Compare the net present value of incentives with:

- one-time relocation and construction costs;
- recurring operating costs;
- compliance and adviser fees;
- restrictions on future changes;
- clawback risk; and
- the value of alternative locations after the incentive expires.

### TAX-043 — What evidence is required before including incentives in a financial forecast?

**State:** Open

Forecasts should distinguish:

- incentives already approved in writing;
- incentives for which the business appears eligible but has not applied;
- discretionary or negotiated incentives;
- benefits dependent on future jobs, wages, investment, or dates;
- refundable versus nonrefundable credits;
- transferable versus nontransferable credits;
- benefits usable only when the business has sufficient tax liability; and
- marketing claims that have not been verified against law or program rules.

### TAX-051 — Can a small machining or manufacturing operating business use an Opportunity Zone structure?

**State:** Open

The answer must distinguish among:

- the small operating company;
- the owners of the operating company;
- outside investors with eligible capital gains;
- a Qualified Opportunity Fund;
- a Qualified Opportunity Zone Business; and
- any separate entity owning or leasing the facility and equipment.

Determine:

- whether eligible capital gain is available or must be raised from investors;
- whether the operating business can satisfy the location and gross-income
  tests;
- how much tangible property must qualify and remain in the zone;
- how leased machinery and real estate are treated;
- how newly purchased and used machinery are treated;
- whether original-use or substantial-improvement rules apply;
- how working capital for construction, equipment, hiring, and startup is
  treated;
- whether inventory, mobile equipment, customer-site work, and remote
  employees affect qualification;
- whether any planned activity is an excluded business;
- which federal reporting and annual asset tests apply;
- which state recognizes, modifies, or rejects the federal treatment; and
- whether legal, accounting, valuation, and compliance costs outweigh the
  expected benefit for a small business.

### TAX-052 — What state and local taxes apply at each candidate address?

**State:** Open

Location analysis should be address-specific where possible. For each
candidate site, identify:

- state income, franchise, margin, or gross-receipts taxes;
- county, city, school-district, transit, occupational, and special-district
  taxes;
- combined sales/use-tax rate;
- taxability of utilities and manufacturing inputs;
- real-property and tangible-personal-property tax;
- inventory tax;
- machinery valuation and depreciation schedules for property tax;
- local wage or employer taxes;
- permitting and business-license taxes or fees;
- available abatements, exemptions, credits, grants, and financing; and
- filing obligations created by the specific city and county boundaries.

Do not rely only on statewide averages because neighboring addresses can have
different local jurisdictions, tax rates, utility providers, and incentive
eligibility.

### TAX-053 — Which local incentives can be combined with Opportunity Zone treatment?

**State:** Open

Investigate whether a candidate project can combine Opportunity Zone treatment
with:

- enterprise, redevelopment, rural, or industrial zones;
- property-tax abatements;
- tax-increment financing;
- sales-tax sharing or exemption;
- workforce and training grants;
- infrastructure assistance;
- utility extensions or discounts;
- job-creation or payroll credits;
- equipment financing;
- brownfield assistance;
- federal or state grants; and
- local land or building programs.

Record anti-stacking rules, application deadlines, required approvals,
performance agreements, reporting, prevailing-wage conditions, community
benefits, job and investment commitments, and clawbacks.

### TAX-054 — At what business size does an Opportunity Zone structure become economical?

**State:** Open

Model the expected tax benefit against:

- legal entity formation;
- fund and operating agreements;
- accounting and tax preparation;
- valuation;
- annual testing and reporting;
- investor relations;
- audits and document retention;
- restrictions on moving or selling property;
- financing complexity; and
- risk that the business changes direction before the intended holding period.

Test multiple levels of eligible capital gain, investment, equipment, real
estate, payroll, revenue, and holding period rather than assuming the structure
works for every small business.

### TAX-055 — Should location research begin with zones or with operating fundamentals?

**State:** Open

Compare these approaches:

1. Find the strongest customer, workforce, supplier, logistics, utility, and
   facility location, then identify available zones and incentives.
2. Start with Opportunity Zones and determine which of them also satisfy the
   operating requirements.

The analysis should identify the risk that a tax-driven location produces
higher recurring cost or weaker access to customers, skills, suppliers, and
infrastructure after incentives expire.

## Employees, Payroll, and Benefits

### TAX-044 — How do taxes affect employees of the business?

**State:** Open

**Original wording:** "how do the taxes impact empoyees of the buisness?"

The eventual answer should explain effects on employees separately from costs
or benefits to the employer. Include:

- federal income-tax withholding;
- Social Security and Medicare withholding;
- state income-tax withholding;
- city, county, school-district, occupational, or other local income taxes;
- states without an individual wage income tax;
- reciprocity agreements between neighboring states;
- unemployment, disability, paid-family-leave, and similar employee
  deductions;
- taxable and nontaxable benefits;
- retirement contributions and employer matches;
- bonuses, commissions, overtime, shift differentials, and profit sharing;
- health savings and flexible spending accounts;
- educational assistance and tool or safety-equipment reimbursements;
- accountable-plan reimbursements versus taxable allowances;
- company vehicles, mileage, meals, travel, lodging, and per diem;
- relocation assistance and temporary assignments;
- employee ownership, stock, options, or other equity compensation;
- severance, retention payments, and signing bonuses; and
- year-end reporting and correction of withholding errors.

### TAX-045 — How does work location change an employee's taxes?

**State:** Open

Analyze employees who:

- live and work in the same state;
- commute across a state line;
- work remotely from another state;
- temporarily install, repair, inspect, or commission products elsewhere;
- travel among customer and supplier sites;
- relocate during the year; or
- work in a city with a local wage or occupational tax.

The answer should distinguish residence-based taxation, work-location
taxation, credits for taxes paid to another state, reciprocity, convenience-of-
the-employer rules, withholding thresholds, and nonresident returns.

### TAX-046 — How do taxes and mandatory deductions affect recruiting and take-home pay?

**State:** Open

Compare employee take-home pay and employer cost by location using realistic
compensation packages. Include:

- wage levels;
- employee income and payroll taxes;
- employer payroll taxes;
- health-insurance contributions;
- retirement contributions;
- workers' compensation;
- unemployment;
- paid-leave programs;
- commuting and housing costs;
- local taxes; and
- the value of state or local benefits funded by those taxes.

Do not assume the lowest-tax state produces the best employee outcome or the
lowest total employment cost.

### TAX-047 — Which benefits can be structured tax-efficiently without reducing employee usefulness?

uniforms, safety equipment, meals, travel, relocation, phone and internet
reimbursement, and employee discounts.

The analysis should consider nondiscrimination rules, eligibility, payroll
administration, employee understanding, and whether the benefit is actually

### TAX-048 — How do owner-employees differ from ordinary employees?

### TAX-049 — Do Opportunity Zone or other business incentives directly benefit employees?

**State:** Open

Identify whether a program:

- changes employee income or withholding;
- funds training, transportation, childcare, housing, or other workforce
  support;
- requires minimum wages, benefits, local hiring, or job retention;
- creates clawback risk that affects employment stability; or
- benefits only investors, property owners, or the operating company.

Do not describe an investor or employer tax benefit as an employee benefit
unless the program or compensation policy creates a direct employee outcome.
