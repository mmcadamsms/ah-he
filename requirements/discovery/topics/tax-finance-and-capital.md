# Tax, Finance, and Capital Questions

## Purpose

This topic tracks how federal, state, and local tax rules affect a machining,
manufacturing, engineering, and physical-product business.

The questions are intended to identify decisions, records, system behavior,
professional advice, and location tradeoffs that must be understood before
relying on a business model or financial forecast.

This backlog is not tax or legal advice. Final decisions must be reviewed by
qualified tax and legal professionals for the jurisdictions where the business
operates, employs people, owns equipment, stores inventory, sells products, or
has customers.

## Current Priority

The current emphasis is:

> "thinking a lot about state and local taxes ... also opportunity zones for small buisness"

Research should therefore begin with location-specific scenarios for a small,
equipment-intensive design and manufacturing business rather than a generic
national tax summary.

## Entity and Ownership Structure

### TAX-002 — Which entity and tax structure best fits the initial business?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** No entity is universally best. Usually compare an LLC's
legal flexibility with default/pass-through, S-corporation, and C-corporation
tax treatment using expected profit, owner services, retained capital, benefits,
state taxes, investors, and exit. Liability, governance, financing, and tax
election are separate decisions.

**Evidence:**
- [IRS Business Structures](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/business-structures) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Entity form determines federal return type while LLC status originates under state law.
- [SBA choose a business structure](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/#business-structure) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Structure changes taxes, liability, fundraising, paperwork, and continuity.

**Conflicting evidence:** S corporations can reduce some employment tax but add
reasonable-compensation and shareholder restrictions; C corporations facilitate
some investment but can create entity/shareholder tax and exit tradeoffs.

**Confidence:** Medium; facts and state law dominate.

**Business-specific validation:** CPA and business/tax counsel must model owner
cash, retained earnings, payroll, benefits, state filings, financing, and exits.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Independent
review supports the "no universal best entity" conclusion, but the eventual
comparison must model LLC legal form separately from federal tax
classification and include state franchise/gross-receipts taxes, investor
eligibility, and retained-capital needs. Changes: treat financing and exit
scenarios as co-equal with first-year owner tax.

Compare sole proprietorship, LLC, partnership, S corporation, C corporation,
and any holding-company or operating-company structure relevant to equipment,
real estate, intellectual property, and outside investment.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Compensation determines deductible entity expense,
payroll/self-employment tax, withholding, benefits, retirement contribution

- [IRS Business Taxes](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/business-taxes) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Wage, self-employment, estimated, and entity income-tax systems differ.

**Conflicting evidence:** Minimizing current wages can preserve cash but may
violate reasonable-compensation rules and reduce benefit/retirement capacity.

**Confidence:** High on mechanisms; medium on optimal mix.

**Business-specific validation:** CPA must document market compensation, basis,
cash needs, benefits, retirement, and quarterly payment/withholding plan.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Independent review confirms that
compensation versus distributions is one of the main tax levers and that
reasonable-compensation, basis, and benefits rules can dominate the result.
Changes: none beyond requiring entity-specific payroll/self-employment
analysis before optimizing owner cash.

Consider wages, guaranteed payments, draws, dividends, self-employment tax,
reasonable-compensation rules, retirement contributions, benefits, and cash
retained for equipment or working capital.

**Conflicting evidence:** Pass-through treatment may suit owner funding, while
institutional investors often prefer corporate equity; an assumed exit may
never occur.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Business-specific validation:** Tax counsel must model at least owner-funded,

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Independent

but taxable, bundled, or part of tangible property in another.

**Confidence:** High that no national answer exists; low per item without states.

**Business-specific validation:** State/local tax counsel or a manufacturing CPA
must approve a SKU/service/sourcing matrix and ruling requests where uncertain.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Independent review confirms no

sourcing, and documentation control. Changes: none; keep the item-by-item

Analyze separately:

- engineering and design services;
- CAD and digital deliverables;
- prototypes and samples;
- manufactured parts and assemblies;
- tooling, molds, fixtures, and gauges;
- setup and nonrecurring engineering charges;
- finishing and outside processing;
- inspection and certification;
- repair, modification, and installation;

- [Federation of Tax Administrators state agencies](https://taxadmin.org/fta-members/) — **Evidence class:** primary authority directory. State revenue agencies publish controlling resale and manufacturing rules/forms.

**Conflicting evidence:** Economic incorporation into a customer part does not
always satisfy a state's statutory resale or direct-use definition.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Business-specific validation:** Validate each material/use with the supplier,
state authority, and SALT professional; accrue use tax when facts change.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Independent
review supports the resale-certificate framing, but many states draw narrower
lines for consumables, tooling, packaging, and prototype work than businesses
expect. Changes: treat incorporation into customer property as necessary but
not sufficient until the state's statute and form instructions say so.

Determine how resale certificates apply to incorporated materials, purchased
components, subcontracted processes, packaging, and products shipped to the
customer.

## Equipment, Depreciation, and Capital Investment

### TAX-010 — How should machines and major equipment be depreciated?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Capitalize the asset and all costs required to place it in
service, assign correct recovery period/convention, then compare regular
depreciation, Section 179, current bonus rules, and state conformity against
taxable income, cash needs, financing, and expected sale. Tax depreciation is
not the same as economic replacement cost.

**Evidence:**
- [IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. IRS explains MACRS, placed-in-service, basis, Section 179, special depreciation, listed-property, and disposition rules.
- [Public Law 119-21](https://www.congress.gov/119/plaws/publ21/PLAW-119publ21.pdf) — **Evidence class:** primary legislation. The 2025 law changed federal depreciation-related provisions, requiring current-year verification.

**Conflicting evidence:** Immediate deductions improve near-term tax cash but can
be unusable, diverge from state treatment, or increase later recapture.

**Confidence:** High on method; medium on current-year optimum.

**Business-specific validation:** CPA must verify class life, basis, placed-in-
service date, business use, elections, taxable income, and every state's conformity.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Independent
review confirms the method and the importance of current-law verification:
federal bonus and Section 179 rules changed in 2025-26 and many states do not
conform. Changes: keep current-year federal law and state conformity as
explicit inputs rather than background assumptions.

Compare normal depreciation, Section 179 expensing, available bonus
depreciation, placed-in-service timing, business-use limits, state conformity,
and the cash-flow impact of each option.

**State:** Exploring

**Conflicting evidence:** Book policy, lender reporting, and tax capitalization
can use different thresholds and lives; reconcile rather than force one ledger view.

**Confidence:** High on principle; medium on mixed projects.

**Business-specific validation:** CPA must review invoices/work orders and define
units of property, safe-harbor elections, book-tax differences, and state rules.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Independent review supports the
capitalization framework and the need to distinguish repairs from improvements
at the unit-of-property level. Changes: none beyond reconciling book, lender,
and tax capitalization policies instead of forcing one threshold.

Consider machine purchases, freight, rigging, installation, electrical work,
foundations, enclosures, safety systems, software, commissioning, major
repairs, rebuilds, fixtures, tooling, and internally created equipment.

### TAX-012 — What tax consequences occur when equipment is sold or replaced?

**State:** Exploring

identify depreciation recapture, capital/ordinary gain or loss, sales/use tax,
debt payoff, lease termination, and state treatment. A trade-in generally does
not eliminate the need to account for disposition; casualty and abandonment
require separate evidence.

**Evidence:**
- [IRS Publication 544](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p544) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. IRS explains basis, gain/loss, business-property dispositions, involuntary conversions, and recapture.
- [IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Depreciation and basis records continue through disposition.

**Conflicting evidence:** Cash proceeds can be low after debt while taxable gain
is high because basis was accelerated; cash and tax results must be modeled separately.

**Confidence:** High.

**Business-specific validation:** CPA must reconcile asset ledger, depreciation,
sale documents, debt/lease, state tax, and Form 4797 treatment before closing.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Independent review confirms sale
proceeds, adjusted basis, recapture, debt, and state treatment must all be
modeled separately. Changes: none, other than explicitly checking lease
termination and trade-in paperwork before assuming net cash matches tax gain.

Include gain or loss, depreciation recapture, trade-ins, financed equipment,
leases, casualty losses, and disposal of obsolete tooling.

### TAX-013 — Should equipment or real estate be owned by a separate entity?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Separation can isolate assets, support succession or
financing, and charge rent, but adds entities, returns, insurance, lender
consents, related-party pricing, sales/property tax, and potential liability or
bankruptcy complexity. Use it only after comparing after-tax cash and legal risk
with direct ownership.

**Evidence:**

- [SBA 504 loans](https://www.sba.gov/loans/504-loans/) — **Evidence class:** observed financing practice. Fixed-asset financing has eligible-use, ownership, and passive/speculative-business constraints.

**Conflicting evidence:** Formal separation does not guarantee liability
protection if contracts, insurance, capitalization, or operations are defective.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Business-specific validation:** Entity/tax counsel, lender, and insurer must
review ownership, leases, guarantees, transfer taxes, rent, and insolvency effects.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Independent
review agrees that separation can help liability, financing, or succession,
but it is not a default tax win and can create rent, transfer-tax, and
bankruptcy complexity. Changes: require an after-tax cash and lender/insurance
comparison before assuming a holdco/opco structure.

Evaluate tax, liability, financing, insurance, related-party rent, succession,
and administrative consequences rather than assuming separation is always
beneficial.

### TAX-031 — What changes if products or services are sold outside the USA?

### TAX-033 — Which tax decisions must block quoting, purchasing, invoicing, or shipment?

- [CBP importer/exporter tips](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/basic-import-export/importer-exporter-tips) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Commodity, origin, classification, and agency requirements must be resolved.

jurisdictions.
