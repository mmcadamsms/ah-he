# Minimum Shop Support Capability Questions

## Purpose

This topic identifies the support equipment, supplies, work areas, utilities,
handling methods, safety controls, maintenance capability, and downstream
processes required to make each proposed anchor manufacturing capability
usable as a real business operation.

Buying a mill, lathe, laser cutter, welder, or coating gun does not by itself
create a complete production capability. The business must understand the
minimum surrounding system needed to receive material, prepare it, hold it,
process it, deburr and clean it, inspect it, protect it, move it, maintain the
equipment, manage waste, and safely deliver a finished result.

These are discovery questions, not approved equipment purchases or facility
requirements. Question IDs are permanent and canonical in this file.

## Research Starting Point

Initial research indicates that several proposed "support" processes create
material facility and compliance obligations:

- abrasive wheels and sanding require machine guarding and may require
  source-capture ventilation;
- welding introduces hot-work, fume, shielding-gas, fire-prevention, and
  qualification requirements;
- chips, swarf, grinding dust, oily waste, and combustible-metal residues
  cannot all be collected or stored using the same equipment;
- solvent cleaning, rust inhibitors, paints, and coatings introduce chemical,
  ventilation, storage, waste, and compatibility questions;
- powder coating may require pretreatment, a controlled application area,
  grounding, powder recovery, combustible-dust controls, fire protection,
  curing ovens, hanging and handling systems, and environmental or local
  permits; and
- laser-cut parts often need downstream deburring, forming, welding,
  inspection, cleaning, and finishing, but a press brake is not automatically
  mandatory if the initial market is intentionally limited to flat parts or
  bending is outsourced.

Exact requirements depend on the materials, quantities, equipment, building,
jurisdiction, insurer, and adopted codes. Relevant starting references include:

- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.22 - Walking-working surfaces and housekeeping](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.22)
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.94 - Ventilation](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.94)
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.106 - Flammable liquids](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.106)
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.107 - Spray finishing](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.107)
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.212 - General machine guarding](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.212)
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.215 - Abrasive wheel machinery](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.215)
- [OSHA Welding, Cutting, and Brazing Standards](https://www.osha.gov/welding-cutting-brazing/standards)
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1200 - Hazard Communication](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200)
- NFPA 33 for spray application, NFPA 51B for hot work, NFPA 86 for ovens and
  furnaces, and NFPA 484 for combustible-metal hazards, subject to the editions
  adopted by the authority having jurisdiction.

## Minimum Viable Shop and Capability Dependencies

### SHP-001 — What is the minimum viable support-equipment baseline for opening the first physical shop?

**State:** Exploring

**Original wording:** "topic of min-bar shop requirements"

**First-pass answer:** The minimum viable baseline is not a universal shopping
list. It is the smallest safe, inspected system that can receive the chosen
stock, move it within rated limits, power and operate the first anchor machine,
deburr and clean its actual output, verify every promised characteristic,
package the result, and control its wastes. Initially exclude welding, forming,
powder coating, broad material coverage, and other vertical integration unless
the first contracted work requires them.

**Evidence:**
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.22 — Walking-working surfaces](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.22) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Floors, aisles, access, and housekeeping are operating prerequisites, not optional accessories.
- [Haas UMC-750 pre-installation guide](https://www.haascnc.com/pre-install-guide/umc-750) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. A representative anchor machine requires model-specific foundation, clearance, electrical, air, coolant, and installation preparation beyond the machine purchase.
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.212 — General machine guarding](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.212) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Point-of-operation, rotating-part, chip, and spark hazards must be guarded.

**Conflicting evidence:** Turnkey-machine marketing can imply that power and
floor space are sufficient. Manufacturer pre-install requirements and OSHA
controls show that this is not a production-ready-shop definition.

**Confidence:** High on the functional categories; low on exact equipment and
cost until the anchor process, products, site, and jurisdiction are fixed.

**Business-specific validation:** Freeze the first 10–20 saleable part families,
then obtain a machine pre-install checklist, insurer/fire-marshal review, rigger
survey, metrology plan, waste profile, and line-item installed-cost quotes.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Core baseline logic is supported, but "minimum" still depends on the offered parts, site, insurer, and jurisdiction, so no universal equipment list or installed cost can be inferred.

### SHP-002 — Which support capabilities are required regardless of the initial anchor machines?

**State:** Exploring

Consider workbenches, tool storage, material handling, cleaning, deburring,
inspection, maintenance, housekeeping, waste, fire protection, PPE, utilities,
and secure storage.

**First-pass answer:** Every opening configuration needs controlled receiving
and storage; rated material handling; task-appropriate benches and tools;
measurement and record control; housekeeping and spill response; PPE, hazard
communication, emergency/fire equipment, lockout capability, secure chemical
and finished-goods storage; preventive maintenance; and lawful waste removal.
Deburring and cleaning are required only to the level demanded by the first
parts, but some safe method must exist.

**Evidence:**
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1200 — Hazard Communication](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Chemical inventory, labels, safety data sheets, and worker information apply across processes.
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132 — General PPE requirements](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. PPE follows a documented hazard assessment rather than a generic starter kit.
- [EPA hazardous-waste generator categories](https://www.epa.gov/hwgenerators/categories-hazardous-waste-generators) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Waste obligations depend on what is generated and in what quantity.

**Conflicting evidence:** Some very small shops rely on personal tools,
informal inspection, and municipal trash. That may describe observed practice,
but it does not establish safe handling, measurement traceability, or lawful
disposal.

**Confidence:** High.

**Business-specific validation:** Conduct a pre-opening job-hazard analysis,
waste determination, fire-code review, and gauge-capability study against the
first customer drawings.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The cited OSHA/EPA sources support receiving, handling, HazCom, PPE, maintenance, and waste as true cross-process baseline controls, while deburring and cleaning remain only as extensive as the first parts require.

### SHP-003 — What complete support-capability matrix is required for each proposed anchor process?

**State:** Exploring

Create separate columns for receiving, storage, preparation, workholding,
utilities, operation, handling, deburring, cleaning, inspection, finishing,
maintenance, waste, safety, training, permits, and shipping.

**First-pass answer:** Use the following gate matrix. “Minimum” means necessary
before selling that process; “conditional” means driven by the accepted part;
“outsource” is acceptable vertical integration only when the supplier,
specification, lead time, and incoming verification are controlled.

| Anchor process | Minimum support before sale | Conditional or initially outsourced |
|---|---|---|
| CNC milling/turning | stock receiving/racks, rated lifting, sawed blanks or purchased blanks, workholding/tooling, power/air/coolant, chip and mist control, deburr/clean, dimensional inspection, maintenance, segregated scrap/coolant, trained operator, packaging | sawing, heat treat, grinding, CMM, plating/coating, special NDT |
| Flat laser cutting | sheet receipt/storage/loading, model-specific power/chiller/air/assist gas/extraction, nesting, unloading/skeleton handling, edge-quality inspection, minimum deburr, scrap segregation, maintenance and packaging | press-brake forming, welding, advanced edge rounding, finishing |
| Welding/fabrication | identified code and materials, cutting/fit-up, qualified power source and consumables, table/clamps, cylinder control, fume capture, screened hot-work area, fire controls, cleaning/grinding, weld inspection and records | machining, stress relief, NDT, code engineering, coating |
| Powder coating | validated pretreatment, dry/clean staging, hanging/grounding, compliant booth and recovery, cure oven, cooling, film/cure/appearance inspection, fire/environmental controls, color/waste management | blasting, multistage conversion coating, large-part coating, laboratory corrosion tests |

**Evidence:**
- [TRUMPF TruLaser 2030 pre-installation manual](https://www.trumpf.com/filestorage/TRUMPF_US/Landingpages/TruLaser_2030_fiber/pdfs/TruLaser-2030-Pre-Install-Manual.pdf) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. Laser installation includes utilities, extraction, gas, placement, and service-space dependencies.
- [ANSI Z49.1:2021 — Safety in Welding, Cutting, and Allied Processes](https://pubs.aws.org/content/free_downloads/AWS-Z49-2021.pdf) — **Evidence class:** standards-body guidance. Welding readiness includes ventilation, fire prevention, cylinders, PPE, and trained work practices.
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.107 — Spray finishing](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.107) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Powder application and drying operations create ventilation, grounding, electrical, and fire-control dependencies.

**Conflicting evidence:** Vendors package optional automation differently, so
their “complete cell” boundaries conflict. The credible boundary is the
saleable-part flow and adopted-code requirements, not the vendor bundle.

**Confidence:** Medium because the matrix is complete at process-family level,
not for a selected model and product specification.

**Business-specific validation:** Populate every cell with owner, in-house or
supplier method, capacity, acceptance criterion, quote, lead time, permit,
training, and contingency for each shortlisted machine and product family.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The matrix is a useful gate, but several cells still remain product- and code-specific, and some support functions can stay outsourced if supplier control and incoming acceptance are genuinely in place.

### SHP-004 — Which support items are mandatory before an anchor machine can produce saleable work?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Mandatory items are those needed to make one conforming
part safely and repeatedly: commissioned utilities and guarding; approved raw
material; lifting and workholding; released program/process instructions;
required tooling and consumables; operator competence; in-process and final
inspection; nonconformance control; deburring/cleaning specified by the
drawing; preservation, identification, packaging, maintenance, and waste
controls. Equipment for features not offered is not mandatory.

**Evidence:**
- [Haas UMC-750 pre-installation guide](https://www.haascnc.com/pre-install-guide/umc-750) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. Production cannot begin until site, utilities, access, and installation conditions are met.
- [NIST policy on metrological traceability](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. A measurement result needs a documented calibration chain and stated uncertainty where traceability is claimed.
- [OSHA machine-guarding standards](https://www.osha.gov/machine-guarding/standards) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Guarding is a pre-operation control, not a later productivity upgrade.

**Conflicting evidence:** A machine can physically cycle without calibrated
inspection, documented process control, or finished-part handling. That is
demonstration capability, not evidence that saleable requirements are met.

**Confidence:** High.

**Business-specific validation:** Run a first-article traveler from receipt
through shipment and block release wherever no responsible owner, acceptance
criterion, calibrated tool, safe method, or disposition path exists.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. This correctly separates machine demonstration from saleable production and keeps mandatory status tied to promised conformity, safe operation, preservation, and lawful waste handling rather than to optional vertical integration.

### SHP-005 — Which support capabilities may initially be outsourced, rented, shared, mobile, or deferred?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Low-frequency, capital-intensive, regulated, or
specialist processes are the best initial outsourcing candidates: heat treat,
plating/anodizing, powder coating, certified welding/NDT, large CMM work,
grinding, forming, blasting, specialty lifting, and hazardous-waste transport.
Rent mobile lifting or extraction only when the rental is rated and compatible.
Do not outsource daily housekeeping, basic inspection, safe handling, process
ownership, or final acceptance.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA guidance on buying versus leasing equipment](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/buy-assets-equipment) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Ownership should be compared with leasing based on cash, useful life, maintenance, and utilization.
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.178 — Powered industrial trucks](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.178) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Rented handling equipment still requires suitable equipment and trained operators.
- [EPA hazardous-waste generator requirements](https://www.epa.gov/hwgenerators) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Hiring a transporter does not erase the generator’s characterization and management duties.

**Conflicting evidence:** Outsourcing lowers capital and compliance burden but
can increase queue time, freight damage, minimum charges, and loss of schedule
control. Neither in-house nor outsource is categorically cheaper.

**Confidence:** High on the selection logic; low on which named processes win
without demand and supplier evidence.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain at least two supplier quotes with
minimum lots, expedite terms, pickup schedule, certifications, reject handling,
and actual recent lead-time performance; compare with a fully burdened in-house
quote.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The outsourcing logic is sound, but outsourcing does not transfer final acceptance, daily safety ownership, or hazardous-waste generator duties, so the business still needs in-house control of those functions.

### SHP-006 — Which missing support capability would become the bottleneck or prevent acceptance of otherwise suitable customer work?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** The first blockers are usually not cycle time at the
anchor machine but unavailable material handling, workholding/tooling,
inspection range or uncertainty, deburring/cleanliness, downstream forming or
finishing, code qualification, and shipping protection. Any promised feature
without capacity and acceptance evidence is a no-bid constraint; a slow but
controlled supplier is a bottleneck, while no qualified path is a capability
gap.

**Evidence:**
- [NIST metrological traceability](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. A shop cannot substantiate tight acceptance results merely because a machine can nominally cut them.
- [TRUMPF laser pre-installation manual](https://www.trumpf.com/filestorage/TRUMPF_US/Landingpages/TruLaser_2030_fiber/pdfs/TruLaser-2030-Pre-Install-Manual.pdf) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. Utilities and extraction can gate machine availability before cutting capacity matters.
- [AWS D1.1/D1.1M structural welding code](https://www.aws.org/standards/page/d1-1-d1-1m-structural-welding-code-steel) — **Evidence class:** standards-body evidence. Code work can require qualified procedures, personnel, inspection, and records beyond owning welding equipment.

**Conflicting evidence:** Sales forecasts often identify the anchor machine as
the constraint; high-mix shops can instead be constrained by setup,
inspection, or outside-process lead time. The product-routing data should
decide.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Business-specific validation:** Route the first 50 representative RFQs,
record every no-bid and queued hour by operation, and rank gaps by lost gross
margin, frequency, lead-time effect, and cost to close.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. Contrary evidence from many job shops shows the anchor machine is often not the first constraint; inspection range, deburr/cleanliness, outside-process lead time, and qualification gaps regularly block otherwise winnable work.

### SHP-007 — What is the complete installed cost of each capability ecosystem rather than only the anchor machine?

**State:** Exploring

Include equipment, freight, rigging, utilities, ventilation, foundations,
guarding, fire protection, tooling, storage, software, training, consumables,
commissioning, maintenance, waste, permits, and working capital.

**First-pass answer:** No defensible installed cost is available until a model,
site, product mix, and jurisdiction are selected. Budget each ecosystem as:
purchase/options and tax; freight/insurance; unloading/rigging; slab/anchoring;
electrical, gas, air, water, drains, HVAC and extraction; guarding/fire work;
software/network; workholding/tooling/metrology/handling; commissioning and
training; permits/professional fees; startup stock/consumables/spares; waste
setup; schedule contingency; and working capital through customer payment.

**Evidence:**
- [Haas UMC-750 pre-installation guide](https://www.haascnc.com/pre-install-guide/umc-750) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. Site preparation and utility work are separate dependencies from machine price.
- [TRUMPF TruLaser 2030 pre-installation manual](https://www.trumpf.com/filestorage/TRUMPF_US/Landingpages/TruLaser_2030_fiber/pdfs/TruLaser-2030-Pre-Install-Manual.pdf) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. The laser ecosystem adds extraction, cooling, compressed air, assist gas, clearances, and connection work.
- [SBA guidance on buying assets and equipment](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/buy-assets-equipment) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Cash flow, maintenance, useful life, and financing belong in the acquisition decision.

**Conflicting evidence:** Rule-of-thumb installation percentages are fast but
can be badly wrong for power upgrades, foundations, fire suppression,
pretreatment, or gas infrastructure. Bottom-up written quotations are more
credible.

**Confidence:** High on cost structure; low on dollars.

**Business-specific validation:** Require a responsibility matrix and written
quotes from OEM, rigger, electrician, mechanical contractor, fire-protection

authority, including exclusions, escalation, tax, contingency, and cash timing.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The answer correctly refuses machine-price thinking and treats installed cost as a full ecosystem problem that cannot be credibly priced before the model, site, utility scope, and compliance obligations are fixed.

### SHP-008 — How much floor, wall, vertical, aisle, service, staging, and expansion space does each capability ecosystem require?

- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.22](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.22) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Walking-working surfaces and access must remain orderly and safe.

teams and material flow need clearance. A tight layout may fit on paper yet

### SHP-009 — What production volume, product mix, customer demand, or avoided outsourcing cost justifies bringing each support process in-house?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Bring a support process in-house only when qualified
demand produces enough contribution after labor, setup/changeover, yield loss,
energy, maintenance, compliance, floor-space opportunity cost, and capital
charge—and when lead-time or quality control has measurable customer value.
Use routing-specific break-even volume, not revenue or machine utilization
alone. Keep specialist or volatile work outsourced.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA break-even point guidance](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/plan-your-business/calculate-your-startup-costs) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Fixed, one-time, and recurring costs must be identified before break-even decisions.
- [NIST MEP supplier scouting](https://www.nist.gov/mep/supplier-scouting) — **Evidence class:** observed program evidence. Qualified external supply is a real capacity option, so ownership should be compared with available suppliers rather than with “no capability.”
- [EPA hazardous-waste generator categories](https://www.epa.gov/hwgenerators/categories-hazardous-waste-generators) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Higher in-house throughput can change waste-generator obligations and cost.

**Conflicting evidence:** Per-piece supplier pricing favors ownership at high
volume, but demand volatility, color/material mix, reject risk, and compliance
can preserve outsourcing’s advantage well beyond a simple purchase-price
break-even.

**Confidence:** High on method; low on threshold.

**Business-specific validation:** Use 12 months of RFQs and supplier invoices
to model volume by routing and batch, then validate with two outside quotes and
a complete in-house installed-cost, staffing, yield, and cash-flow model.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The break-even framing is right, but the threshold remains highly sensitive to volatility, setup mix, compliance burden, and space opportunity cost, so no generic utilization target should be inferred.

### SHP-010 — What phased acquisition sequence creates a coherent shop without buying support equipment too early?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Sequence by validated orders and dependencies: (1)
define offered parts and acceptance limits; (2) secure site, permits,
utilities, safety and material flow; (3) acquire common handling, inspection,
storage, housekeeping, and maintenance basics; (4) install and qualify one
anchor ecosystem; (5) stabilize throughput and supplier handoffs; then (6)
internalize the measured bottleneck. Reserve space and utility capacity early,
but defer process-specific vertical integration.

**Evidence:**
- [Haas UMC-750 pre-installation guide](https://www.haascnc.com/pre-install-guide/umc-750) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. Facility prerequisites must precede delivery and commissioning.
- [TRUMPF TruLaser 2030 pre-installation manual](https://www.trumpf.com/filestorage/TRUMPF_US/Landingpages/TruLaser_2030_fiber/pdfs/TruLaser-2030-Pre-Install-Manual.pdf) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. Extraction, utilities, access, and environmental conditions have to be planned before installation.
- [SBA guidance on buying assets and equipment](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/buy-assets-equipment) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Leasing and delayed ownership can preserve cash when useful life or demand is uncertain.

**Conflicting evidence:** Buying a complete line at once can reduce later
disruption and integration risk, but it commits capital before demand and
operating capability are demonstrated. Phased ownership is more credible for
an uncertain startup unless contracted volume supports the full line.

**Confidence:** High.

**Business-specific validation:** Set acquisition gates for signed backlog,
qualified process, sustained utilization, outside-process spend, delivery
losses, cash reserve, permits, staffing, and approved installed-cost estimate.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. A dependency-first sequence is well supported by OEM pre-install evidence; reserve utility and layout headroom early, but defer process-specific vertical integration until validated orders and measured bottlenecks justify it.

## Benches, Tables, Storage, and General Shop Tools

### SHP-011 — Which bench grinders, belt sanders, disc sanders, deburring wheels, buffers, and finishing tools are required initially?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Start with only the guarded tools required by the first
parts: often a variable-speed hand deburring kit and files/scrapers, plus one
source-captured stationary station with compatible wheels or belts only if
recurring burr volume, edge specification, or material condition justifies it. Add a
buffer, disc sander, dedicated stainless/aluminum station, automated edge
rounder, or tumbler only after edge specification, material, volume, and dust
hazard justify it. A general grinder is not a universal finishing process.

**Evidence:**
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.215 — Abrasive wheel machinery](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.215) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Stationary grinders require guards, work rests, exposure adjustment, and wheel controls.
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.94 — Ventilation](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.94) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Grinding, polishing, and buffing exhaust depends on machine and hood configuration.
- [OSHA combustible-dust technical manual](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/otm_secIV_chap6.pdf) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Fine combustible-metal dust needs hazard-specific collection and housekeeping.

**Conflicting evidence:** Low-cost multipurpose machines reduce capital, but
shared abrasives can cross-contaminate alloys and a common collector can create
an incompatible-dust hazard.

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Business-specific validation:** Make a part/material/edge-finish matrix,
perform dust-hazard review, and trial actual parts before selecting each
station, abrasive, speed, hood, and collector.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The minimum should stay narrower than a general-finishing catalog: hand deburring alone may be sufficient at launch, and a stationary abrasive station is only mandatory if recurring burr volume, edge spec, or material condition justifies it.

### SHP-012 — Which materials and operations must be segregated across abrasive machines and dust-collection systems?

**State:** Exploring

Address carbon steel, stainless steel, aluminum, magnesium, titanium, coatings,
and contamination-sensitive work.

**First-pass answer:** At minimum, segregate carbon-steel abrasives from
stainless and contamination-sensitive work; keep coating/paint dust out of
clean-metal systems; and do not combine combustible aluminum, magnesium, or
titanium fines with ferrous sparks or an unapproved common collector. Magnesium
and reactive-metal work requires material-specific engineering; “one grinder,
one vacuum” is not a safe default.

**Evidence:**
- [OSHA combustible-dust technical manual](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/otm_secIV_chap6.pdf) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Metal dust combustibility, ignition sources, collection, and housekeeping must be evaluated by material and process.
- [OSHA combustible-dust standards page](https://www.osha.gov/combustible-dust/standards) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. OSHA identifies aluminum, magnesium, and other metal dusts as potentially explosible and points to material-specific consensus standards.
- [Nickel Institute guidance on fabrication of stainless steels](https://nickelinstitute.org/media/8daa5b1b3d6822c/11007_fabricationofchromium-nickelstainlesssteels.pdf) — **Evidence class:** supplier/practitioner technical guidance. Stainless fabrication practice avoids iron contamination from tools and work surfaces.

**Conflicting evidence:** Small amounts of mixed coarse swarf may be accepted
by some recyclers, but dust-collection compatibility and surface-contamination
requirements are different questions and can require stricter segregation.

**Confidence:** High for segregation principle; medium for exact collector
architecture.

**Business-specific validation:** Obtain SDS and particle data for every alloy
and coating, perform a combustible-dust hazard analysis with the collector
supplier/fire authority, and obtain customer cleanliness requirements.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The segregation principle is well supported, especially for reactive metals and stainless-contamination-sensitive work; a shared collector should be treated as unsafe until the dust hazard analysis proves compatibility.

### SHP-013 — What guarding, tool-rest, spark containment, source capture, noise control, and PPE are required for each abrasive station?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Each station needs the manufacturer’s intact guard,
correct wheel/belt rating, controlled exposure, stable mounting, emergency
stop, spark path and noncombustible clearance, source capture compatible with
the dust, and a documented PPE/noise assessment. For bench grinders, OSHA
limits work-rest clearance to 1/8 inch and the adjustable tongue opening to 1/4
inch. PPE does not replace guarding or capture.

Screen air emissions from preparation/coating/ovens; industrial wastewater and
sewer pretreatment; chemical and hazardous-waste inventory; stormwater;
combustible-dust and fire-code review; building/mechanical/electrical/fuel-gas
permits; oven/booth listings; zoning/occupancy; and insurer conditions. Powder
itself may reduce VOC, but pretreatment, fuel combustion, dust and wastes remain.

**Evidence:**
- [EPA Clean Air Act permitting](https://www.epa.gov/caa-permitting) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Air-permit applicability depends on source, pollutants, quantity and state/local implementation.
- [EPA Industrial Effluent Guidelines](https://www.epa.gov/eg/industrial-effluent-guidelines) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Metal-finishing wastewater and local pretreatment requirements can apply to cleaning/conversion processes.
- [EPA industrial stormwater guidance](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/industrial-stormwater-guidance) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Outdoor exposure of materials, scrap and wastes can trigger permit/control duties.
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.107](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.107) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Spray and powder operations require fire, ventilation and electrical controls regardless of permit status.

**Conflicting evidence:** A vendor may state that powder coating is “permit
free” because it has little solvent; permit applicability is site- and process-
specific and can arise from other line stages.

**Confidence:** High that multi-agency screening is required; low on which
permits apply without a location and chemistry.

**Business-specific validation:** Submit a process description, maximum annual
throughput, SDS, emission data, wastewater flow/analysis, equipment drawings,
chemical inventory and waste profile to local/state air, sewer, fire, building,
zoning and environmental authorities before ordering.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The answer correctly rejects any universal permit checklist and treats air, wastewater, stormwater, fire, building, zoning, and insurer review as parallel site-specific screens.

### SHP-060 — What coating-thickness, cure, adhesion, color, gloss, texture, appearance, corrosion, and rework acceptance tests are required?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Tests come from the customer/coating specification.
Minimum commercial control commonly includes calibrated dry-film thickness,
recorded cure profile or validated solvent/cure check, adhesion coupons, visual
appearance under defined lighting and approved color/gloss standard. Add
texture, impact/flexibility, hardness, corrosion exposure, holiday or chemical
resistance only where specified. Rework needs its own approved strip/recoat or
touch-up limits.

**Evidence:**
- [ASTM D7091 — Nondestructive dry-film thickness](https://www.astm.org/d7091-22.html) — **Evidence class:** consensus test-method evidence. It defines nondestructive measurement practice for coatings on ferrous and nonferrous metal.
- [ASTM D3359 — Adhesion by tape test](https://www.astm.org/d3359-23.html) — **Evidence class:** consensus test-method evidence. Adhesion can be classified using a defined procedure.
- [ASTM D523 — Specular gloss](https://www.astm.org/d0523-14r18.html) — **Evidence class:** consensus test-method evidence. Gloss requires geometry and instrument control.
- [ASTM B117 — Salt spray](https://www.astm.org/b0117-19.html) — **Evidence class:** consensus test-method evidence. Accelerated corrosion exposure is a comparative test and does not by itself predict service life.

**Conflicting evidence:** Visual approval may be enough for decorative work,
while specification-driven sectors need instrumented and destructive tests.
Salt-spray hours are often overgeneralized as real-world life; ASTM cautions
that correlation depends on the system and service.

**Confidence:** High that the contract controls; medium on startup minimum.

**Business-specific validation:** For each target market, obtain the controlling
drawing/specification, approved color standard and sampling plan; qualify gauges,
coupons, lab tests, repair limits and customer first article.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree with qualifications. The contract-controls-acceptance conclusion is right, and the answer appropriately warns against overreading salt-spray data, but startup minimum tests still vary materially by customer, substrate, and warranty.

### SHP-061 — How much labor, cleaning time, lost powder, color-change time, rack maintenance, reject risk, energy, and floor space must be included in powder-coating economics?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Include all touch labor from load/prep/mask/hang through
unhang/inspect/pack; bath checks and waste handling; hook stripping and rack
repair; booth and gun cleaning; color purge and lost/reclaimed powder; oven
warmup and loaded energy; rework/scrap; filters and PPE; maintenance/downtime;
floor and staging opportunity cost; permits, testing and supervision. Model by
batch/color/part envelope, not coating pounds or spray minutes alone.

**Evidence:**
- [Powder Coating Institute resources](https://www.powdercoating.org/?page=Resources) — **Evidence class:** supplier/practitioner evidence. Training and process guidance cover pretreatment, transfer efficiency, color change, cure, maintenance and quality—not spray labor alone.
- [U.S. DOE process-heating systems guidance](https://www.energy.gov/eere/amo/process-heating-systems) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Ovens should be evaluated as process-heating systems with startup, exhaust, insulation, controls and utilization losses.
- [EPA hazardous-waste generators](https://www.epa.gov/hwgenerators) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Pretreatment and cleanup wastes add characterization, accumulation and disposal work/cost.

**Conflicting evidence:** High transfer-efficiency claims can be achieved under
stable production, but frequent color changes, small complex parts, poor hooks,
reclaimed-color limits and rejects can dominate startup economics.

**Confidence:** High on cost categories; low on values.

**Business-specific validation:** Time at least three representative batches
including full changeover and cleanup; meter gas/electricity, weigh issued/
reclaimed/waste powder, inspect yield, and obtain labor, floor and waste costs
before approving capacity.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The economics model correctly includes labor, color change, rejects, maintenance, energy, waste, and floor space instead of relying on spray time or powder pounds alone.

### SHP-062 — When is continued outsourcing safer or more economical than installing an in-house powder-coating line?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Continue outsourcing when demand is below a stable line
load, part size or color mix varies widely, required pretreatment/certification
is specialized, local suppliers meet quality and lead time, or the site cannot
economically obtain fire, utility, wastewater, air and zoning approval.
Internalize only when an end-to-end qualified line improves contribution,
delivery or strategic control after compliance, staffing and working capital.

**Evidence:**
- [SBA equipment acquisition guidance](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/manage-your-business/buy-assets-equipment) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Ownership should be weighed against cash, utilization, maintenance and flexibility.
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.107](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.107) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Bringing spray/powder finishing in-house adds nontrivial ventilation, fire, grounding and operating controls.
- [EPA Industrial Effluent Guidelines](https://www.epa.gov/eg/industrial-effluent-guidelines) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. In-house pretreatment can create regulated wastewater obligations absent from a spray-gun purchase comparison.

**Conflicting evidence:** Outsourcing transfers facility burden and preserves
flexibility, but supplier queues, minimum lots, freight damage and loss of color
control can justify ownership. Only actual supplier and line data can resolve it.

**Confidence:** High on the rule; low on the business conclusion.

**Business-specific validation:** Audit two qualified coaters and compare their
12-month delivered cost, lead time, yield and capacity commitment with a
fire/environmentally approved integrator quote and pilot-derived in-house
labor, energy, changeover, reject and cash-flow model.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Continued outsourcing can be safer and more economical when demand, approvals, pretreatment burden, or color mix are weak, and the answer correctly requires an end-to-end qualified comparison before internalizing.

## Capability Readiness and Acquisition Decisions

waste, emergency and shipping methods; capacity/lead-time model; complete cost

Marketing claims must match this demonstrated envelope.

- [Haas UMC-750 pre-installation guide](https://www.haascnc.com/pre-install-guide/umc-750) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. Equipment commissioning depends on completed site, utility, access and installation responsibilities.

capacity, safety, traceability, maintenance recovery or quoted economics.

**Confidence:** High.

**Business-specific validation:** Conduct a cross-functional production-
readiness review and ship a paid or customer-approved pilot through the full
route; close every safety, quality, permit, capacity and margin blocker before

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The readiness checklist captures the real pre-marketing gates—scope, safety, quality, permits, capacity, cost, contingency, and demonstrated sample output—rather than treating a demo part as readiness proof.

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Plan early for utility upgrades/transformers and
switchgear; slabs, pits and anchors; cranes and rigging access; compressors/

makeup air; powder booths, ovens, pretreatment and fire suppression; wastewater
connections; custom racks/tooling; metrology rooms; permits and inspections.
Actual lead time must come from dated supplier and authority commitments.

**Evidence:**
- [TRUMPF TruLaser 2030 pre-installation manual](https://www.trumpf.com/filestorage/TRUMPF_US/Landingpages/TruLaser_2030_fiber/pdfs/TruLaser-2030-Pre-Install-Manual.pdf) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. Site access, foundations, environment, utilities, extraction, gas and cooling are pre-delivery dependencies.

- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.107](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.107) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Finishing systems can require building-integrated ventilation, electrical and fire protection rather than portable accessories.

### SHP-065 — Which support equipment should be standardized across several anchor processes?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Standardize only genuinely common, compatible systems:
approved PPE families; labeled storage and spill kits; calibration/asset
records; modular carts and containers; common fasteners and hand-tool
platforms; maintenance work-order/spares methods; network/data interfaces; and
compressed-air quality where the strictest user governs. Do not standardize
vacuums, dust collectors, abrasives, chemicals, lifting attachments or scrap
containers across incompatible materials merely to reduce SKUs.

**Evidence:**
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.132](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.132) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. PPE can be standardized only after hazard and fit assessment.
- [OSHA combustible-dust technical manual](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/otm_secIV_chap6.pdf) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Shared housekeeping/collection equipment can be unsafe across incompatible dusts.
- [NIST metrological traceability](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. A common asset-control system can support multiple measurement processes while preserving calibration identity.

**Conflicting evidence:** Platform standardization reduces training and spares,
but forced commonality can compromise capacity, cleanliness, fire protection
or measurement uncertainty. Compatibility outranks purchasing leverage.

**Confidence:** High.

**Business-specific validation:** Review each proposed common item against
load/range, material compatibility, cleanliness, listing, calibration,
availability and failure consequence; standardize only after all user
processes approve it.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. Standardization is useful only where compatibility is real; the answer correctly rejects false SKU reduction across incompatible dusts, chemicals, lifting attachments, and cleanliness regimes.

### SHP-066 — Which support equipment is reasonable to buy used, and what inspection or refurbishment is required?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Used nonprecision benches, cabinets, racks, carts and
simple manual handling equipment can be reasonable after load/condition review.
Used machine tools, welders, compressors, saws, presses, hoists, forklifts,
dust collectors, booths, ovens and metrology require service history,
model/serial trace, manuals, parts support, safety/listing assessment, measured
performance and priced refurbishment. Avoid orphaned or modified safety-
critical equipment whose compliant condition cannot be demonstrated.

**Evidence:**
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.212 machine guarding](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/1910.212) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Machine-guarding duties apply to machines in operation, so existing/used equipment still requires compliant guarding.
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.179](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.179) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Cranes require rated-load marking, inspection, testing and maintenance.
- [NIST metrological traceability](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. A used gauge’s brand or prior calibration does not establish current traceable performance.

**Conflicting evidence:** Used purchase price can be low, but missing controls,
obsolete parts, transport, decontamination, calibration and downtime can exceed
new-equipment installed cost and warranty value.

**Confidence:** High on diligence; medium on categories.

**Business-specific validation:** Make purchase contingent on an independent
OEM/qualified-technician inspection, powered test under representative load,
oil/fluid and contamination review where relevant, calibration/geometry report,
code/listing review and fixed refurbishment/rigging quote.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. The used-equipment guidance appropriately distinguishes simple low-risk assets from safety-critical or precision equipment that needs service history, testing, parts support, and refurbishment costing before purchase.

### SHP-067 — What spare parts, preventive maintenance, calibration, service contracts, manuals, and employee skills are required to keep each support capability available?

calibration/verification intervals based on use and drift; current electrical/
pneumatic/software documentation; service escalation and response; lockout;
and trained primary/backup operators and maintainers. Contracts are justified
where response, proprietary tools or safety certification cannot be supported.

**Evidence:**
- [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 — Control of hazardous energy](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Maintenance requires defined energy-control procedures and authorized training.
- [NIST metrological traceability](https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability) — **Evidence class:** primary official evidence. Calibration intervals and traceability need documented results, not a permanent sticker assumption.
- [Haas maintenance checklist](https://www.haascnc.com/content/dam/haascnc/hfo-microsite/hfo-allendale/training-documents/Maintenance_Information.pdf) — **Evidence class:** authoritative technical manual. OEM schedules show recurring lubrication, coolant, filter, cleaning and inspection work.

**Conflicting evidence:** Fixed annual service is easy to administer, while
condition/use-based maintenance may reduce unnecessary work. Safety devices and
regulatory inspections still need prescribed intervals.

**Confidence:** High.

**Business-specific validation:** Complete a failure-mode and criticality review
for each selected asset; price OEM response, identify local technician skill,
set minimum spares from lead time and consequence, and validate maintenance
time in the capacity model.

**Second-opinion review:** GPT-5.4 — Agree. A manual- and criticality-based asset plan is the right minimum for availability, and the answer correctly treats calibration, lockout, spares, documentation, and backup skills as operating necessities rather than optional extras.

### SHP-070 — How will actual utilization, throughput, quality, labor, maintenance, safety, space, energy, consumable, and waste data determine whether each support capability should expand, change, or be removed?

**State:** Exploring

**First-pass answer:** Measure each capability by routing and product family:

energy and gas per good unit; consumables; waste and recovery value; and
contribution after capital. Expand a proven constrained process, correct an
unstable process, outsource a noncore low-load process, and remove only after
customer and contingency impacts are assessed.

**Evidence:**
- [NIST MEP manufacturing services](https://www.nist.gov/mep) — **Evidence class:** observed program evidence. Manufacturing improvement is based on measured productivity, quality, technology and operational performance rather than equipment count.

- [OSHA Recommended Practices for Safety and Health Programs](https://www.osha.gov/safety-management) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Leading indicators, worker participation and periodic program evaluation supplement injury counts.
- [EPA sustainable materials management](https://www.epa.gov/smm) — **Evidence class:** primary official guidance. Waste prevention and recovery should be measured across the material lifecycle.

**Conflicting evidence:** High utilization can signal demand or chronic
overloading; low utilization can signal optional capacity or strategic
redundancy. Utilization alone cannot decide expansion or removal.

**Confidence:** High on the measurement framework; medium on decision thresholds.

**Business-specific validation:** Establish 90-day baselines and quarterly

risk and cash; require a documented outsource/backup and customer-impact review
